2023 July · Q11: Consider a dataset with 60 points in which all points are either 0 or 1. We use a Bernoulli distribution with the parameter $p$ to model this problem. The prior and posterior distributions for the parameter $p$ are Beta(10,5) and Beta(30,45) respectively. How many data points have the value 0 in this dataset? Note that $p=P(x=1)$ as usual.

Do minute lagao, jawab aur confidence (S/U/G) khud likh lo. Poora solution neeche P1 mein hai (is PYQ ka tag [SRC]).

🧭 Position — yeh kahan baithta hai

  • Pichhla: 5.1 (B22) Bernoulli MLE $=k/n$, 5.2 (B23) wahi recipe doosri distributions par. Dono mein sirf data bolta tha. B22 ke S4 mein "posterior $\propto$ likelihood $\times$ prior" ki sirf pehchaan thi; yahan woh poora kaam karta hai.
  • Prerequisites: T5 module (B22) — S3 (Bernoulli likelihood $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$), S4 (Bayes setup), G3 ($\ln$ aur derivative, kyunki mode derivative se nikalta hai).
  • Yeh kya unlock karta hai: T5 ka end — niche timed set. Aage T7 (GMM) mein E-step ka responsibility $\gamma_k=\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x)}{\sum_j\pi_jf_j(x)}$ bhi Bayes' rule hai (discrete prior $\pi_k$ ke saath), isliye "prior × likelihood, phir normalise" ka yeh dimaag wahan wapas kaam aata hai.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

MLE kehta hai "data ne jo dikhaya wahi $p$". Par 3 tosses mein 3 heads dekh kar $\hat p=1$ (coin hamesha head!) maan lena risky hai. Bayesian soch: data aane se pehle ek andaza rakho ($p$ ke liye ek curve $[0,1]$ par), data aane par us curve ko update karo. Beta($\alpha,\beta$) aisi curves ki ek family hai: $\alpha$ "kitne ones ki kalpana", $\beta$ "kitne zeros ki kalpana".

Update ka rule bahut seedha hai: nayi ones $\alpha$ mein, nayi zeros $\beta$ mein jodo. Isliye prior Beta(4,3) ke baad 8 ones aur 5 zeros dekhne par Beta(12,8). Prior ko "pseudo-counts" ki tarah padhna sabse aasan hai: $\alpha+\beta$ jitna zyada, prior utna "mazboot" aur data ko utna zyada chahiye use hilane ke liye.

Analogy (labelled): naye cricketer ka average [ADDED] — selector ke paas pehle se ek andaza hai (is level ke players ka average ≈ 30). Naya player 3 pariyon mein 90 banaye to selector uska average 90 nahi maanta; woh purane andaze aur naye data ko milata hai. Jaise-jaise pariyan badhti hain, naya data andaze par zyada bhaari padta hai. Yahi $w=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta+n}$ ghatna hai.

Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: average koi probability nahi, aur "pseudo-counts" asli purane tosses nahi hain — $\alpha,\beta$ $0.5$ jaise non-integer bhi ho sakte hain. Prior ek maanyata hai, record nahi.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • Posterior koi Bernoulli nahi hota (P4/P6 ke options mein "Br(0.5)", "Bernoulli(0.1)" jaal hain): posterior $p$ ke upar ek distribution hai, Bernoulli toss ke outcome par nahi.
  • $\alpha,\beta$ data ke counts nahi; data ke counts $k$ aur $n-k$ hain, jo $\alpha,\beta$ mein jode jaate hain.
  • Beta ka mean $\alpha/\beta$ nahi, $\dfrac\alpha{\alpha+\beta}$ hai.
  • Posterior mean, MLE aur posterior mode teen alag cheezen hain (sirf Beta(1,1) prior par mode = MLE).
  • Prior data ko "hata" nahi deta: $n$ badhne par prior ka asar ghatta hai, data ka badhta hai.

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

Block 1 — Beta density (L49, L50)

$$f(p;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}}{B(\alpha,\beta)},\qquad 0<p<1,\qquad B(\alpha,\beta)=\int_0^1p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}\,dp.$$

Symbols: $p$ = Bernoulli ka anjaan parameter (ab ek random variable); $\alpha,\beta>0$ = prior ke "hyper-parameters"; $B(\alpha,\beta)$ = normaliser jo $p$ par depend nahi karta. Assumption: $p\in(0,1)$.

Words mein: $p^{\alpha-1}$ bada hota hai jab $p$ $1$ ke paas ho, $(1-p)^{\beta-1}$ jab $p$ $0$ ke paas; dono ka product ek curve banata hai jiska shape $\alpha,\beta$ se tay hota hai.

Notice that… formula sirf "$p^{\text{kuch}}(1-p)^{\text{kuch}}$" hai — bilkul wahi shape jo Bernoulli likelihood $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ ka hota hai. Yahi baat update ko addition bana deti hai (Block 3).

Small worked example: Beta(1,1): $f=\dfrac{p^0(1-p)^0}{B(1,1)}=1$ ($B(1,1)=1$), yaani Uniform(0,1) (L50).

Block 2 — mean, mode, variance (L51)

$$\mathbb E[p]=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta},\qquad \text{mode}=\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}\ \ (\alpha,\beta>1),\qquad \text{Var}(p)=\frac{\alpha\beta}{(\alpha+\beta)^2(\alpha+\beta+1)}.$$

Symbols: mean = $p$ ka average (integral se); mode = woh $p$ jahan density sabse oonchi hai. Assumption: mode formula tab valid jab $\alpha>1$ aur $\beta>1$ (warna peak boundary par ya density do peaks wali hoti hai, D5).

Words mein: mean "$\alpha$ ka hissa kul $\alpha+\beta$ mein" hai; mode mein har pseudo-count se ek ghata do ($\alpha-1,\ \beta-1$) kyunki density mein power $\alpha-1,\ \beta-1$ hain.

Notice that… mean aur mode ke formulas ek jaise dikhte hain par alag hain (denominator $\alpha+\beta$ vs $\alpha+\beta-2$). Beta(2,5): mean $\frac27=0.286$, mode $\frac15=0.2$ (Visual 2).

Small worked example: Beta(4,3): mean $\frac47=0.5714$, mode $\frac{3}{5}=0.6$ (code ✓; numerically integrate karke bhi mean $0.5714$).

Block 3 — Bayes ⇒ conjugate update (L52, L53)

$$\pi(p\mid D)\ \propto\ \underbrace{p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k}}_{\text{likelihood }L(p)}\cdot\underbrace{p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}}_{\text{prior}}=p^{\alpha+k-1}(1-p)^{\beta+n-k-1}\ \Rightarrow\ p\mid D\sim\text{Beta}(\alpha+k,\ \beta+n-k).$$

Symbols: $D$ = $n$ Bernoulli points, $k$ = ones ki ginti, $n-k$ = zeros ki ginti; $\pi(p)$ = prior; $\pi(p\mid D)$ = posterior. Assumption: points i.i.d. Bernoulli($p$) (G2), $p=P(x=1)$.

Words mein: posterior ko normalise karne ki zaroorat nahi — $p$ wale factors dekh kar hi pehchaan jaate hain ki yeh Beta($\alpha+k,\beta+n-k$) hai; normaliser apne aap theek hota hai.

Notice that… $\alpha$ mein ones jaate hain, $\beta$ mein zeros (jab $p=P(\text{success}=1)$). Total pseudo-count $\alpha+\beta$ ab $\alpha+\beta+n$ ho gaya (sanity check: P1, P4 mein kaam aata hai).

Small worked example: prior Beta(2,2), $n=10$, $k=7$ ⇒ posterior Beta($2+7,\ 2+3$) $=$ Beta(9,5) (WE1).

Important insight: Bayesian update "sirf counting" hai: prior ke pseudo-counts + data ke asli counts. Koi integral, koi derivative nahi — is topic ke har PYQ ka pehla step yahi update hai.

Block 4 — posterior mean as weighted average (L54)

$$\frac{\alpha+k}{\alpha+\beta+n}=\underbrace{\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta+n}}_{w}\cdot\underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}}_{\text{prior mean}}+\underbrace{\frac{n}{\alpha+\beta+n}}_{1-w}\cdot\underbrace{\frac kn}_{\text{MLE}}.$$

Symbols: $w\in(0,1)$ prior ka vazan; $1-w$ data ka vazan. Assumption: $n\ge1$ (MLE $k/n$ define ho).

Words mein: posterior mean = prior mean aur MLE ke beech ek point, jahan "kitna prior pe bharosa" $\alpha+\beta$ (pseudo-points) aur "kitna data pe" $n$ (asli points) se tay hota hai.

Notice that… $n\to\infty$ par $w\to0$ ⇒ posterior mean $\to$ MLE. $n=0$ par $w=1$ ⇒ posterior mean = prior mean. Verification: $w\cdot\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta+n}$ aur $(1-w)\frac kn=\frac k{\alpha+\beta+n}$; dono jodo to $\frac{\alpha+k}{\alpha+\beta+n}$.

Small worked example: P4/P5 ka Beta(4,3), 8 ones, 13 points: $w=\frac7{20}$; $0.35\cdot\frac47+0.65\cdot\frac8{13}=0.2+0.4=0.6$ ✓ (code).

Block 5 — posterior mode (MAP) aur MLE ka rishta (L55)

$$\text{mode of Beta}(\alpha+k,\beta+n-k)=\frac{\alpha+k-1}{\alpha+\beta+n-2}\quad\xrightarrow{\ \alpha=\beta=1\ }\quad\frac{k}{n}=\hat p_{MLE}.$$

Symbols: MAP = maximum a posteriori = posterior ka peak. Assumption: $\alpha+k>1$ aur $\beta+n-k>1$.

Words mein: MAP wahi $p$ hai jo (prior $\times$ likelihood) ko maximise kare. Agar prior flat hai (Beta(1,1)) to maximise sirf likelihood hota hai, isliye MAP = MLE.

Notice that… Beta(1,1) prior par posterior mean $\frac{k+1}{n+2}$ hai jo MLE $\frac kn$ nahi hai (P6/P7: mean $0.108$, mode/MLE $0.100$).

Small worked example: P6/P7: Beta(11,91): mode $\frac{10}{100}=0.1$ $=$ MLE ✓; mean $\frac{11}{102}=0.1078$.

Block 6 — counts wapas nikaalna (L56) — P1 ka case

$$k=\alpha_{post}-\alpha_{prior},\qquad n-k=\beta_{post}-\beta_{prior},\qquad n=(\alpha_{post}+\beta_{post})-(\alpha_{prior}+\beta_{prior}).$$

Symbols: subscripts prior/post = pehle/baad wale Beta ke parameters. Assumption: same Bernoulli model, $p=P(x=1)$ (isliye $\alpha\leftrightarrow$ ones).

Words mein: update sirf addition tha, to subtraction usko ulta kar deta hai.

Notice that… teen quantities ek-doosre ka consistency check dete hain: $k+(n-k)=n$.

Small worked example: prior Beta(5,3) ⇒ posterior Beta(20,13): $k=15$, $n-k=10$, $n=25$; check $33-8=25$ ✓ (WE2).

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Prior padho: Beta($\alpha,\beta$) likho. Dhyan do ki "success" (jis par $p=P(\text{success})$ hai) kaun hai: defective, converted, heads, ya "1".
  2. Data ko do counts mein todo: $k$ = successes (ones), $n-k$ = failures (zeros). Agar sawaal sirf $n$ aur fraction de, to $k$ khud nikalo.
  3. Add karo: $\alpha'=\alpha+k$, $\beta'=\beta+(n-k)$. Sanity: $\alpha'+\beta'=\alpha+\beta+n$.
  4. Sawaal ka shabd dekho: "posterior" ⇒ Beta($\alpha',\beta'$) likh do; "posterior mean / expected value / Bayes estimate" ⇒ $\frac{\alpha'}{\alpha'+\beta'}$; "MAP / mode / most likely" ⇒ $\frac{\alpha'-1}{\alpha'+\beta'-2}$; "MLE" ⇒ $\frac kn$ (prior ignore).
  5. Options ho to: har option ko pehchaano ki woh kaun sa statistic hai (P8 mein har galat option kisi aur quantity ka sahi number tha).
  6. Verify: posterior mean prior mean aur MLE ke beech hona chahiye (Block 4); mode formula tabhi jab $\alpha',\beta'>1$.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

D1 · Beta prior "conjugate" kyun kehlata hai

Conjugate ka matlab: prior aur posterior ek hi family mein. Yahan Bernoulli likelihood $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ aur Beta prior $p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$ dono "$p^{a}(1-p)^{b}$" shape ke hain; multiply karne par powers jud jaati hain, shape wahi rehti hai. Isi liye har naya data posterior ko Beta rakhta hai aur update sirf parameter-addition hai. Normaliser $B(\alpha',\beta')$ apne aap aa jaata hai kyunki koi bhi density jiska shape $p^{\alpha'-1}(1-p)^{\beta'-1}$ hai, woh Beta($\alpha',\beta'$) hi hai.

D2 · Mean ki formula ka derivation (Beta integral se)

$\mathbb E[p]=\displaystyle\int_0^1p\cdot\frac{p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}}{B(\alpha,\beta)}dp=\frac{B(\alpha+1,\beta)}{B(\alpha,\beta)}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)}{\Gamma(\alpha)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+1)}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}.$ Step: $p\cdot p^{\alpha-1}=p^{(\alpha+1)-1}$, isliye integral $B(\alpha+1,\beta)$ ban gaya; $\Gamma(\alpha+1)=\alpha\Gamma(\alpha)$ aur $\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+1)=(\alpha+\beta)\Gamma(\alpha+\beta)$ se ratio $\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}$. Check: Beta(4,3) mein numerical integral $0.5714=\frac47$ ✓ (code).

D3 · Mode ka derivation aur mean–median–mode ka kram

$\ln f=(\alpha-1)\ln p+(\beta-1)\ln(1-p)+\text{const}$; $\frac{d}{dp}=\frac{\alpha-1}p-\frac{\beta-1}{1-p}=0\Rightarrow(\alpha-1)(1-p)=(\beta-1)p\Rightarrow p=\dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}$. Doosra derivative $-\frac{\alpha-1}{p^2}-\frac{\beta-1}{(1-p)^2}<0$ jab $\alpha,\beta>1$ ⇒ yeh maximum hai. Beta(2,5): mode $0.2$, median $0.2644$ (code), mean $0.2857$ — right-skewed density mein mode $<$ median $<$ mean. Beta(6,3) (left-skew) mein ulta: mean $0.667$, mode $0.714$.

D4 · Prior ka asar "dhulna" aur posterior ka patla hona

$w=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta+n}\to0$ jab $n\to\infty$, isliye posterior mean aur MLE ka fark $\approx\frac{\alpha+\beta}{n}\times|\text{prior mean}-\text{MLE}|$, yaani $O(1/n)$. Posterior variance bhi $\approx\frac{\hat p(1-\hat p)}n$ ke order par ghatti hai: P4 ka posterior variance $0.0114$ prior ke $0.0306$ se kam (code ✓). Do alag priors (P8) isi wajah se 100 points ke baad paas aa jaate hain.

D5 · $\alpha$ ya $\beta\le1$: U-shape, boundary mode, Beta(0.5,0.5)

$\alpha,\beta<1$ par density $p=0$ aur $p=1$ ki taraf infinity tak jaati hai (U-shape) aur beech mein sabse neechi hoti hai, isliye "mode" ka formula $\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}$ meaningless (do boundary peaks) ho jaata hai. Beta(0.5,0.5) ek "kam-jaankari" prior (Jeffreys) ke taur par use hota hai: Beta(1,1) se kam pseudo-count ($1$ vs $2$ kul). Par posterior mein koi bhi data aate hi ($k\ge1$, $n-k\ge1$) $\alpha',\beta'>1$ ho jaate hain aur mode ka formula wapas valid ho jaata hai (P8 mein $3.5$ aur $97.5$).

Edge case: prior Beta(1,1), $k=0$ ($n$ points mein koi success nahi): posterior Beta(1,$n+1$), $\alpha'=1$ ⇒ density $p=0$ par sabse badi, mode $0$ (=MLE); lekin posterior mean $\frac1{n+2}>0$ (Laplace ka rule). MLE "kabhi nahi" kehta hai, mean nahi — yahi prior ki asli kimat hai.

D6 · Kaun sa point-estimate kab: mean vs mode

Posterior mean woh number hai jo posterior ke under expected squared error ko minimise karta hai; mode (MAP) woh hai jo posterior density ko maximise karta hai. Dono jab posterior symmetric aur unimodal ho (Beta($a,a$)) to barabar hain; asymmetric ho to alag. PYQ mein sawaal ka shabd hi tay karta hai ("mean", "expected value" vs "mode"/"most likely"), isliye formula ka chunaav shabd se hota hai, preference se nahi.

📌 Memory card — 5.3 Beta priors, posterior updates, mean and mode

  • Core model: $p$ ek random variable, prior Beta($\alpha,\beta$) $\propto p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$; Bernoulli data ka likelihood bhi $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ shape ka ⇒ posterior $\propto$ prior $\times$ likelihood bhi Beta. $\alpha,\beta$ pseudo-counts ki tarah (ones, zeros).
  • Backbone chain: success define karo → data ko $(k,\ n-k)$ mein todo → $\alpha'=\alpha+k$, $\beta'=\beta+n-k$ (sanity $\alpha'+\beta'=\alpha+\beta+n$) → sawaal ka shabd dekho (mean / mode / MLE / "posterior") → bound check (mean prior mean aur MLE ke beech).
  • Formulas (conditions): Beta mean $\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\beta}$ · mode $\frac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}$ ($\alpha,\beta>1$) · posterior mean $\frac{\alpha+k}{\alpha+\beta+n}=w\cdot\text{prior mean}+(1-w)\cdot\frac kn$, $w=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\alpha+\beta+n}$ · MAP $\frac{\alpha+k-1}{\alpha+\beta+n-2}$; Beta(1,1): MAP $=k/n=$ MLE aur mean $=\frac{k+1}{n+2}$ · counts wapas: $k=\alpha_{post}-\alpha_{prior}$, $n-k=\beta_{post}-\beta_{prior}$.
  • Top 3 traps: (1) ones $\to\beta$ ya zeros $\to\alpha$ (ya $\beta+n$) — ones $\to\alpha$, zeros $\to\beta$; (2) mean ki jagah mode / MLE / prior mean likhna (P5, P8 ke options); (3) posterior ko Bernoulli bataana ya prior ke pseudo-counts bhool jaana (P4, P6).
