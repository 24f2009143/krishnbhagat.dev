Tier 1 — general foundations

G1 · Random variable, PMF / PDF, expectation

A. Core idea

Random variable $X$ = kisi random experiment ka number (coin: heads = 1, tails = 0). Discrete $X$ ke liye PMF $P(X=x)$ har value ki probability deta hai (sab $\ge0$, jod $=1$). Continuous $X$ ke liye PDF $f(x)$ density deta hai: probability = area, kul area $=1$. Expectation $E[X]=\sum_xx\,P(X=x)$ = lambe samay ka average.

B. Notation and operations

  • $X\sim\text{Bernoulli}(p)$ padhte hain "$X$ Bernoulli($p$) se aaya".
  • $P(X=x;\,p)$ mein semicolon ke baad $p$ parameter hai (fixed, anjaan knob); $x$ data ki value hai. Yeh farq S2 mein kaam aayega.
  • Indicator: $x\in\{0,1\}$ ke liye $x^1=x$, $x^0=1$ — isi se $p^x(1-p)^{1-x}$ ek line mein dono cases deta hai.

C. Worked examples

  • Bernoulli($p$): $P(X=1)=p$, $P(X=0)=1-p$, $E[X]=0\cdot(1-p)+1\cdot p=\mathbf p$. Matlab: 0/1 data ka average hi $p$ ka natural guess hai (isi ka saboot 5.1 mein).
  • PDF: $f(x)=\lambda e^{-\lambda x}$, $x\ge0$ ka area $\int_0^\infty\lambda e^{-\lambda x}dx=[-e^{-\lambda x}]_0^\infty=1$ ✓ (code-verified). 5.2 mein yahi exponential model aayega.

D. Common mistakes

  • PDF ki value ko probability samajhna — PDF $\ge1$ ho sakti hai (jaise $[0,0.5]$ par $f=2$); continuous $X$ ke liye $P(X=x)=0$.
  • PMF ka jod 1 check na karna.

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: "Model" ka asli matlab kya hai? A: data banane ka ek probability recipe jisme ek anjaan knob $\theta$ hai. MLE = us knob ki woh setting jisse dekha hua data sabse zyada probable lage.

G2 · Independence, i.i.d., product rule

A. Core idea

Do events independent hain agar $P(A\cap B)=P(A)P(B)$. i.i.d. = independent and identically distributed: har data point ek-doosre se bekhabar, aur sab ek hi distribution (ek hi $p$) se. Isliye poore dataset ki probability = har point ki probability ka product.

B. Notation and operations

  • $P(x_1,\dots,x_n;\,\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^nP(x_i;\,\theta)$ — "identically" ka matlab har factor mein wahi $\theta$.
  • Product "aur" ke liye (independent events), jod "ya" ke liye (disjoint events).

C. Worked examples

  • Tosses H, T, H, $P(H)=p$: $P(\text{HTH})=p\cdot(1-p)\cdot p=\mathbf{p^2(1-p)}$.
  • Teen independent fair tosses sab heads: $0.5\times0.5\times0.5=0.125$.
  • Order: sequence HTH ki probability $p^2(1-p)$ hai; "kisi bhi order mein 2 heads" ki $3p^2(1-p)$ (Binomial, S1).

D. Common mistakes

  • Independent events ki probabilities jodna.
  • Time-series jaisi dependent cheez par i.i.d. laga dena (product tab galat).
  • Bahut saari probabilities (har $<1$) ka product bahut chhota ho jaata hai — $0.3^{1000}$ computer mein $0$ ho jaata hai (underflow). Iska ilaaj $\ln$ hai (G3).

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: Likelihood ko product kyun likhte hain? A: i.i.d. assumption ki wajah se. Agar data dependent ho to product wali formula hi galat hogi.

G3 · $\ln$, derivative, maximisation

A. Core idea

$\ln$ ek increasing function hai, isliye $f>0$ ke liye $\arg\max f=\arg\max\ln f$ (value badalti hai, kaun sa input best hai woh nahi). $\ln(ab)=\ln a+\ln b$ aur $\ln(a^m)=m\ln a$ — product jod ban jaata hai, power coefficient. Smooth function ka maximum: derivative $=0$ aur second derivative $<0$; saath mein boundaries bhi check karo.

B. Notation and operations

  • $\dfrac d{dp}\ln p=\dfrac1p$; $\dfrac d{dp}\ln(1-p)=-\dfrac1{1-p}$ (chain rule — minus sign yahin chhootta hai).
  • $\arg\max$ = woh input; $\max$ = woh value.

C. Worked examples

  • $f(p)=p^3(1-p)^7$: $\ln f=3\ln p+7\ln(1-p)$; $\dfrac3p-\dfrac7{1-p}=0\Rightarrow3(1-p)=7p\Rightarrow p=\mathbf{0.3}$; $\dfrac{d^2}{dp^2}=-\dfrac3{p^2}-\dfrac7{(1-p)^2}<0$ ✓ (code-verified).
  • $f(p)=(1-p)^5$: $\ln f=5\ln(1-p)$, derivative $-\dfrac5{1-p}$ kabhi $0$ nahi hota; $f$ sirf ghatta hai ⇒ max boundary $p=0$ par (yahi 5.1 ka tricky case, WE3).

D. Common mistakes

  • $\dfrac d{dp}\ln(1-p)$ mein minus bhoolna.
  • Derivative zero karke ruk jaana (na $\ell''$, na boundary check).
  • Product par seedha product-rule lagana — pehle $\ln$ lo, yeh bahut aasaan hai.

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: Hum $\ln$ kyun lete hain? A: teen wajah: (i) product → sum, derivative aasan; (ii) underflow se bacha ($0.3^{1000}$ double mein $0$ hai, par $1000\ln0.3=-1203.97$ theek hai — code-verified); (iii) $\arg\max$ nahi badalta.

Tier 2 — T5-specific maths

S1 · Bernoulli, Binomial, Geometric

A. Core idea

Bernoulli($p$): ek trial, success (1) ki probability $p$. Binomial($n,p$): $n$ trials mein success ki ginti $K$. Geometric($p$): pehle success tak ke trials ki ginti $T$ (success wala trial bhi gin-ti mein shaamil).

B. Notation and operations

  • Bernoulli: $P(X=x;p)=p^x(1-p)^{1-x}$ (L33).
  • Binomial: $P(K=k;p)=\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}$, $k=0,\dots,n$.
  • Geometric: $P(T=t;p)=(1-p)^{t-1}p$, $t=1,2,3,\dots$ — $t-1$ failures, phir 1 success.
  • "Success" kaun hai (heads / type A / defective) sawaal tay karta hai; $p$ usi ki probability hai.

C. Worked examples

  • $p=0.3$: $P(X=0)=\mathbf{0.7}$.
  • Binomial $n=10$, $p=0.3$, $k=3$: $\binom{10}3(0.3)^3(0.7)^7=120\times0.027\times0.0823543\approx\mathbf{0.2668}$.
  • Geometric $p=0.2$, $T=5$: $(0.8)^4(0.2)=0.4096\times0.2=\mathbf{0.08192}$.

D. Common mistakes

  • $p$ ko galat label se jodna (P3/P4 ka trap).
  • Geometric mein "5 tosses" ko "5 failures" samajhna (Geometric $T=5$ = 4 failures + 1 success).
  • Binomial mein coefficient $\binom nk$ ko MLE ke liye zaroori samajhna — $p$ par depend hi nahi karta (D1).

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: Aaj ke PYQs mein kaun sa model kahan? A: P1–P4: Bernoulli data (counts mein diya hua). P5: Bernoulli (4 tosses) + Geometric (pehle head tak).

S2 · Likelihood vs probability

A. Core idea

Formula wahi, sawaal alag. Probability: parameter fixed, data variable — "agar $p=0.3$ ho, to 3 heads ki probability?" Likelihood: data fixed, parameter variable — "yeh data dekha; kaun sa $p$ ise sabse achhi tarah explain karta hai?" $L(\theta)=P(\text{data};\theta)$, $\theta$ ka function.

B. Notation and operations

  • $L(\theta)$ ya $L(\theta;x)$; $\theta$ par $0$ se $1$ (ya poore range) tak badalti hai.
  • $\theta$ par $L$ ka kul area 1 nahi hota — yeh density nahi hai.
  • Relative likelihood $L(\theta)/L(\hat\theta)\in[0,1]$ — do $\theta$ ko compare karne ke liye.

C. Worked examples

  • Data HTH (2 heads / 3 tosses): $L(p)=p^2(1-p)$. $L(0.3)=0.063$, $L(0.5)=0.125$, $L(\tfrac23)=\tfrac4{27}\approx\mathbf{0.1481}$ ⇒ in teen mein $\tfrac23$ sabse achha.
  • Area: $\int_0^1p^2(1-p)\,dp=\tfrac13-\tfrac14=\tfrac1{12}\neq1$ ✓ (code-verified) — isliye $L$ probability distribution nahi.

D. Common mistakes

  • $L(p)$ ko "$p$ sach hone ki probability" padhna. Woh posterior hai — usko prior chahiye (5.3).
  • Alag-alag datasets ke likelihood ki value seedha compare karna.

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: Kya MLE $\hat p=0.3$ ka matlab "$p=0.3$ hone ki 30% chance" hai? A: Nahi. Matlab: sabhi $p$ mein $0.3$ ne dekhe hue data ko sabse zyada probability di. Doosra sawaal (kaun sa $p$ zyada probable hai) Bayesian hai.

S3 · Log-likelihood and the MLE recipe

A. Core idea

$\hat\theta_{MLE}=\arg\max_\theta L(\theta)$. 5-step recipe: (1) model + i.i.d. ⇒ $L$ likho; (2) $\ell=\ln L$; (3) $\ell'(\theta)$; (4) $\ell'=0$ solve; (5) verify: $\ell''<0$ ya concavity, aur boundary / edge cases.

B. Notation and operations

  • $\ell(\theta)=\ln L(\theta)$; hat ($\hat\theta$) = data se nikala estimate.
  • Bernoulli ke liye $\ell(p)=k\ln p+(n-k)\ln(1-p)$ (L36).

C. Worked example (Bernoulli, har algebra step)

  • $L(p)=p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ ⇒ $\ell(p)=k\ln p+(n-k)\ln(1-p)$.
  • $\ell'(p)=\dfrac kp-\dfrac{n-k}{1-p}$. Zero karo: $\dfrac kp=\dfrac{n-k}{1-p}$.
  • Cross-multiply: $k(1-p)=(n-k)p\Rightarrow k-kp=np-kp\Rightarrow k=np\Rightarrow\boxed{\hat p=\dfrac kn}$.
  • $\ell''(p)=-\dfrac k{p^2}-\dfrac{n-k}{(1-p)^2}$ — $k$ aur $n-k$ mein se kam se kam ek $>0$ ⇒ $\ell''<0$ ⇒ concave ⇒ ek hi maximum.

D. Common mistakes

  • $\ell'$ mein $-\dfrac{n-k}{1-p}$ ka minus bhoolna.
  • $\ell''$ check na karna, ya $k=0$ / $k=n$ jaise boundary cases bhool jaana (L38).
  • $k$ (ones) aur $n-k$ (zeros) ki adla-badli.

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: Sequence ke likelihood aur Binomial ke likelihood mein $\binom nk$ ka fark hai — kya MLE badalta hai? A: Nahi. $\binom nk$ $p$ se bahar ka constant hai; $\arg\max$ wahi rehta hai.

S4 · Bayes' rule: prior → posterior (sirf setup)

A. Core idea

Bayes' rule: $P(\theta\mid\text{data})=\dfrac{P(\text{data}\mid\theta)\,P(\theta)}{P(\text{data})}$, yaani posterior $\propto$ likelihood $\times$ prior. MLE prior ko ignore karta hai. Bernoulli ke liye natural prior Beta($\alpha,\beta$) hai: $p\in[0,1]$ par density $\propto p^{\alpha-1}(1-p)^{\beta-1}$. Likelihood ke saath multiply karne par posterior phir Beta hota hai: $\text{Beta}(\alpha+k,\ \beta+n-k)$ [ADDED] (yeh B24 ka asli topic hai; yahan sirf pehchaan).

B. Notation and operations

  • Prior mean $\dfrac\alpha{\alpha+\beta}$; posterior mean $\dfrac{\alpha+k}{\alpha+\beta+n}$; $\alpha,\beta$ "pseudo-counts" jaise kaam karte hain.
  • Beta mode $\dfrac{\alpha-1}{\alpha+\beta-2}$ (jab $\alpha,\beta>1$).

C. Worked example [ADDED]

  • Prior Beta(1,1) (uniform), data $k=3$, $n=10$ ⇒ posterior Beta(4,8): mean $\tfrac4{12}=\tfrac13$, mode $\tfrac{3}{10}=0.3$ — mode wahi hai jo MLE $k/n$ (code-verified).
  • Prior Beta(2,2), data 5 ones, 3 zeros ⇒ Beta(7,5).

D. Common mistakes

  • Posterior mein $\alpha+k$ ke saath $\beta+k$ jodna (sahi: $\beta+(n-k)$, yaani zeros).
  • Prior $\alpha,\beta$ ko data counts samajh lena (woh sirf pseudo-counts hain).

E. Why T5 needs this

Q: 5.1 se kya rishta? A: $k=0$ par MLE $\hat p=0$ aata hai (poori tarah "kabhi nahi"); uniform prior ke saath posterior mean $\tfrac{k+1}{n+2}>0$. Yahi wajah hai ki prior seekhna padta hai — B24.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

0/1 data hai (heads/tails, spam/not-spam, defective/ok, type A/B). Model ka sirf ek knob hai: $p$ = success ki probability. MLE ka sawaal: $p$ ko kis value par set karein ki jo data humne sach mein dekha, uski probability sabse badi ho? Bernoulli mein jawab bahut seedha nikalta hai — success ka fraction: $\hat p=\dfrac{k}{n}$. Saboot batata hai ki yeh fraction sirf "obvious guess" nahi, optimal guess bhi hai.

Analogy (labelled): radio dial [ADDED] — $p$ ek dial hai, $L(p)$ us dial-position par signal ki taakat. MLE = sabse tez signal wali position. Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: (i) radio ka sabse tez signal asli station hota hai, par $L(p)$ ka peak sirf data ka best-fit hai — chhote $n$ par woh sach $p$ se door ho sakta hai; (ii) signal-strength curve ka area kuch matlab nahi rakhta, aur $L$ bhi probability distribution nahi hai (area $\neq1$).

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • $\hat p=0.3$ ka matlab "$p=0.3$ hone ki probability $0.3$" nahi hai (S2).
  • $\hat p$ sach $p$ ke barabar hone ki guarantee nahi — $n=10$ aur $n=1000$ dono mein $\hat p=0.3$ ho sakta hai, par bharosa bahut alag hai (Visual 2).
  • Calculus wala "derivative = 0" hamesha kaam nahi karta: $k=0$ ya $k=n$ par maximum boundary par hai (WE3).

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

Block 1 — Bernoulli model aur likelihood

$$P(X=x;\,p)=p^{x}(1-p)^{1-x},\quad x\in\{0,1\}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad L(p)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}p^{x_i}(1-p)^{1-x_i}=p^{k}(1-p)^{n-k},\quad k=\sum_{i=1}^nx_i.$$

Symbols: $x_i$ = $i$-th observation (0 ya 1); $p=P(X=1)\in[0,1]$ = anjaan parameter; $n$ = data points; $k$ = ones ki ginti, $n-k$ zeros ki. Assumption: data i.i.d. Bernoulli($p$) hai (G2).

Words mein: har 1 $p$ ka factor deta hai, har 0 $(1-p)$ ka; jodne par $p$ ki power = ones, $(1-p)$ ki power = zeros.

Notice that… $L$ data par sirf $k$ aur $n$ ke through depend karta hai — sequence ka order maayne nahi rakhta ("count hi kaafi hai").

Block 2 — log-likelihood aur MLE (L36, L37)

$$\ell(p)=k\ln p+(n-k)\ln(1-p),\qquad \ell'(p)=\frac kp-\frac{n-k}{1-p}=0\;\Longrightarrow\;\hat p=\frac kn,\qquad \ell''(p)=-\frac k{p^2}-\frac{n-k}{(1-p)^2}<0.$$

Symbols: $\ell=\ln L$; $\ell',\ell''$ = $p$ ke respect mein pehla aur doosra derivative. Assumption: $0<k<n$ (interior); boundary L38 mein.

Words mein: $\ell'=0$ ka matlab $\dfrac{k}{p}=\dfrac{n-k}{1-p}$, yaani $\dfrac p{1-p}=\dfrac k{n-k}$ — model ke odds = data ke odds.

Notice that… $\ell''<0$ har $p\in(0,1)$ par — curve concave hai, isliye jo stationary point milta hai woh global maximum hai.

Aur ek aam confusion: Bernoulli sequence ka likelihood $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ hai, Binomial count ka $\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}$. Fark sirf $p$-free constant $\binom nk$ ka hai — $\hat p$ dono mein wahi.

Small worked example: data $1,0,0,0,1$ ⇒ $n=5$, $k=2$. $L(p)=p^2(1-p)^3$; $\hat p=\tfrac25=0.4$; $L(0.4)=0.4^2\times0.6^3=0.16\times0.216=0.03456$ (code ✓).

Important insight: Bernoulli MLE = relative frequency. "Obvious" estimator hi optimal estimator hai, aur saboot yeh batata hai ki kyun (odds match).

This is why P1–P4 sirf "$\hat p=$ fraction" ko ulta chalane ke sawaal hain (fraction diya hai, count nikaalo), aur P5 mein bas counts jodne padte hain.

Block 3 — Geometric data aur pooling (L39)

$$P(T=t;\,p)=(1-p)^{t-1}p,\ t=1,2,\dots\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad L(p)\propto p^{\,S}(1-p)^{\,F}\ \Rightarrow\ \hat p=\frac{S}{S+F}$$

Symbols: $T$ = pehle success tak trials; $S$ = kul successes, $F$ = kul failures (saare independent experiments jo ek hi $p$ share karte hain, milakar). Assumption: experiments independent, same coin.

Words mein: ek geometric observation $t$ = $t-1$ failures + 1 success. Do independent experiments ke likelihood multiply hote hain, isliye $p$ aur $(1-p)$ ki powers jud jaati hain.

Notice that… experiment kaise roka gaya (fixed $n$ tosses ya "head aane tak") $p$-dependence ka shape nahi badalta; sirf $p$-free constant badalta hai (D5).

Block 4 — invariance (L40)

$$\widehat{g(p)}=g(\hat p)\qquad\text{(jaise }\widehat{1-p}=1-\hat p,\ \ \widehat{\tfrac p{1-p}}=\tfrac{\hat p}{1-\hat p}\text{)}.$$

Symbols: $g$ ek one-to-one function. Words mein: parameter ko kisi aur naam (reparametrise) se likhein to bhi best data-fit wahi rehta hai; sirf label badalta hai. Notice that $q=1-p=P(\text{type B})$ lete hi $L=(1-q)^kq^{n-k}$ banta hai aur $\hat q=\tfrac{n-k}n=1-\hat p$ nikalta hai (P3/P4 ke liye kaam ka).

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Data padho: $n$ kul points; "success" kise kehte hain (1 / heads / type A) aur $p$ kiski probability hai — pehle yahi tay karo.
  2. Model likho: i.i.d. Bernoulli($p$); agar "head aane tak" jaisi baat ho to wahan Geometric ($(1-p)^{t-1}p$).
  3. Likelihood: sab factors multiply karo aur powers jodo: $L(p)=p^{S}(1-p)^{F}$, $S$ = successes, $F$ = failures.
  4. Log lo: $\ell(p)=S\ln p+F\ln(1-p)$.
  5. Differentiate: $\ell'(p)=\dfrac Sp-\dfrac F{1-p}$.
  6. Zero karo aur solve: $S(1-p)=Fp\Rightarrow S=(S+F)p\Rightarrow\hat p=\dfrac S{S+F}$.
  7. Verify: $\ell''<0$; saath mein $S=0$ ya $F=0$ jaise boundary cases dekho.
  8. Jawab ko sawaal ki bhasha mein padho: kya poochha — fraction, ones ka count, zeros ka count, ya type B? Kya diya hua $\hat p$ 1 ka hai ya A ka?

Exam fast path: counts → $\hat p=\text{successes}/\text{trials}$; $\hat p$ diya ho to successes $=n\hat p$. Phir wahi label wapas padho.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

D1 · Count kaafi hai (sufficiency) aur Bernoulli vs Binomial

$L(p)=p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ data par sirf $k$ aur $n$ se tika hai: do sequences jinmein ones ki ginti barabar ho (HHT aur THH) same $L$ dete hain. Binomial count $K$ ka likelihood $\binom nkp^k(1-p)^{n-k}$ hai — $\binom nk$ $p$ se free constant, $\arg\max$ wahi. Isliye PYQs mein data "sequence" ya "count" kisi bhi roop mein aaye, $\hat p=k/n$.

D2 · Concavity ka saboot (ek hi maximum kyun)

$\ell''(p)=-\dfrac k{p^2}-\dfrac{n-k}{(1-p)^2}$. $p\in(0,1)$ par dono terms $\le0$, aur $k$ ya $n-k$ mein se kam se kam ek $>0$ ($n\ge1$ ho to). Isliye $\ell''<0$ har jagah — $\ell$ strictly concave hai, to $\ell'=0$ ka koi bhi solution unique global maximum hai. Seedha check: $n=10$, $k=3$, $p=0.3$ par $\ell''=-\dfrac3{0.09}-\dfrac7{0.49}=-47.62$, aur formula $-\dfrac n{\hat p(1-\hat p)}=-\dfrac{10}{0.21}=-47.62$ ✓.

D3 · Boundary cases ($k=0$ ya $k=n$)

$k=0$: $L=(1-p)^n$ ghatta hai ⇒ $\hat p=0$. $k=n$: $L=p^n$ badhta hai ⇒ $\hat p=1$. Formula $\hat p=k/n$ in cases mein bhi sahi jawab deta hai ($0$ aur $1$), par rasta calculus ka $\ell'=0$ nahi, boundary comparison hai. Exam mein agar "all zeros / all ones" dikhe to calculus mat chalao; seedha $\hat p=0$ ya $1$ aur yeh note karo ki prior se yeh theek hota hai (5.3).

D4 · Invariance (L40) — kyun kaam karta hai

Agar $g$ one-to-one ho aur $q=g(p)$, to $L$ ko $q$ ke function ki tarah likho: $\tilde L(q)=L(g^{-1}(q))$. Ek hi values dono mein aati hain, bas label alag. $L$ ka maximum $p=\hat p$ par hai ⇒ $\tilde L$ ka maximum $q=g(\hat p)$ par. P3/P4: $q=1-p$ ⇒ $\hat q=1-\hat p$.

D5 · Stopping rule — fixed $n$ vs "head aane tak" [ADDED]

P5 mein ek experiment ka size fixed tha (4 tosses), doosre ka random (head aane tak). Fir bhi dono ka $p$-dependent hissa ek hi shape ka hai: $p^{\text{heads}}(1-p)^{\text{tails}}$; sirf $p$-free constants (jaise $\binom nk$) alag hote hain. Isi liye pooled MLE seedha "kul heads / kul tosses" nikalta hai. (Yeh syllabus ke bahar ki "likelihood principle" wali baat ka seedha nateeja hai; exam ke liye bas pooling rule yaad rakhna kaafi hai.)

D6 · Do raaste, ek number: mean se bhi $k/n$ [ADDED]

0/1 data ke liye $\sum_i(x_i-v)^2=k(1-v)^2+(n-k)v^2$; iska minimiser $v=\dfrac kn$ (T4 ka "mean hi minimiser hai", L7; $k=5$, $n=8$ ke liye grid-search se bhi $0.625$ ✓). Matlab Bernoulli MLE aur least-squares dono $k/n$ dete hain — Bernoulli mein yeh sanyog hai, har distribution mein nahi (5.2 mein dekhenge).

D7 · Curvature aur $n$ — peak patla kyun hota hai [ADDED]

$\ell''(\hat p)=-\dfrac n{\hat p(1-\hat p)}$: $n$ badhne par curvature badhti hai, peak patla hota hai (Visual 2: $n=10,100,1000$ ke liye $\hat p=0.3$ wahi, curve alag). $\hat p$ khud nahi badla; bas us par data ka "bharosa" badha.

D8 · Prior ke saath MLE ka rishta (B24 ka preview) [ADDED]

Uniform prior Beta(1,1) ke saath posterior Beta($k+1$, $n-k+1$) hota hai; uska mode $\dfrac{k}{n}$ = MLE, par mean $\dfrac{k+1}{n+2}$ (e.g. $k=3$, $n=10$: mode $0.3$, mean $\tfrac13$; $k=0$: mean $\tfrac1{n+2}>0$). Poora 5.3 B24 mein.

Suppose you toss a coin four times and observe no heads.

You then give the coin to your friend, who tosses it until the first head occurs. Your friend tosses the coin a total of five times.

Let $p$ denote the probability that the coin comes up heads. Find the maximum likelihood estimate of $p$.

Accepted answer (source): 0.10 to 0.12.

[?] "Five times" ki reading. Maine literally padha: dost ne kul 5 tosses kiye aur 5th toss par pehla head aaya, yaani $P(T=5)=(1-p)^4p$. Dusri reading (5 tails ke baad head, 6 tosses) $\hat p=\tfrac1{10}=0.10$ deti hai. Dono accepted range 0.10–0.12 mein aate hain (meri reading ka $\tfrac19=0.111$ aur dusri ka $0.10$), isliye range dono ko alag nahi kar sakti. ✍️ Try first

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: MLE of $p$. Given: experiment 1 — 4 tosses, 0 heads. Experiment 2 — "until the first head", 5 tosses total.
  • Trigger clues: "tosses it until the first head" ⇒ Geometric; "give the coin to your friend" ⇒ same coin, independent ⇒ pool. Prerequisites: S1, S2, S3, G2 — Procedural + Conceptual. Core principle: L39 (likelihoods multiply, powers jud jaati hain).

2. 🧭 Methods (2 genuinely different)

M1 General: product likelihood + calculus. M2 Shortcut: saare tosses ek list mein likho aur $S/(S+F)$ lo. Numerical grid-search sirf verification hai, alag method nahi.

3. 🧮 Working

M1 · Likelihood + calculus (General)

Step 1 (exp 1): 4 independent tails: $P=(1-p)^4$.

Step 2 (exp 2): pehla head 5th toss par: $P(T=5)=(1-p)^4p$.

Step 3: independent, ek hi coin: $L(p)=(1-p)^4\cdot(1-p)^4p=p\,(1-p)^8$.

Step 4: $\ell(p)=\ln p+8\ln(1-p)$, $\ell'(p)=\dfrac1p-\dfrac8{1-p}$.

Step 5: $\ell'=0\Rightarrow1-p=8p\Rightarrow\hat p=\dfrac19\approx\mathbf{0.1111}$.

Step 6: $\ell''=-\dfrac1{p^2}-\dfrac8{(1-p)^2}<0$; $p=\tfrac19$ par $=-81-10.125=-91.125$ ✓ maximum.

M2 · Pooled counts (Shortcut)

Step 1: saare tosses likho — exp 1: T T T T; exp 2: T T T T H. Kul $4+5=9$ tosses.

Step 2: heads $S=1$, tails $F=4+4=8$.

Step 3: L39: $\hat p=\dfrac S{S+F}=\dfrac19\approx\mathbf{0.111}$.

Step 4: check: $L\propto p^1(1-p)^8$ — M1 ke step 3 jaisa ✓.

6. ⚠️ Traps

  • Sirf dost ka data: $\tfrac15=0.2$. Sirf pehla experiment: $\hat p=0$ (boundary).
  • Dono estimates ka average: $\dfrac{0+0.2}2=0.1$ — yeh accepted range ke kinare par hai, to galat tareeka bhi "sahi" lag sakta hai. Sahi tareeka counts jodna hai, estimates nahi (WE4).
  • Geometric mein success wale toss ko gin-na bhoolna ($(1-p)^{t}$ ya $t=4$ le lena).
  • Binomial coefficient ya $\tbinom nk$ jodna — $p$ se free hai, jawab nahi badalta.
