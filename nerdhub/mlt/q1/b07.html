🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • PCs = naye axes. PCA purane $x_1,x_2,\dots$ axes ko ghuma kar (rotate karke) aise naye perpendicular axes deta hai jo data ke failaav (spread) ke saath line up hon. PC1 = sabse lamba failaav, PC2 = baaki mein sabse lamba (aur PC1 ke perpendicular), …
  • Perpendicular kyun? Kyunki $C$ symmetric hai. Symmetric matrix ke alag-alag eigenvalues wale eigenvectors automatically perpendicular hote hain (neeche proof, 3 line ka). Aur PC$_k$ ki definition hi "pichhle sab PCs ke perpendicular directions mein max variance" hai.
  • Direction hai, arrow nahi. $w$ aur $-w$ ek hi line hain — dono same variance, dono valid PC. MSQ mein $\pm$ dono options sahi hote hain (P7).
  • Flat direction = aakhri PC. Agar har point ek plane $a^Tx=0$ mein hai, to normal $a$ ki direction mein koi spread nahi ⇒ $\lambda=0$ ⇒ $a/\|a\|$ sabse chhota PC; baaki saare PCs plane ke andar.
  • Do kitaabein, ek kahani (dual). $C$ ka eigenvector $\mathbb R^d$ mein (features ki combination), $K=X^TX$ ka eigenvector $\mathbb R^n$ mein (points ki combination). $X$ guna karne se points-combination features-space mein aa jaata hai: $w\propto X\alpha$.

Analogy [ADDED] — tirchha shoebox. Ek pancake-jaisa (chapta, lamba) points ka badal socho. Uske charon taraf sabse tight fit hone wala shoebox rakho. Box ki teen edges = PC1 (lambai), PC2 (chaudai), PC3 (motai). Box ki edges hamesha ek-doosre ke perpendicular hoti hain, chahe box kitna bhi tirchha rakha ho. Agar badal bilkul chapta hai (sab points ek plane mein), to motai zero — woh edge (plane ka normal) hi zero-variance PC hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Agar lambai aur chaudai barabar ho (square cross-section, $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$), to box ko us plane mein kitna bhi ghumao, fit same — PCs unique nahi (cube dataset P9). (2) PCA sirf variance (second moment) dekhta hai, asli shape nahi — ek gol ring aur ek bhara hua disc dono ke PCs same ho sakte hain.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "$C$ ke koi bhi do eigenvectors perpendicular" — nahi. Repeated eigenvalue ke eigenspace mein do non-perpendicular eigenvectors mil sakte hain (e.g. $C=I$: $(1,0,0)$ aur $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1,0)$ dono eigenvectors, dot $=0.707$). PCA choose karta hai orthonormal set.
  • "Pichhle ek PC ke perpendicular ho to agla PC ho sakta hai" — nahi, sabhi pichhle PCs ke perpendicular hona chahiye (P4 ke options 1, 3 isi trap mein).
  • "PC direction = mean ki direction ya kisi data point ki direction" — zaroori nahi; P5 mein coincidence hai kyunki saare points origin wali line par hain.
  • "$K$ aur $C$ ke eigenvectors ek scalar se jude hain" — nahi; alag dimensions ($\mathbb R^n$ vs $\mathbb R^d$), matrix $X$ jodta hai (P2 option 1, P3).

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\text{(F1)}\ \ C=C^T\ \Rightarrow\ C=W\Lambda W^T,\quad W^TW=I,\quad \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0)$$ $$\text{(F2)}\ \ Cw_j=\lambda_jw_j,\ Cw_k=\lambda_kw_k,\ \lambda_j\neq\lambda_k\ \Rightarrow\ w_j^Tw_k=0$$ $$\text{(F3)}\ \ w_k=\arg\max_{\|u\|=1,\ u\perp w_1,\dots,w_{k-1}}u^TCu,\qquad \operatorname{Var}_{w_k}=\lambda_k$$ $$\text{(F4)}\ \ a^T\tilde x_i=0\ \forall i\ \Rightarrow\ C\tfrac{a}{\|a\|}=0\quad(\text{zero-variance PC}=\pm\tfrac{a}{\|a\|})$$ $$\text{(F5)}\ \ K\alpha=\lambda_K\alpha,\ \|\alpha\|=1,\ \lambda_K>0\ \Rightarrow\ w=\frac{X\alpha}{\sqrt{\lambda_K}},\quad Cw=\frac{\lambda_K}{n}w$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $C\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ centred data ka covariance ($\tfrac1n$); symmetric aur PSD. $W=[w_1\ \cdots\ w_d]$ — columns PCs, unit length, mutually perpendicular ("orthonormal").
  • $\lambda_k$ eigenvalue = variance along $w_k$ (B5). Order: bada se chhota.
  • (F3) mein "$u\perp w_1,\dots,w_{k-1}$" — PC$_k$ ki definition mein hi orthogonality hai.
  • $a\in\mathbb R^d$, constraint vector; $\tilde x_i$ centred points (affine constraint $a^Tx=b$ bhi centring ke baad $a^T\tilde x=0$ ban jaata hai — B6 D3 §4).
  • $X\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ (columns = centred points), $C=\tfrac1nXX^T$, $K=X^TX\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$. (F5) mein $\lambda_K>0$ zaroori (warna $X\alpha=0$).

Meaning in words: (F1) "symmetric matrix ke liye perpendicular unit eigenvectors ka poora set milta hai." (F2) "alag eigenvalues ⇒ eigenvectors automatically perpendicular." (F3) "PC$_k$ = pichhle sab ke perpendicular directions mein sabse zyada spread wali direction." (F4) "jis direction mein data hilta nahi, woh $\lambda=0$ wala PC." (F5) "$K$ ka eigenvector $X$ se guna karke aur $\sqrt{\lambda_K}$ se divide karke $C$ ka unit eigenvector; eigenvalue $n$ se divide hota hai."

Small worked example (F2). $C=\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\1&3\end{bmatrix}$: eigenvalues $4,2$ (distinct), eigenvectors $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ aur $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$; dot $=\tfrac12(1-1)=0$ ✓. Ab $C=2I$: eigenvalue 2 repeated; $(1,0)$ aur $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ dono eigenvectors, dot $=0.707\ne0$ — par PCA inme se nahi, koi orthonormal pair (jaise $(1,0),(0,1)$) chunta hai. (Code-verified.)

Important insight: $d$-dimensional data mein agar $d-1$ PCs pata hain, to aakhri PC unique up to sign hai (bacha hua 1-D orthogonal complement). Agar sirf $d-2$ pata hain (jaise P4: $d=4$, 2 PCs), to PC3 ek 2-D plane mein kahin bhi ho sakta hai — isliye question "could be" poochhta hai, aur answer sirf "perpendicular + unit" test se nikalta hai.

This is why projection coordinates (B8) itne simple hain: $W$ orthonormal ⇒ $W^{-1}=W^T$ ⇒ naye coordinates $z=W^Tx$, yaani har coordinate bas $w_k^Tx$ (dot product). Aur alag PCs par scores uncorrelated hote hain: $\tfrac1n\sum_i(w_j^T\tilde x_i)(w_k^T\tilde x_i)=w_j^TCw_k=\lambda_kw_j^Tw_k=0$.

⚙️ How it works — mechanism

A. "Kaun PC$_k$ ho sakta hai?" (P4 type)

  1. Har option ki length check: $\|v\|=1$? (Scale factor ke andar ke squares jodo × factor².)
  2. Har pichhle PC ke saath dot product: $v^Tw_j=0$ for all $j<k$. Scale factors ($\tfrac1{\sqrt3}$ etc.) ignore kar sakte ho — zero ka test sirf integer vectors par karo.
  3. Agar $d-1$ PCs diye hain: bacha hua direction unique (3-D mein cross product $w_1\times w_2$).
  4. Agar $C$ bhi diya hai: $Cv=\lambda v$ check karo aur $\lambda$ ka order (PC3 ka $\lambda$ PC2 se chhota/barabar).

B. Constraint → direction (P7 type)

  1. Constraint ko $a^Tx=0$ form mein likho; $a$ = coefficients.
  2. $\pm a/\|a\|$ = zero-variance PC (sabse chhota $\lambda$).
  3. Koi option $v$ jo $\perp a$ hai ($v^Ta=0$) — plane ke andar, positive-variance PC ho sakta hai (data ke bina confirm nahi).
  4. Jo option na $\perp a$ na $\parallel a$ — generic data ke liye PC nahi.

C. $K$ ke eigenvector se $C$ ka PC (P2/P3 type, $d\gg n$)

  1. $K=X^TX$ ($n\times n$) banao — chhota matrix.
  2. $K\alpha=\lambda_K\alpha$, $\|\alpha\|=1$ solve karo.
  3. $w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$ — yeh unit length PC hai ($\|X\alpha\|^2=\alpha^TK\alpha=\lambda_K$).
  4. Variance along $w$ $=\lambda_K/n$; $XX^T$ ka eigenvalue $=\lambda_K$ $=n\lambda_C$.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — proofs, edge cases, connections

§1 Proof of F2 (alag eigenvalues ⇒ perpendicular)

$w_j^TCw_k$ ko do tareeke se likho. $Cw_k=\lambda_kw_k$ se: $w_j^TCw_k=\lambda_kw_j^Tw_k$. $C=C^T$ se: $w_j^TCw_k=(Cw_j)^Tw_k=\lambda_jw_j^Tw_k$. Ghatao: $(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)\,w_j^Tw_k=0$. $\lambda_j\neq\lambda_k$ ⇒ $w_j^Tw_k=0$. ∎ (Symmetry hi kaam aayi — non-symmetric matrix mein yeh fail hota hai, e.g. $\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$ ke eigenvectors $(1,0),(1,1)$ perpendicular nahi.)

§2 Ties: eigenspace + Gram–Schmidt

Agar $\lambda$ $m$ baar repeat ho, to uske saare eigenvectors ek $m$-dim subspace banate hain. Us subspace ka koi bhi orthonormal basis valid PCs hain. Spectral theorem guarantee deta hai ki aisa basis exist karta hai (Gram–Schmidt se banta hai). Isliye: value ($\lambda$) unique, direction nahi. Cube (P9) mein $m=3$: har unit $v$ eigenvector.

§3 F3 kyun eigenvector deta hai (Lagrange)

$\max u^TCu$ s.t. $u^Tu=1$: $\nabla(u^TCu-\lambda(u^Tu-1))=2Cu-2\lambda u=0$ ⇒ $Cu=\lambda u$, aur objective $=u^TCu=\lambda$ ⇒ max $=\lambda_1$. PC2 ke liye extra constraint $u^Tw_1=0$ aata hai; multiplier $\gamma$ ke saath $2Cu-2\lambda u-\gamma w_1=0$; $w_1^T$ se guna karo: $2w_1^TCu-\gamma=2\lambda_1w_1^Tu-\gamma=0-\gamma$ ⇒ $\gamma=0$ ⇒ phir se $Cu=\lambda u$, aur $w_1$ ke perpendicular eigenvectors mein sabse bada $\lambda_2$. Induction se baaki.

§4 Uncorrelated scores

$z_{ik}=w_k^T\tilde x_i$. $\tfrac1n\sum_iz_{ij}z_{ik}=w_j^TCw_k=\lambda_k\delta_{jk}$. Naye coordinates mein covariance matrix $=\Lambda$ (diagonal). B4 dataset check: $z_1=(2.83,-2.83,0,0)$, $z_2=(0,0,-1.41,1.41)$ ⇒ $\tfrac14\sum z_1z_2=0$, $\tfrac14\sum z_1^2=4$, $\tfrac14\sum z_2^2=1$ (code-verified). "PCA decorrelates" ka matlab yahi hai.

§6 Dual (F5) ka proof + cost

$K\alpha=\lambda_K\alpha$ ⇒ $X$ se guna: $XX^T(X\alpha)=\lambda_K(X\alpha)$ ⇒ $C(X\alpha)=\tfrac{\lambda_K}n(X\alpha)$. Length: $\|X\alpha\|^2=\alpha^TX^TX\alpha=\lambda_K$ ⇒ $w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$ unit. Cost: $C$ ka eigen-decomposition $\sim d^3$; $K$ banana $\sim n^2d$ + eigen $\sim n^3$. $d\gg n$ par bahut sasta. Extra: centred data ka rank $\le n-1$, isliye $K$ ka kam se kam ek eigenvalue 0 bhi hota hai, aur $C$ ($d\times d$) ke kam se kam $d-n+1$ zeros — $C^{-1}$ exist hi nahi karta (P3 option 3 out). Numerical check ($d=8,n=3$, random centred): $\operatorname{eig}(K)=\operatorname{eig}(XX^T)_{\text{top}}=n\cdot\operatorname{eig}(C)_{\text{top}}$, $\|w\|=1$, $Cw=\tfrac{\lambda_K}nw$ ✓ (code-verified).

§7 Sign convention

Software (numpy, sklearn) PC ka sign kabhi bhi flip kar sakta hai. Projection scores ka sign bhi flip hoga, variance nahi. Exam mein: dono signs equally correct; MSQ mein dono tick karo (P7).

M1 · $C=I$

📌 Memory card — 2.2 PC directions & orthogonality

Core model: PCs = $C$ ke orthonormal eigenvectors, $\lambda$ ke order mein. Symmetric $C$ ⇒ orthonormal set exist (spectral theorem); distinct $\lambda$ ⇒ automatic perpendicular (F2); ties ⇒ eigenspace mein free choice.

Backbone chain: $C=C^T$ → $C=W\Lambda W^T$ → $w_k$ unit, $w_j^Tw_k=0$ → "could be PC$_k$" = unit + $\perp$ all previous → constraint normal = λ=0 PC → $d\gg n$: $w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $(\lambda_j-\lambda_k)w_j^Tw_k=0$ (C symmetric)
  • $w_k=\arg\max_{\|u\|=1,\,u\perp w_{<k}}u^TCu$
  • $a^T\tilde x_i=0\ \forall i$ ⇒ $C\,a=0$
  • $K\alpha=\lambda_K\alpha$, $\lambda_K>0$ ⇒ $w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$, $\lambda_C=\lambda_K/n$
  • $C=kI$ ⇒ $v^TCv=k$ har unit $v$
  • $d-1$ PCs known ⇒ last unique up to sign

Top 3 traps: (1) sirf $w_1$ ke saath dot check (P4 opt 1, 3); (2) $\pm$ pair mein ek hi tick (P7); (3) $C$–$K$ eigenvectors ko scalar multiple samajhna / $X^T$ vs $X$ ulta (P2 opt 1, P3). Bonus: $L_1$ vs $L_2$ "unit" (P6), normal = smallest PC, PC1 nahi.

Trigger bank: "could be the $k$-th PC" → dot tests · "every point satisfies" → normal = λ=0 PC · "collinear" → normalise line direction · "$d\gg n$ / kernel matrix" → $X\alpha$ · "any unit vector" → $C=kI$ · "always orthogonal" → True in PCA (ties caveat).
