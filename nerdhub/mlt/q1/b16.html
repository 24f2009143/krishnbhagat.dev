🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • PCA ek hi cheez maangta hai: dot products. B12 ne dikhaya ki dual PCA data ko sirf $x_i^Tx_j$ ke through dekhta hai. B13 ne dikhaya ki kernel $k(x_i,x_j)$ bhi ek aisa hi dot product hai — kisi (bade, shayad infinite) feature space $\phi(x)$ mein. To dual PCA ka $x_i^Tx_j$ hatao, $k(x_i,x_j)$ rakho: ho gaya kernel PCA = PCA us feature space mein, jahan $\phi$ kabhi explicitly nahi banta.
  • Nonlinear structure seedha ho jaata hai. Do concentric rings ko koi seedhi line alag nahi kar sakti (standard PCA mein dono directions barabar variance deti hain). Par Gaussian feature space mein "andar ki ring" aur "bahar ki ring" ke features alag jagah baithte hain, aur ek hi PC unhe alag kar deta hai.
  • Centering feature space mein hoti hai. PCA ko mean-zero data chahiye. Par yahan data $\phi(x_i)$ hai, aur uska mean $\bar\phi$ hum dekh nahi sakte. Ilaaj: Gram matrix ko hi centre kar do — $K^c=HKH$. Input data centred ho tab bhi $\bar\phi\ne0$ ho sakta hai (Example 1).
  • Baaki sab B12 jaisa. $K^c$ ke eigenvectors $\beta$ (points ki space ke weights), variance $\lambda/n$, score $\sqrt\lambda\,\beta_i$, coefficients $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$. Naya point: $k(x,x_j)$ ke $n$ values chahiye.
  • Components $d$ se zyada ho sakte hain. Standard PCA mein nonzero components $\le\min(d,n-1)$. Yahan limit feature space ki dimension aur $n-1$ hai. Gaussian mein feature space infinite hai, to limit $n-1$ (jab points generic hon).

Analogy [ADDED] — "kaun kisse kitna milta-julta hai" ka table. Class ke $n$ students ka ek similarity-table $K$ hai (har pair ki similarity). Pehle table ko "class ke average student" ke hisaab se re-baseline karo (centering: har student ki similarity us average se naapo). Phir table ke sabse bade patterns (eigenvectors) nikalo — har pattern batata hai kaun se students ek tarah behave karte hain, aur pattern ki taaqat $\lambda$ hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Table koi bhi raay nahi — woh valid kernel (PSD) honi chahiye, warna $\lambda$ negative aa sakte hain. (2) "Average student" ek asli student nahi, feature space ka mean $\bar\phi$ hai, jise hum kabhi dekh nahi paate — sirf $\theta_i$ aur $P$ se uska asar pata chalta hai. (3) Patterns students ke weights ($\beta\in\mathbb R^n$) hain; asli PC $w$ feature space mein rehta hai aur explicitly likha nahi jaata.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "Input data centred hai to $K$ pehle se centred hai" — nahi. Sirf linear kernel mein sach hai. Polynomial: input mean $0$ ho tab bhi $\bar\phi=(1,0,\tfrac23)$ (Example 1).
  • "$K^c$ ka eigenvector PC hai" — nahi. $\beta\in\mathbb R^n$ points ki space mein hai; PC $w=\tilde\Phi\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ feature space mein (B12 jaisa pul).
  • "Components $\le d$" — nahi (P1 option 1, P4). Limit $\min(D_{\text{eff}},n-1)$.
  • "Kernel PCA hamesha 2-D mein sundar tasveer deta hai" — nahi. Naya variance bahut saare components mein bikhra hota hai (Visual 3: PC1 sirf $14.9\%$), aur sahi kernel / parameter chunna padta hai.
  • "$\lambda_{K^c}$ hi covariance ka eigenvalue hai" — nahi, $n$ se divide karo: $\lambda/n$ (P3).

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

Setting: points $x_1,\dots,x_n\in\mathbb R^d$; kernel $k(x,y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$; $\Phi=[\phi(x_1)\cdots\phi(x_n)]$ (columns), $K=\Phi^T\Phi$, $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$. $\phi$ shayad infinite-dimensional ho, isliye $\Phi$ sirf soch mein hai — hum sirf $K$ se kaam karte hain. Convention $C=\tfrac1n\tilde\Phi\tilde\Phi^T$ (B5, B12 se).

N19 (centering in feature space).

$$\tilde\phi(x_i)=\phi(x_i)-\bar\phi,\qquad \bar\phi=\frac1n\sum_{j=1}^n\phi(x_j),\qquad \tilde\Phi=\Phi H.$$
  • Symbols: $\bar\phi$ = feature-space mean; $\tilde\Phi$ = centred feature matrix; $H=I-\tfrac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$ (N20).
  • Assumption: PCA ko mean-zero data chahiye (B4). Yahan "data" $\phi(x_i)$ hai, $x_i$ nahi.
  • Meaning in words: har feature vector se unka average ghatao. Hum $\bar\phi$ ko explicitly nahi nikaal sakte (bahut bada / infinite), par uske dot products $K$ se nikal sakte hain.

Notice that… input centred ($\sum x_i=0$) hone se $\bar\phi=0$ nahi hota jab $\phi$ nonlinear ho. Example 1: points $-1,0,1$, $\phi(x)=(1,\sqrt2x,x^2)$ ⇒ $\bar\phi=(1,0,\tfrac23)\ne0$.

N20 (centered Gram matrix) — main formula, P5 ka formula.

$$K^c=HKH,\quad H=I-\tfrac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T;\qquad k^c_{ij}=k_{ij}-\theta_i-\theta_j+P,\quad \theta_i=\frac1n\sum_{l=1}^nk_{il},\ \ P=\frac1{n^2}\sum_{a,b}k_{ab}.$$
  • Symbols: $\theta_i$ = $K$ ki $i$-th row ka mean; $P$ = poore $K$ ka mean (grand mean). Source ka hint $k_{ij}-\theta_i\mathbf 1_j^T-\mathbf 1_i\theta_j^T+P\mathbf 1_i\mathbf 1_j^T$ isi ka entry-wise roop hai.
  • Meaning in words: $K^c_{ij}=\tilde\phi(x_i)^T\tilde\phi(x_j)$ — centred features ka dot product. Har entry mein $i$-th aur $j$-th row ka mean ghatao, aur grand mean ek baar wapas jodo (kyunki do baar ghatane se ek hissa dobara kat gaya).
  • Proof (ek line): $\tilde\Phi=\Phi H\Rightarrow K^c=\tilde\Phi^T\tilde\Phi=H\Phi^T\Phi H=HKH$ ($H$ symmetric). Entry-wise: $\tilde\phi_i^T\tilde\phi_j=\phi_i^T\phi_j-\phi_i^T\bar\phi-\bar\phi^T\phi_j+\bar\phi^T\bar\phi=k_{ij}-\theta_j-\theta_i+P$.
  • Properties (checks): symmetric; PSD ($=\tilde\Phi^T\tilde\Phi$); har row ka jod $0$ ($K^c\mathbf 1=0$); diagonal $\ge0$; rank $\le n-1$; $\operatorname{tr}K^c=\sum_i\|\tilde\phi(x_i)\|^2$.

Small example: P5 ka data: $K=\begin{bmatrix}4&1\\1&4\end{bmatrix}$, $\theta=(2.5,2.5)$, $P=2.5$ ⇒ $K^c_{11}=4-2.5-2.5+2.5=1.5$, $K^c_{12}=1-2.5-2.5+2.5=-1.5$ ⇒ $K^c=\begin{bmatrix}1.5&-1.5\\-1.5&1.5\end{bmatrix}$ (full solution P5 mein).

Important insight: centering Gram matrix par ek matrix operation hai ($HKH$) — feature space mein kabhi jaana nahi padta. Isliye "infinite-dimensional Gaussian space mein centering" bhi $n\times n$ arithmetic hai.

This is why… P5 jaise sawaal mein $K$ banana (kernel evaluate karna) aur phir centering formula lagana hi poora sawaal hai.

N21 (kernel-PCA eigenproblem) — B12 M2/M3 ka direct copy, $K\to K^c$.

$$K^c\beta_k=\lambda_k\beta_k\ (\|\beta_k\|=1,\ \lambda_k>0),\qquad \text{variance}_k=\frac{\lambda_k}{n},\qquad \text{EVR}_k=\frac{\lambda_k}{\sum_j\lambda_j}=\frac{\lambda_k}{\operatorname{tr}K^c},$$ $$w_k=\tilde\Phi\,\frac{\beta_k}{\sqrt{\lambda_k}}=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha^{(k)}_i\,\tilde\phi(x_i),\qquad \alpha^{(k)}=\frac{\beta_k}{\sqrt{\lambda_k}}.$$
  • Symbols: $\lambda_k,\beta_k$ = $K^c$ ke eigenpairs; $w_k$ = $k$-th kernel PC (feature space mein, unit); $\alpha^{(k)}$ = coefficients (P5 stem ka $w_k=\phi(X)\alpha_k$ isi ka roop hai).
  • Assumption: $\lambda_k>0$; $\beta_k$ unit.
  • Meaning in words: $\tilde\Phi\tilde\Phi^T$ (feature-space covariance $\times n$) aur $\tilde\Phi^T\tilde\Phi=K^c$ ke nonzero eigenvalues same hain (B12 M2: $\tilde\Phi\tilde\Phi^T(\tilde\Phi\beta)=\tilde\Phi K^c\beta=\lambda(\tilde\Phi\beta)$). $\|\tilde\Phi\beta\|^2=\beta^TK^c\beta=\lambda$, isliye $\sqrt\lambda$ se divide. Covariance $\tfrac1n\tilde\Phi\tilde\Phi^T$ ka eigenvalue $\lambda/n$.

Notice that… $w_k$ ko likhne ke liye $\tilde\phi(x_i)$ chahiye — jo hum nahi jaante. Isliye kernel PCA mein PC "implicit" rehta hai; sirf $\alpha$ aur scores kaam ke hain.

Important insight: P3 isi se: top eigenvalue of $K^c=10$, $n=100$ ⇒ covariance ka top eigenvalue $10/100=0.1$. $\phi$ ya kernel ka degree jaanna zaroori nahi.

N22 (scores).

$$z_{ik}=w_k^T\tilde\phi(x_i)=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,\beta_{k,i}\quad(\text{training});\qquad z_k(x)=\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha^{(k)}_j\,\tilde k(x,x_j)=\sum_{j=1}^n\alpha^{(k)}_j\bigl(k(x,x_j)-\theta_j\bigr)\quad(\text{naya }x).$$
  • Symbols: $\tilde k(x,x_j)=\tilde\phi(x)^T\tilde\phi(x_j)=k(x,x_j)-\tfrac1n\sum_lk(x,x_l)-\theta_j+P$ — naye point ke liye centred kernel; vector roop $\tilde k_x=H\bigl(k_x-\tfrac1nK\mathbf 1\bigr)$, $k_x=(k(x,x_1),\dots,k(x,x_n))^T$.
  • Why the shortcut (second equality): $K^c\mathbf 1=0$ aur symmetric ⇒ $\beta_k\perp\mathbf 1$ ($\lambda_k>0$) ⇒ $\sum_j\alpha_j=0$. To $\tfrac1n\sum_lk(x,x_l)$ aur $P$ wale terms $\sum_j\alpha_j(\cdot)$ mein bahar nikalkar $0$ ho jaate hain; sirf $-\theta_j$ bachta hai.
  • Meaning in words: naye point ka score = uski training points se kernel-similarities (mean $\theta_j$ hata ke) ka $\alpha$-weighted jod. Training point par $z_i=\sqrt\lambda\beta_i$ (B12 M4 jaisa).

Notice that… $\theta_j$ ghatana zaroori hai. Bina uske answer galat ho sakta hai jab $\alpha\cdot\theta\ne0$ (Example 3: sahi $0.393$, galat $0$).

N23 (kitne components).

$$\#\{\lambda_k>0\}\ \le\ \min\bigl(D_{\text{eff}},\,n-1\bigr),\qquad D_{\text{eff}}=\dim\bigl(\text{affine hull of }\phi(x_1),\dots,\phi(x_n)\bigr).$$
  • Symbols: $D_{\text{eff}}\le D$ = feature space ki (effective) dimension; $n-1$ centering ki wajah se.
  • Meaning in words: limit $d$ (input dimension) se nahi, feature space aur sample count se aati hai. Standard PCA mein $\phi(x)=x$, $D_{\text{eff}}=d$ — wahan $\le\min(d,n-1)$ (B10 J5).
  • Values: $(1+x^Ty)^m$: $D=\binom{d+m}{m}$, constant coordinate ka variance zero ⇒ $D_{\text{eff}}=\binom{d+m}m-1$. $(x^Ty)^m$: $D_{\text{eff}}=\binom{d+m-1}{m}$. Gaussian: $\infty$ ⇒ bound $=n-1$ (generic points). Sab code-verified neeche P2/P4 mein.

Important insight: P4 ka "Yes" aur P1 ke option 1 ka "False" isi ek line se: nonlinear $\phi$ ke liye $D_{\text{eff}}$ ya $n-1$ $d$ se bahut bada ho sakta hai.

N24 (linear kernel ⇒ standard PCA). $k(x,y)=x^Ty$: $K=X^TX$, $K^c=\tilde X^T\tilde X$ ($\tilde X$ = column-centred $X$). Isliye eigenvalues $=n\times$ (standard PCA ke variances), scores same (sign tak), aur centering automatically ho jaati hai (input pehle se centre karne ki zaroorat nahi). Code: random $6\times3$ data par $K^c$, $\tilde X^T\tilde X$ aur $n\,C$ ke eigenvalues teeno $17.762,\ 1.550,\ 0.411$ ✓.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

A. Training (kernel PCA)

  1. Kernel aur uske parameters chuno (degree $m$, constant $c\ge0$, ya $\sigma$). Kernel valid hona chahiye (B14–B15).
  2. Gram matrix banao: $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$, $n\times n$ symmetric. (Input ko pehle centre karne ki zaroorat nahi — par karne se koi nuksaan bhi nahi.)
  3. Centre karo: $\theta_i$ = $i$-th row ka mean, $P$ = poore $K$ ka mean, $k^c_{ij}=k_{ij}-\theta_i-\theta_j+P$ (ya $K^c=HKH$).
  4. Checks (10 second): $K^c$ symmetric? har row ka jod $0$? diagonal $\ge0$? Teeno hone chahiye.
  5. Eigen-decompose karo: $K^c\beta_k=\lambda_k\beta_k$, $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$, har $\beta_k$ unit.
  6. Sirf $\lambda_k>0$ rakho (count $\le\min(D_{\text{eff}},n-1)$). Variance $\lambda_k/n$; EVR $\lambda_k/\operatorname{tr}K^c$; kitne rakhne hain — B10 ka CEV rule.
  7. Coefficients $\alpha^{(k)}=\beta_k/\sqrt{\lambda_k}$. Training scores $z_{ik}=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,\beta_{k,i}$.

B. Naya point $x$

  1. $k_x=(k(x,x_1),\dots,k(x,x_n))^T$ nikalo ($n$ kernel evaluations).
  2. $\theta$ ghatao: $z_k(x)=\sum_j\alpha^{(k)}_j\,(k(x,x_j)-\theta_j)$ (N22; $\sum_j\alpha_j=0$ ki wajah se baaki terms ghayab).

C. Sanity checks

  1. $\sum_k\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}K^c=\sum_i K^c_{ii}$ (eigenvalues ka jod = diagonal ka jod).
  2. Training score ka jod $\sum_iz_{ik}=0$ (kyunki $\beta_k\perp\mathbf 1$).
  3. Linear kernel par standard PCA se compare (N24).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. $K^c=HKH$ — proof, aur $H$ ke gun

$H=I-\tfrac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$ symmetric hai aur $H^2=H$ (idempotent): $H^2=I-\tfrac2n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T+\tfrac1{n^2}\mathbf 1(\mathbf 1^T\mathbf 1)\mathbf 1^T=I-\tfrac1n\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$. $H\mathbf 1=0$. Isliye $K^c\mathbf 1=HKH\mathbf 1=0$ — rows ka jod zero. $\Phi H$ ka $j$-th column $\phi(x_j)-\tfrac1n\Phi\mathbf 1=\phi(x_j)-\bar\phi$ ✓ (N19). Entry-wise expand: $(HKH)_{ij}=k_{ij}-\tfrac1n\sum_ak_{aj}-\tfrac1n\sum_bk_{ib}+\tfrac1{n^2}\sum_{a,b}k_{ab}=k_{ij}-\theta_j-\theta_i+P$ (symmetric $K$ ⇒ column mean = row mean). Code: matrix aur entry-wise forms $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ difference $\sim10^{-16}$.

2. Component count — $D_{\text{eff}}$ ke asli numbers (code-verified)

Centred rank = $\dim\operatorname{span}\{\phi(x_i)-\bar\phi\}$ = affine hull ki dimension. $(1+x^Ty)^m$ ke features = degree $\le m$ ke saare monomials (constant shamil): $D=\binom{d+m}m$. Constant coordinate sab points par same ⇒ centering ke baad $0$ ⇒ $D_{\text{eff}}=D-1$. $(x^Ty)^m$ (homogeneous) mein constant nahi hota ⇒ $D_{\text{eff}}=\binom{d+m-1}m$.

3. Linear kernel = standard PCA (N24 ka proof)

$k=x^Ty$ ⇒ $K=X^TX$. $H$ ko $X$ par lagao: $K^c=HX^TXH=(XH)^T(XH)=\tilde X^T\tilde X$, jahan $\tilde X=XH$ column-centred data hai. B12 M2 ke hisaab se $\tilde X^T\tilde X$ aur $\tilde X\tilde X^T=nC$ ke nonzero eigenvalues same ⇒ $\lambda_{K^c}=n\lambda_C$ ⇒ variance $\lambda_{K^c}/n=\lambda_C$ ✓. PCs $w=\tilde X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ = standard PCA ke PCs. Isliye kernel PCA standard PCA ka generalisation hai: kernel badlo, baaki same.

4. Naye point ke liye centred kernel — derivation

$\tilde k(x,x_j)=(\phi(x)-\bar\phi)^T(\phi(x_j)-\bar\phi)=k(x,x_j)-\bar\phi^T\phi(x_j)-\phi(x)^T\bar\phi+\bar\phi^T\bar\phi$. Ab $\bar\phi^T\phi(x_j)=\tfrac1n\sum_lk(x_l,x_j)=\theta_j$, $\phi(x)^T\bar\phi=\tfrac1n\sum_lk(x,x_l)=:\bar k_x$, $\bar\phi^T\bar\phi=P$. To $\tilde k(x,x_j)=k(x,x_j)-\bar k_x-\theta_j+P$. Vector roop: $\tilde k_x=k_x-\bar k_x\mathbf 1-\theta+P\mathbf 1=H(k_x-\theta)$ [kyunki $H(k_x-\theta)=k_x-\theta-\mathbf 1\overline{(k_x-\theta)}$ aur $\overline{(k_x-\theta)}=\bar k_x-P$]. Score $z=\alpha^T\tilde k_x=\alpha^Tk_x-\bar k_x\sum\alpha_j-\alpha^T\theta+P\sum\alpha_j=\alpha^T(k_x-\theta)$ kyunki $\sum\alpha_j=0$. Training point par $x=x_i$: $z=\alpha^T(K_{\cdot i}-\theta)=\alpha^TK^c_{\cdot i}=\tfrac{(K^c\beta)_i}{\sqrt\lambda}=\sqrt\lambda\beta_i$ ✓ (N22 ka pehla roop).

5. Reconstruction aur pre-image — kernel PCA ki seema

Feature space mein reconstruction error nikal sakte hain: $\|\tilde\phi(x_i)\|^2-\sum_{k\le K'}z_{ik}^2=K^c_{ii}-\sum_{k\le K'}\lambda_k\beta_{k,i}^2$ (B11 jaisa). Par input space mein wapas jaana (pre-image: kaun sa $x$ ke $\phi(x)$ reconstructed vector ke paas hai) ka koi closed-form nahi, aur answer unique bhi nahi. Isliye B11 ka "storage / reconstruct" hisaab kernel PCA mein seedha nahi lagta; yahan $\alpha$ aur $n$ points store karne padte hain. (Yeh bank ke kisi sawaal mein nahi aaya.)

7. Cost aur scaling

$K$ banana $O(n^2)$ kernel evaluations; eigen-decomposition $O(n^3)$; memory $O(n^2)$. Naya point: $O(n)$ evaluations. Standard PCA: $O(d^3)$ (primal). Isliye kernel PCA bade $n$ par mehenga hai, aur $d\gg n$ par dual (B12) tarah sasta. (Order-of-magnitude, exact timings nahi.)

3. 🧮 Option-by-option working

Option 1 — "components $\le d$"

M1 · Direct reasoning

Step 1: Nonzero components ki sankhya $=\operatorname{rank}K^c\le\min(D_{\text{eff}},\,n-1)$ (N23).

Step 2: Is bound mein $d$ aata hi nahi; $D_{\text{eff}}$ feature space ki dimension hai, jo $d$ se bahut badi (ya $\infty$) ho sakti hai.

M2 · Concrete witness (Example 1)

Step 2: $K^c=\tfrac19\begin{bmatrix}19&-2&-17\\-2&4&-2\\-17&-2&19\end{bmatrix}$ ke eigenvalues $4,\ \tfrac23,\ 0$.

Step 3: Do nonzero eigenvalues, jabki $d=1$ ⇒ $2>1$. Statement toot gayi. False ✗.

Option 2 — "har $\phi$ ke liye valid kernel"

M1 · Direct

Step 1: Koi bhi $\phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^D$ lo. Define $k(x,y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$.

Step 2: Yeh symmetric hai, aur kisi bhi points ke liye $K=\Phi^T\Phi$ ($\Phi$ ke columns $\phi(x_i)$) ⇒ $c^TKc=\|\Phi c\|^2\ge0$ (PSD).

Step 3: To $k$ valid hai. True ✓.

M2 · Concrete witness

Step 1: Ek ajeeb $\phi$ lo: $\phi(x)=(\sin x,\ \cos x)^T$, $d=1$, $D=2$.

Step 2: $k(x,y)=\sin x\sin y+\cos x\cos y=\cos(x-y)$.

Step 3: Points $0,\ 1,\ 2$: Gram ke eigenvalues $\ge0$ (code: $\min\approx8\times10^{-17}$, yaani $0$ tak). $\phi$ jo bhi ho, $\phi^T\phi$ valid. True ✓.

Option 3 — "$ak_1+bk_2$, $a,b>0$"

M1 · Feature map

Step 1: $k_1=\phi_1^T\phi_1$, $k_2=\phi_2^T\phi_2$.

Step 2: $\phi=(\sqrt a\,\phi_1,\ \sqrt b\,\phi_2)$ (concatenate) ⇒ $\phi(x)^T\phi(y)=ak_1+bk_2$ ($\sqrt a,\sqrt b$ real kyunki $a,b>0$).

Step 3: True ✓ (C1).

M2 · PSD quadratic form

Step 1: Koi bhi $c$: $c^T(aK_1+bK_2)c=a\,c^TK_1c+b\,c^TK_2c$.

Step 2: Dono terms $\ge0$ kyunki $a,b>0$ aur $K_1,K_2$ PSD.

Step 3: Sum $\ge0$; symmetric bhi ⇒ PSD ⇒ True ✓.

Option 4 — "kernel kabhi negative nahi"

M1 · Direct

Step 1: Guarantee sirf diagonal par hai: $k(x,x)=\|\phi(x)\|^2\ge0$.

Step 2: Off-diagonal $k(x,y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$ do vectors ka dot product hai — negative ho sakta hai (opposite directions).

Step 3: False ✗.

M2 · Concrete witness

Step 1: $k(x,y)=xy$ ($\phi(x)=x$), valid.

Step 2: $k(1,-1)=-1<0$.

Step 3: Ek valid kernel ne negative value di ⇒ False ✗.

📌 Memory card — 3.4 Kernel PCA

Core model: Kernel PCA = dual PCA $+$ feature-space centering. $K\to K^c=HKH$, eigenpair $(\lambda,\beta)$ of $K^c$; PC variance $\lambda/n$; training score $\sqrt\lambda\,\beta_i$.

Backbone chain: kernel $k$ → $K_{ij}$ → $\theta_i$, $P$ → $K^c_{ij}=k_{ij}-\theta_i-\theta_j+P$ → $K^c\beta=\lambda\beta$ ($\|\beta\|=1$) → keep $\lambda>0$ → $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ → score $z(x)=\sum_j\alpha_j(k(x,x_j)-\theta_j)$.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $K^c=HKH$, $H=I-\tfrac1n\mathbf1\mathbf1^T$ — sab kernels, hamesha
  • $\lambda_C=\lambda_{K^c}/n$; EVR $=\lambda_k/\operatorname{tr}K^c$ ($n$ cancel)
  • $K^c$: symmetric, PSD, rows ka jod $0$, diag $\ge0$, rank $\le n-1$
  • Components $\le\min(D_{\text{eff}},n-1)$; $(1+x^Ty)^m$: $\binom{d+m}m-1$; $(x^Ty)^m$: $\binom{d+m-1}m$; Gaussian: $n-1$
  • Linear kernel ⇒ standard PCA ($\le d$)
  • Naye point: $z=\sum\alpha_j(k(x,x_j)-\theta_j)$ ($\sum\alpha_j=0$)

Top 3 traps: (1) $K$ ko $K^c$ maan lena ya "input centred ⇒ skip centering"; centering formula mein $+P$ bhoolna (P5). (2) $\lambda_{K^c}$ ko variance likhna ($\div n$ bhoolna, P3) ya $\div(n-1)$. (3) "components $\le d$" ya "PC explicit hai" — kernel PCA mein $k>d$ possible, aur PC $\phi$-space mein hai, input space mein nahi (P1, P2, P4).
