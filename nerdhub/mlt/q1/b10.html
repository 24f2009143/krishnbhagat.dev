  2. (B8 Pythagoras) $w_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$, $w_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(-1,1)$, centred point $\tilde x=(3,1)$. $(z_1,z_2)$ aur $z_1^2+z_2^2$?

2023 October · Original Q7 · ID 640653669239 · SA · 4 marks · [SRC]

Consider a dataset of 50 points, all of which lie in $R^5$. The eigenvalues of the covariance matrix are: $1.7,\ 1.4,\ 0.25,\ 0.20,\ 0.005$. If PCA is run and the top-$k$ principal components are retained, what is a good choice of $k$?

Hint: A good PCA should capture at least 95% of the variance.

2 minute, bina neeche padhe. Answer + Confidence S / U / G note karo.

🧭 Position — yeh kahan baitha hai

Peeche (prerequisites)Yeh subtopicAage (kya unlock hota hai)
B4: variance, covariance, eigenvalues, $\operatorname{tr}C=\sum\lambda$ · B7: variance along PC $j$ $=\lambda_j$ · B8: G4 Pythagoras, G5 score covariance $\lambda_l\delta_{kl}$ · B9: dual $\lambda_K=n\lambda_C$, rank $\le n-1$ Total variance, explained variance ratio, cumulative variance, threshold se $k$ chunna, rank = nonzero eigenvalues ki ginti B11: reconstruction error $=$ discarded eigenvalues ka jod, storage ($k(d+n)$ numbers) · B12: dual PCA mein rank aur Gram matrix · B16: kernel PCA mein centred kernel ke eigenvalues

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Total variance ek "budget" hai. Data kitna phailta hai — sab directions ko milake. PCA is budget ko perpendicular directions mein baant deta hai: PC$_j$ ke hisse mein $\lambda_j$ aata hai. Sab hisse jodo to wapas poora budget milta hai.
  • Explained variance ratio = hissa ÷ budget. "PC1 ne 48% explain kiya" ka matlab: total phailav ka 48% PC1 ki line par hai.
  • $k$ chunna = kitna loss afford karoge. "95% chahiye" ka matlab: discard kiye hue chhote eigenvalues ka jod total ka $\le5\%$ ho. Isliye hum sabse chhote eigenvalues pehle discard karte hain.
  • Discarded variance hi reconstruction error hai. Jo hissa discard hua, wahi average squared error ban jaata hai (B11 mein poora proof; yahan bas J6).
  • Rank = kitne PCs ke paas sach mein variance hai. Jin directions mein data bilkul flat hai (variance 0), unke eigenvalues 0 hain; unhe rakhne ka koi fayda nahi. Centred $n$ points ke liye zyada se zyada $n-1$ nonzero eigenvalues.
  • Ratio scale se bachta hai. $C$ ho ya $X^TX$ ($=n\times$ wahi nonzero eigenvalues), hissa ÷ budget same: $n$ cancel ho jaata hai.

Analogy [ADDED] — ek pizza ke slices. Poora pizza = total variance. PCs sabse bade slice se chhote slice ki taraf: $1.7,\ 1.4,\ 0.25,\ 0.20,\ 0.005$. "95% chahiye" matlab itne bade slices uthao ki pizza ka kam se kam 95% tumhari plate mein ho; bacha hua chhota tukda chhod do.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Pizza ke slices barabar kaam ke hote hain; variance ka bada hissa "zyada useful" hone ki guarantee nahi — PCA unsupervised hai, label nahi dekhta. (2) Slices ko tum jahan se chaho kaat sakte ho; PCs ka order aur directions data se tay hote hain, koi choice nahi. (3) Pizza mein "95%" koi rule nahi; PCA mein bhi 95% sirf ek convention hai (sawaal mein hint ke roop mein diya gaya).

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "Explained variance ratio $=\lambda_k$" — nahi. Ratio $\lambda_k/\sum_j\lambda_j$ hai; $\lambda_k$ khud variance hai (unit$^2$ mein), ratio unitless.
  • "$95\%$ ka matlab $0.95\,d$ components" — nahi. Kitne components lagenge yeh spectrum (eigenvalues ka shape) tay karta hai.
  • "$n$ points hain to $n$ components" — nahi. Centred data mein rank $\le n-1$, aur $d$ se bhi bada nahi.
  • "Bada explained variance = achhi classification" — nahi. PCA labels nahi dekhta.
  • "$X^TX$ ke eigenvalues covariance ke variances hain" — nahi. Woh $n$ guna bade hain; sirf ratio same rehta hai.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\text{(J1)}\ \ V_{\text{tot}}=\operatorname{tr}(C)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde x_i\|^2$$ $$\text{(J2)}\ \ \text{EVR}_k=\frac{\lambda_k}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_j},\qquad \text{CEV}(k)=\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{k}\lambda_j}{\sum_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_j}$$ $$\text{(J3)}\ \ k^*=\min\{k:\ \text{CEV}(k)\ge\tau\}\iff\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j\le(1-\tau)\,V_{\text{tot}}$$ $$\text{(J4)}\ \ \lambda_j(X^TX)=n\,\lambda_j(C)\ \Longrightarrow\ \text{EVR}_k\ \text{same from }C\text{ or }X^TX$$ $$\text{(J5)}\ \ \#\{j:\lambda_j>0\}=\operatorname{rank}(C)=\operatorname{rank}(X)\le\min(d,\,n-1)$$ $$\text{(J6)}\ \ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|\tilde x_i-\hat x_i\|^2=\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j=\bigl(1-\text{CEV}(k)\bigr)V_{\text{tot}}$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $X\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ — data matrix, columns $x_i$ ($d$ features, $n$ points); dataset centred. $C=\tfrac1nXX^T\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ (convention $1/n$, B4).
  • $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\dots\ge\lambda_d\ge0$ — $C$ ke eigenvalues, descending order mein. $\lambda_j$ = variance along PC$_j$ (B7).
  • $V_{\text{tot}}$ — total variance (assume $V_{\text{tot}}>0$). $\tau\in(0,1]$ — threshold (jaise $0.95$). $k^*$ — sabse chhota qualifying $k$.
  • $\hat x_i$ — $\tilde x_i$ ka top-$k$ PCs se reconstruction ($\hat x=W_kW_k^T\tilde x$, B8 G6). $\sum_{j>k}$ — $j=k+1,\dots,d$.
  • J4: $K=X^TX$ ($n\times n$) ke nonzero eigenvalues $XX^T$ ke nonzero eigenvalues ke barabar hain; $C=\tfrac1nXX^T$ isliye $\lambda_j(K)=n\lambda_j(C)$ (B9). Dono mein zero eigenvalues ki ginti alag ho sakti hai, par nonzero hisse same.
  • J5: $X$ centred hai (columns ka jod 0), isliye rank $\le n-1$; aur rank $\le d$ hamesha.

Words mein: J1 — total variance sab eigenvalues ka jod (aur sab points ki squared length ka average). J2 — hissa = eigenvalue ÷ jod; cumulative = pehle $k$ ka jod ÷ jod. J3 — sabse chhota $k$ jahan cumulative threshold paar kare; ya, ulte tareeke se, discarded tail budget $(1-\tau)V_{\text{tot}}$ ke andar ho. J4 — $X^TX$ ya $C$ se ratio same. J5 — nonzero eigenvalues ki ginti = rank. J6 — discarded eigenvalues = average squared reconstruction error.

Notice that… (1) $\text{CEV}(k)$ $k$ ke saath badhta hai aur $\text{CEV}(d)=1$ (aur $\text{CEV}(\text{rank})=1$ bhi). (2) J3 mein "$\ge$" hai, "$>$" nahi — exactly 95% bhi qualify karta hai (Example 3). (3) Ratio mein sirf eigenvalues ka anupaat aata hai, isliye data ko ek hi constant $c$ se scale karne par EVR nahi badalta.

Small worked example [ADDED]: $\lambda=(4,1)$ (B4 dataset). $V_{\text{tot}}=5$. $\text{EVR}_1=\tfrac45=0.8$, $\text{EVR}_2=0.2$, $\text{CEV}(1)=0.8$. $k=1$ par discard kiya hua $=\lambda_2=1$ $\Rightarrow$ J6 se average squared error $=1$, jo $0.2\times5=1$ ✓ (code-verified).

Important insight: "kitne PCs rakhein" ka sawaal sirf eigenvalue spectrum par tika hai — directions $w_j$ ki zaroorat hi nahi. Isliye PYQs mein sirf eigenvalues diye jaate hain.

This is why… P1 aur P4 mein data, points, ya PCs ki jagah sirf eigenvalues diye gaye hain: ek line mein ratio nikal jaata hai.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Eigenvalues kis matrix ke hain? $C$ (covariance) ya $K=X^TX$ (Gram) — likha dekho. Ratio dono se same (J4); absolute variance maange to $K$ wale ko $n$ se bhaago.
  2. Descending order mein likho. Agar list shuffle hai to pehle sort karo; cumulative tabhi sahi hai.
  3. Total nikalo: "baaki sab zero" likha ho to sirf listed eigenvalues ka jod (J1). Poori list dekho; ek bhi chhooti to ratio galat.
  4. Ratio ya cumulative: "proportion along first PC" ⇒ $\lambda_1/V_{\text{tot}}$. "$\ge$ threshold" ⇒ cumulative.
  5. Threshold sawaal: upar se jodte jao, pehla $k$ jahan $\text{CEV}(k)\ge\tau$ — par ek step peeche ($k-1$) bhi fail check karo.
  6. Shortcut (tail budget): $(1-\tau)V_{\text{tot}}$ nikalo; sabse chhote eigenvalues ek-ek karke discard karo jab tak budget na toot'e (J3 ka doosra roop).
  7. Rank: nonzero eigenvalues gino; centred ho to $\le n-1$ aur $\le d$ (J5). $d\gg n$ mein $K$ ($n\times n$) se gino.
  8. Format: decimal maange to decimal ($0.476$), percent nahi; rounding end mein.

Spectrum $6,3,1$, $V_{\text{tot}}=10$, $\tau=0.90$. $\text{CEV}=(0.60,\,0.90,\,1.00)$. $\text{CEV}(2)=0.90$ exactly ⇒ "at least 90%" mein $k^*=2$ (J3 mein $\ge$). Example 6 — tricky: independent features, phir bhi chhota $k$ [ADDED]

Do spectra, dono "independent features" ke ($C$ diagonal). (a) $d=21$, sab variances $1$: $\text{CEV}(19)=0.9048$, $\text{CEV}(20)=0.9524$ ⇒ $k^*=20=d-1$. 🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. J1: $\operatorname{tr}(C)=\sum\lambda_j=\tfrac1n\sum\|\tilde x_i\|^2$ — proof

Trace linear hai aur $\operatorname{tr}(ab^T)=b^Ta$: $\operatorname{tr}(C)=\tfrac1n\sum_i\operatorname{tr}(\tilde x_i\tilde x_i^T)$, phir $=\tfrac1n\sum_i\tilde x_i^T\tilde x_i$, phir $=\tfrac1n\sum_i\|\tilde x_i\|^2$. Ab $C=W\Lambda W^T$ ($W$ orthogonal, B4 spectral theorem): $\operatorname{tr}(C)=\operatorname{tr}(W\Lambda W^T)$, $=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda W^TW)$, $=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda)=\sum_j\lambda_j$ (cyclic property). B4 dataset: $\tfrac14(\ldots)=5=4+1$ ✓.

2. J6: average squared reconstruction error $=\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j$ — proof

B8 G3, G4 se: $\tilde x_i=\sum_jz_{ij}w_j$ aur residual $r_i=\tilde x_i-\hat x_i=\sum_{j>k}z_{ij}w_j$ (orthonormal $w_j$) ⇒ $\|r_i\|^2=\sum_{j>k}z_{ij}^2$. Average lo: $\tfrac1n\sum_i\|r_i\|^2=\sum_{j>k}\tfrac1n\sum_iz_{ij}^2=\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j$ (G5: PC$_j$ scores ka variance $\lambda_j$, mean 0). Code-check: $d=200$, $n=12$, $k=3$ aur $k=7$ dono par error aur tail sum barabar nikle (114.64 aur 48.87).

3. J5: $\operatorname{rank}\le n-1$ aur $d\gg n$ ka matlab

$\operatorname{rank}(XX^T)=\operatorname{rank}(X)$, aur centred $X$ ke columns ka jod $0$ hai, to columns linearly dependent: $X\mathbf 1=0$ ⇒ rank $\le n-1$. $C$ ke nonzero eigenvalues ki ginti $=\operatorname{rank}(X)\le\min(d,n-1)$. $d\gg n$ mein $d$ eigenvalues mein se kam se kam $d-n+1$ zero hain; isliye top $d-1$ eigenvectors hamesha 100% variance rakhte hain (P3 ka [?] yahin se aata hai).

4. Ratio kab badalta hai: scaling vs standardisation

Poore data ko $c\neq0$ se guna karo: $C\to c^2C$, saare $\lambda_j\to c^2\lambda_j$, ratio same. Par ek feature ko alag scale karo (jaise cm se m) to $C$ ke eigenvalues ka anupaat badal jaata hai, kyunki variance ek axis mein alag se chhoti/badi ho jaati hai. Isi liye features ko standardise karte hain; tab $C$ correlation matrix ban jaata hai, jiska trace $=d$ (har feature variance 1). Bank ke sawaal sab "diya hua $C$/eigenvalues" par chalte hain, isliye yahan zaroorat nahi.

5. "$d-1$ eigenvectors lagte hain" kya batata hai (P3 ke liye, code-verified)

Maan lo $k=d-2$ components 95% se kam dete hain par $k=d-1$ dete hain. Pehli shart: $\lambda_{d-1}+\lambda_d>0.05V_{\text{tot}}$. Kyunki $\lambda_d\le\lambda_{d-1}$: $2\lambda_{d-1}>0.05V_{\text{tot}}$ ⇒ $\lambda_{d-1}>0.025V_{\text{tot}}$. Isliye pehle $d-1$ eigenvalues har ek $2.5\%$ se bade hain, unka jod $<100\%$ ⇒ $(d-1)\times0.025<1$ ⇒ $d\le40$. Aur $d$ bada ho to koi ek PC dominate nahi kar sakta ($\lambda_1<1-0.025(d-2)$). Yani spectrum flat hai: variance bahut saari directions mein faila hai, koi chhota set sab explain nahi karta ⇒ features ke beech redundancy kam.

Par yeh heuristic hai: $d=3$, spectrum $(0.90,\,0.07,\,0.03)$: $\text{CEV}(1)=0.90<0.95$, $\text{CEV}(2)=0.97$ ⇒ $d-1=2$ lagte hain, phir bhi $\lambda_1=90\%$ ho sakta hai (features kaafi correlated). Aur $d\gg n$ context mein (P2) "$d-1$ eigenvectors se 95%" shart hamesha sach hai (Deeper 3). Isliye P3 mein key "low correlation" literal implication nahi, intended reading hai.

Consider a dataset of 50 points, all of which lie in $R^5$. The eigenvalues of the covariance matrix are:

$$1.7,\quad 1.4,\quad 0.25,\quad 0.20,\quad 0.005$$

If PCA is run and the top-$k$ principal components are retained, what is a good choice of $k$?

Hint: A good PCA should capture at least 95% of the variance.

✍️ Try first

Total nikalo, phir cumulative. $k=3$ par exact kitna % hai? Confidence: S / U / G?

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: sabse chhota $k$ jo kam se kam 95% variance rakhe.
  • Given: $C$ ke eigenvalues (already descending). $n=50$, $d=5$ — dono is sawaal mein kaam ke nahi (distractor). Eigenvalues seedha covariance ke hain, to $n$ se bhaagna nahi.
  • Trigger clues: "capture at least 95% of the variance", "top-$k$ retained".
  • Prerequisites: B4 variance/trace (Procedural), B7 variance along PC $=\lambda$ (Conceptual) · Core principle: J2–J3 (cumulative explained variance).

2. 🧭 Methods (2 genuinely different)

M1 General — upar se cumulative jodo jab tak $\ge95\%$. M2 Shortcut — neeche se: 5% ka loss-budget nikalo, chhote eigenvalues discard karte jao jab tak budget na toot'e. Dono J3 ke do roop hain par chalte alag tareeke se (head-sum vs tail-budget).

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · upar se cumulative

Step 1 (total): $1.7+1.4=3.1$; $3.1+0.25=3.35$; $3.35+0.20=3.55$; $3.55+0.005=3.555$.

Step 2 ($k=1$): $1.7/3.555=0.4782$ → 47.82%.

Step 3 ($k=2$): $3.1/3.555=0.8720$ → 87.20%.

Step 4 ($k=3$): $3.35/3.555=0.9423$ → 94.23% $<95\%$ ✗.

Step 5 ($k=4$): $3.55/3.555=0.9986$ → 99.86% $\ge95\%$ ✓.

Step 6: pehla qualifying $k=\mathbf4$.

M2 · neeche se loss-budget

Step 1: total $=3.555$ (upar jaisa). Allowed loss $=0.05\times3.555=0.17775$.

Step 2: sabse chhota eigenvalue discard: $0.005\le0.17775$ ✓ ⇒ $k=4$ chalta hai.

Step 3: agla chhota bhi discard: $0.005+0.20=0.205$ ⇒ $0.205>0.17775$ ✗ ⇒ $k=3$ nahi chalta.

Step 4: $k=4$ chalta hai, $k=3$ nahi ⇒ $\mathbf4$.

📌 Memory card — 2.4 explained variance, rank, dimension selection

Core model: total variance $=\sum\lambda_j$; PC$_j$ ka hissa $\lambda_j/\sum\lambda$; $k$ chunna $=$ pehla $k$ jahan cumulative $\ge\tau$; rank $=$ nonzero eigenvalues ki ginti.

Backbone chain: kis matrix ke eigenvalues → descending → total (baaki zero?) → ratio/cumulative → pehla $k$ + $k-1$ fail check → decimal/percent format.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $V_{\text{tot}}=\operatorname{tr}C=\sum_j\lambda_j$ — $1/n$ convention, centred
  • $\text{EVR}_k=\lambda_k/\sum\lambda$, $\text{CEV}(k)=\sum_{j\le k}\lambda_j/\sum\lambda$ — descending order
  • $k^*=\min\{k:\text{CEV}(k)\ge\tau\}$ ⟺ tail $\le(1-\tau)V_{\text{tot}}$ — "$\ge$", not "$>$"
  • $\lambda_j(X^TX)=n\lambda_j(C)$ — ratio same; ek hi matrix use karo
  • rank $\le\min(d,n-1)$ — centred data
  • avg sq. reconstruction error $=\sum_{j>k}\lambda_j$ — $1/n$ convention

Top 3 traps: (1) numerator ko $n$ se bhaaga par denominator nahi; (2) 94.2% ko 95% maan lena / "$\ge$" vs "$>$" boundary; (3) percent vs decimal, ya denominator mein poora jod nahi.

Trigger bank: "at least 95% variance, choose $k$" → cumulative · "proportion along first PC" → $\lambda_1/\sum$ · "eigenvalues of $X^TX$" → $n$ cancel · "$d\gg n$, nonzero eigenvalues" → $\le n-1$ · "$d-1$ eigenvectors capture 95%" → flat spectrum (heuristic, [?]).
