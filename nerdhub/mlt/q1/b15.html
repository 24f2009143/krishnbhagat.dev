  3. $K_1(x,y)+K_2(x,y)$
  4. $K_1(x,y)-K_2(x,y)$

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Normalisation = sabko ek hi lambai par laana. $k(x,x)=\|\phi(x)\|^2$ batata hai ki point $x$ ka feature vector kitna lamba hai. $k(x,y)$ lambai aur angle dono mila ke deta hai. $k(x,y)/\sqrt{k(x,x)k(y,y)}$ lambai nikaal deta hai aur sirf angle ka cosine bachta hai: $\cos\theta\in[-1,1]$. Yeh bhi ek valid kernel hai (har vector ko uski lambai se bhaag diya).
  • Scale rule: kernel ko $c$ se guna = feature ko $\sqrt c$ se guna. Kyunki dot product mein $\phi$ do baar aata hai: $(\sqrt c\,\phi(x))^T(\sqrt c\,\phi(y))=c\,\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$. Yahi P7 ka asli idea hai: $100$ bahar hai, to feature par $\sqrt{100}=10$.
  • Gaussian = exponential kernel ka cosine version. $e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}$ valid hai; usko normalise karo to Gaussian nikalta hai. Isliye Gaussian ka $k(x,x)=1$: sab features unit sphere par hain, aur $k(x,y)=\cos\theta>0$ (angle $90^\circ$ se kam).
  • Distance similarity nahi hai. $\|x-y\|$ ka diagonal $0$ hai par off-diagonal positive — aisi table kisi arrows ki dot-product table ho hi nahi sakti (zero lambai ke arrow ka kisi se bhi dot product $0$ hoga, positive nahi).
  • Jodo ✓, ghatao ✗. Do valid kernels ka positive combination hamesha valid; difference tab hi valid jab difference ki table khud PSD ho — yahan ($x^Ty-\text{Gaussian}$) ka diagonal origin par $-1$ aata hai.

Analogy [ADDED] — shishe wali photo. Har point ek tasveer hai jiska "size" ($\|\phi\|$) alag hai. Normalise karna = sab tasveeron ko ek hi size par chhapna; ab sirf kaisi dikhti hain (angle) bachta hai. $c$ se kernel guna = sab ko ek hi bade size ($\sqrt c$ radius) par chhapna.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Asli feature space aksar bahut bade (ya infinite) dimension ka hota hai; sphere ki 2-D drawing sirf schematic hai. (2) Normalise karne se "kam similar" points ka rank badal sakta hai: do points ka $k$ bada ho par unke lambai bhi bade ho — normalise karne par similarity chhoti ho sakti hai. (3) Normalise sirf tab, jab har point par $k(x,x)>0$ — $\phi(x)=0$ ho to $0/0$ hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "Normalise karne se kernel centered ho jaata hai" — nahi. Normalisation lambai ek karta hai; centering (mean hataana, B16) alag cheez hai.
  • "$100k$ ka $\phi$ hai $100\phi$" — nahi, $10\phi$. $\phi$ ke upar $\sqrt{\text{scale}}$.
  • "$\exp(\text{kuch})$ lagane se kernel valid ho jaata hai" — $e^{\text{valid kernel}}$ valid hai, par $e^{-\|x-y\|^3}$ nahi (neeche example mein).
  • "Valid kernels ka difference bhi valid" — guarantee nahi; yahan $K_1-K_2$ hamesha invalid hai.

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

N15 (normalised / cosine kernel). $k$ valid, $k(x,x)>0$ for all $x$ under consideration:

$$\tilde k(x,y)=\frac{k(x,y)}{\sqrt{k(x,x)}\,\sqrt{k(y,y)}}=\left(\frac{\phi(x)}{\|\phi(x)\|}\right)^{\!T}\!\left(\frac{\phi(y)}{\|\phi(y)\|}\right)=\cos\theta_{xy}.$$
  • Symbols: $\phi$ koi feature map jisse $k=\phi^T\phi$; $\|\phi(x)\|=\sqrt{k(x,x)}$ (kyunki $k(x,x)=\phi(x)^T\phi(x)$); $\theta_{xy}$ = $\phi(x)$ aur $\phi(y)$ ke beech ka angle.
  • Assumption: $\phi(x)\ne0$ har $x$ par (warna $0/0$). $\phi$ wahi dono arguments par.
  • Meaning in words: $\tilde k$ ek valid kernel hai jiska $\phi$ unit-length vectors hain; $\tilde k(x,x)=1$ aur $|\tilde k(x,y)|\le1$.
  • Proof (valid): C3 (N13) with $f(x)=1/\sqrt{k(x,x)}$: $\tilde k(x,y)=f(x)\,k(x,y)\,f(y)$, aur C3 same-$f$ sandwich ko valid rakhta hai. Seedha: $\tilde\phi=\phi/\|\phi\|$.

Notice that… $|\tilde k|\le1$ wahi Cauchy–Schwarz hai jo B14 ke N14(c) mein tha: $k(x,y)^2\le k(x,x)k(y,y)$ ko $k(x,x)k(y,y)$ se bhaago.

N16 (scale rule). $c\ge0$:

$$c\,k(x,y)=\bigl(\sqrt c\,\phi(x)\bigr)^T\bigl(\sqrt c\,\phi(y)\bigr).$$
  • Symbols: $c$ = non-negative constant; $\sqrt c\,\phi$ naya feature map.
  • Meaning in words: kernel ka scale $c$ ho to feature ka scale $\sqrt c$; feature ka scale $\alpha$ ho to kernel ka scale $\alpha^2$.
  • P7 ke liye: $k_1=100\,\tilde k$ ⇒ $\phi_1=\sqrt{100}\,\tilde\phi=10\,\phi/\|\phi\|$.
  • Combined: $a\,\tilde k+b$ ($a,b\ge0$) ka $\phi=\bigl(\sqrt a\,\phi/\|\phi\|,\ \sqrt b\bigr)$ — ek extra constant coordinate.

Important insight (N15+N16): $k_1(x,x)=c$ har point par — sab features radius $\sqrt c$ sphere par. Yeh "sahi option pehchano" ka fastest test hai: option ka $\|\phi_1(x)\|^2$ nikalo aur dekho $c$ aata hai ya nahi (P7: $10^2=100$ ✓).

N17 (Gaussian ke do chehre). $\sigma>0$:

$$e^{-\|x-y\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}\ =\ \underbrace{e^{-\|x\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}}_{f(x)}\ e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}\ \underbrace{e^{-\|y\|^2/(2\sigma^2)}}_{f(y)}\ =\ \frac{e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}}{\sqrt{e^{\|x\|^2/\sigma^2}\,e^{\|y\|^2/\sigma^2}}}.$$
  • Why (algebra): $\|x-y\|^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2-2x^Ty$ ⇒ $-\dfrac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}=-\dfrac{\|x\|^2}{2\sigma^2}-\dfrac{\|y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}+\dfrac{x^Ty}{\sigma^2}$. Exponent ka jod = factors ka product.
  • Valid kyun: $x^Ty/\sigma^2$ valid ⇒ $e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}$ valid (C5) ⇒ same-$f$ sandwich (C3) valid. Doosri tarah: yeh $\exp$-kernel ka N15-normalised roop hai.
  • Meaning in words: Gaussian = $e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}$ ka cosine kernel; isliye $K_2(x,x)=1$ (P10 option 1 ka reason).

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

A. "Naye kernel ka $\phi$ batao" (P7 type)

  1. $k_{\text{new}}$ ko $f(x)\,k(x,y)\,f(y)$ (aur/ya $+$ constant) shape mein likho. Normalisation mein $f(x)=\sqrt c/\sqrt{k(x,x)}$.
  2. $\phi_{\text{new}}=f\cdot\phi$ (aur constant ke liye ek extra coordinate $\sqrt b$).
  3. Sanity: $\|\phi_{\text{new}}(x)\|^2=k_{\text{new}}(x,x)$ nikalo. Normalised case mein yeh $c$ hona chahiye.
  4. Ek numeric pair se option test karo ($\phi(x)=x$, $x=(3,4)$, $y=(4,3)$ jaise: $\|x\|=5$ aasaan).

B. "Kaun se kernels valid hain" (P8 type)

  1. Har option ko kisi known family se match karo: linear, polynomial ($c\ge0$?), Gaussian, Laplacian, combination.
  2. Match nahi hua ya shak hai: pehle $k(x,x)\ge0$ aur $2\times2$ Cauchy–Schwarz $k(x,y)^2\le k(x,x)k(y,y)$ — kam time mein "invalid" pakad leta hai.
  3. Fir bhi pata nahi: do points ($0$ aur ek unit vector) ki $2\times2$ Gram ke eigenvalues.
  4. Valid sabit karne ke liye closure / $\phi$. Zero-diagonal wale formula ($\|x-y\|$) automatic invalid: PSD matrix ka diagonal zero ho to poori row zero hoti hai (D3-4).

C. "Positive combination" (P9 type)

  1. Saare coefficients $\ge0$? Haan ⇒ valid (concatenate scaled features, ya $c^TKc$ ke terms jodo).
  2. Koi minus? "Hamesha valid?" poochha ho to ek concrete valid pair lo jahan $k(x,x)$ negative aaye (e.g. $k_1=x^Ty$, $k_2=$ Gaussian, $x=0$).

D. "$K_1,K_2$ ki list" (P10 type)

  1. Pehle reduce karo: $K_2(x,x)=1$ ⇒ normalisation trivial; $\exp(-\|x\|^2/2\sigma^2)\exp(-\|y\|^2/2\sigma^2)\exp(x^Ty/\sigma^2)=K_2$ (N17).
  2. Reduce karne par $K_1+K_2$ (valid) jaisa roop aaye to ✓. Minus aaye to diagonal origin par dekho: $K_1(0,0)-K_2(0,0)=0-1$.

🧪 Worked examples [ADDED]

Example 1 (simple) — linear kernel ka cosine version, Gram ke saath

$k(x,y)=x^Ty$ on $\mathbb R^2$; points $a=(1,0)$, $b=(0,1)$, $c=(1,1)$.

  1. $k(a,a)=1$, $k(b,b)=1$, $k(c,c)=2$; $k(a,b)=0$, $k(a,c)=1$, $k(b,c)=1$.
  2. $\tilde k(a,c)=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt1\sqrt2}=0.7071$, $\tilde k(b,c)=0.7071$, $\tilde k(a,b)=0$.
  3. Gram of $\tilde k$: $$\tilde K=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0.7071\\0&1&0.7071\\0.7071&0.7071&1\end{bmatrix}.$$ Diagonal $1$ — sab unit vectors.
  4. Eigenvalues $0,\ 1,\ 2$ (code) — sab $\ge0$ ✓. Ek eigenvalue $0$: teen vectors $\mathbb R^2$ mein hain, rank $\le2$.
  5. Angles: $\cos^{-1}0.7071=45^\circ$ ($a,c$), $90^\circ$ ($a,b$) — seedha $\phi(x)/\|\phi(x)\|=x/\|x\|$.

Example 2 (exam-level) — $c\,\tilde k+b$ ka $\phi$, polynomial kernel par

$k(x,y)=(xy+1)^2$ on $\mathbb R$. $k_3(x,y)=4\,\tilde k(x,y)+9$. $\phi_3$ aur $k_3(1,2)$, $k_3(1,1)$ nikalo.

  1. Feature map: $(xy+1)^2=1+2xy+x^2y^2$ ⇒ $\phi(x)=(1,\ \sqrt2x,\ x^2)^T$. Check $x=1,y=2$: $\phi(1)=(1,1.414,1)$, $\phi(2)=(1,2.828,4)$; dot $=1+4+4=9=(2+1)^2$ ✓.
  2. Values: $k(1,1)=4$, $k(2,2)=25$, $k(1,2)=9$ ⇒ $\tilde k(1,2)=\dfrac{9}{\sqrt{4}\sqrt{25}}=\dfrac9{10}=0.9$.
  3. $k_3(1,2)=4(0.9)+9=\mathbf{12.6}$.
  4. $\phi_3(x)=\bigl(2\,\phi(x)/\|\phi(x)\|,\ 3\bigr)\in\mathbb R^4$ ($\sqrt4=2$, $\sqrt9=3$ — scale rule!).
  5. Check: $\phi_3(1)^T\phi_3(2)=4\cos\theta+9=4(0.9)+9=12.6$ ✓; $\phi_3(1)^T\phi_3(1)=4+9=13=k_3(1,1)$ ✓ (code).
  6. Gram of $\tilde k$ on points $1,2,3$ ($k(3,3)=100$, $k(1,3)=16$, $k(2,3)=49$): $$\begin{bmatrix}1&0.9&0.8\\0.9&1&0.98\\0.8&0.98&1\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{eigenvalues }0.0014,\ 0.2100,\ 2.7887\ (\ge0\ \checkmark).$$ Entries $\tilde K_{12}=0.9$, $\tilde K_{13}=16/\sqrt{4\cdot100}=0.8$, $\tilde K_{23}=49/\sqrt{25\cdot100}=0.98$.

Example 3 (tricky) — diagonal pass, par phir bhi invalid; aur "exp laga do" ka dhokha

  • $k(x,y)=(xy-1)^2$ on $\mathbb R$. Even power, to $k(x,x)=(x^2-1)^2\ge0$ ✓ — diagonal test pass. Par points $1,2$: $k(1,1)=0$, $k(2,2)=9$, $k(1,2)=1$ ⇒ $$K=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&9\end{bmatrix},\ \det=-1,\ \text{eigenvalues }-0.110,\ 9.110.$$ Cauchy–Schwarz bhi toot-ta hai: $1^2>0\cdot9$. $c<0$ polynomial kernel mein ek term $-2xy$ aata hai (negative coefficient) — binomial expansion mein coefficient $\ge0$ nahi.
  • $k(x,y)=e^{-|x-y|^3}$ on $\mathbb R$. Dikhne mein Gaussian ($e^{-|x-y|^2}$) jaisa — par points $0,\ 0.5,\ 1,\ 1.5$ par smallest eigenvalue $-0.125$ (code) ⇒ invalid. Exponent $p=1$ (Laplacian) aur $p=2$ (Gaussian) valid hain; $p=3$ nahi.

Non-examples

  • $k(x,y)=\|x-y\|^2$ (squared distance): $k(x,x)=0$ par $k(0,1)=1$ ⇒ Gram on $\{0,1\}$: $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-1,\ 1$ ✗.
  • $\tilde k$ ko invalid $k$ par laga ke "valid banana": $k=-x^Ty$ ke saath $\tilde k$ defined hi nahi ($k(x,x)<0$ ⇒ $\sqrt{\cdot}$ real nahi). Normalisation sirf valid kernel ko valid rakhta hai, kharab ko theek nahi karta.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. Normalisation ≠ centering; aur Gram ka trace

$\tilde K$ ka diagonal $1$ hai, to $\operatorname{tr}\tilde K=n$ (points ki sankhya). Eigenvalues ka jod $n$ — yeh "total variance" jaisi cheez nahi, kyunki yahan mean nahi hataya. Kernel PCA (B16) mein features ko center karna padta hai: $K^c_{ij}=k_{ij}-\theta_i-\theta_j+P$ (source ka hint). Normalise karne ke baad bhi $\tilde\phi$ ka mean zero nahi hota, to centering baad mein alag se lagti hai.

2. Gaussian = exp-kernel ka cosine; features unit sphere par

$\kappa(x,y)=e^{x^Ty/\sigma^2}$ valid (C5). $\tilde\kappa=\kappa/\sqrt{\kappa(x,x)\kappa(y,y)}$ ka exponent: $\dfrac{x^Ty}{\sigma^2}-\dfrac{\|x\|^2}{2\sigma^2}-\dfrac{\|y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}=-\dfrac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}$ ⇒ $\tilde\kappa=$ Gaussian (random $\sigma=1.3$ par dono $0.58540$, code). Isliye Gaussian features unit sphere par: $K_2(x,x)=1$. $K_2(x,y)=e^{-\|x-y\|^2/2\sigma^2}>0$ ⇒ $\cos\theta>0$ ⇒ koi bhi do Gaussian features ka angle $90^\circ$ se kam. Example: $\sigma=1$, $\|x-y\|=2$: $K_2=e^{-2}=0.1353$, angle $82.2^\circ$ (code) — door ke points almost orthogonal, par kabhi exactly nahi.

3. Laplacian = Gaussians ka positive mixture (P8 option 4 ka proof)

Identity (standard integral): $b\ge0$ ke liye $$e^{-b}=\frac1{\sqrt\pi}\int_0^\infty u^{-1/2}\,e^{-u}\,e^{-b^2/(4u)}\,du.$$ Numerically check: $b=1$ par integral $0.367879$, $e^{-1}=0.367879$ ✓ (code; $b=0.3,\ 2.5$ par bhi). $b=\|x-y\|/\sigma$ rakho: $$e^{-\|x-y\|/\sigma}=\int_0^\infty\underbrace{\frac{u^{-1/2}e^{-u}}{\sqrt\pi}}_{w(u)>0}\ \underbrace{e^{-\|x-y\|^2/(4\sigma^2u)}}_{\text{Gaussian, }\sigma_u^2=2\sigma^2u}\,du.$$ Har $u$ par integrand ek valid (Gaussian) kernel; weight $w(u)>0$. Positive weights ka jod (aur uski limit, integral) valid rehta hai — C1 + "PSD matrices ka set closed" (B14 D3-3). Isliye Laplacian valid. ($\ell_1$ wala: $e^{-\sum_i|x_i-y_i|/\sigma}=\prod_ie^{-|x_i-y_i|/\sigma}$ — 1-D Laplacian kernels ka product, C4.) Exam mein sirf pehchaan chahiye; yeh proof "kyun" ke liye hai.

4. Zero diagonal ⇒ zero matrix (distance kernel kyun nahi chalta)

$K$ PSD aur $K_{ii}=0$ ho to $K_{ij}^2\le K_{ii}K_{jj}=0$ (Cauchy–Schwarz) ⇒ poori row $i$ zero. Ya: $\operatorname{tr}K=\sum\lambda_i=0$ aur $\lambda_i\ge0$ ⇒ sab $\lambda_i=0$ ⇒ $K=0$. $\|x-y\|$ ka Gram diagonal zero hai par off-diagonal $>0$ ⇒ PSD ho hi nahi sakta. (Side note: $-\|x-y\|^2$ "conditionally positive definite" hota hai — woh alag cheez hai aur is bank ke kisi sawaal mein nahi aayi.)

5. Difference $K_1-K_2$: kab valid, kab nahi

$K_1-K_2$ PSD ⇔ $K_1\succeq K_2$ (har $c$ par $c^TK_1c\ge c^TK_2c$). Yeh closure se nahi aata — har pair ka alag sawaal. P10 mein $K_1=x^Ty$, $K_2$ Gaussian: $x=0$ par $0-1=-1$ ⇒ hamesha invalid ($\sigma$ kuch bhi). Gram on $\{0,2\}$ ($\sigma=1$): $\begin{bmatrix}-1&-0.1353\\-0.1353&3\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-1.0046,\ 3.0046$ ✓ (code). Ek aur chhupa case: constant ghatao — $K_2-0.5$: diagonal $0.5\ge0$ ✓, har $2\times2$ pair Cauchy–Schwarz ✓, par door ke teen points par Gram $\approx\begin{bmatrix}0.5&-0.5&-0.5\\-0.5&0.5&-0.5\\-0.5&-0.5&0.5\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $-0.5,\ 1,\ 1$ (code) ⇒ invalid. (P10 ka transfer sawaal yahi hai.)

6. Positive combination infinite dimensions mein bhi

$\phi=(\sqrt a\,\phi_1;\ \sqrt b\,\phi_2)$ concatenation hai. $\phi_1\in\mathbb R^{D_1}$, $\phi_2\in\mathbb R^{D_2}$ ke dimensions alag, ya infinite ($\ell^2$) ho sakte hain — concatenation phir bhi chalta hai ($D_1+D_2$). Isliye P9 mein "$\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2$" sirf input ka dimension hai, feature ka nahi. $a=0$ ya $b=0$ bhi allowed ("$\ge0$").

7. $\phi$ unique nahi hota ("may correspond" kyun)

$Q$ orthogonal ($Q^TQ=I$) ho to $\hat\phi=Q\phi$ ka dot product $\phi(x)^TQ^TQ\phi(y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$ — same kernel. Example: $Q=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ ($90^\circ$ rotation), $u=(1,2)$, $v=(3,-1)$: $u^Tv=1$, $(Qu)^T(Qv)=1$ ✓. Isliye options mein "kaun sa $\phi$ ho sakta hai" poochte hain — ek se zyaada sahi ho sakte hain (aur coordinate order badalna bhi chalta hai). P7 mein sirf ek option $\|\phi_1\|^2=100$ wala hai, par sahi $\phi_1$ ka rotation bhi sahi hota.
