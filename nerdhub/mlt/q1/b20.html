Show answer

Barabar $F$ ⇒ centres nahi khisake ⇒ agla assign wahi ⇒ sab frozen (L20). Ruka hua state local fixed point hai: har point apne nearest centre par aur har centre apne cluster ka mean (L17, L22) — global optimum ki guarantee nahi.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

Sochiye ek shehar mein $K$ dukaan kholni hain. Pehli kisi bhi ek jagah. Doosri kahan? Agar bilkul random jagah chuno to wahi pehli ke bagal mein bhi khul sakti hai — bekaar, kyunki ek hi ilaake ke customers do dukaanon mein bant gaye aur door ke customers ko koi dukaan nahi. K-means++ kehta hai: jahan abhi ke dukaanon se door ke customers hain, wahan agli dukaan khulne ki sambhavna zyada. "Door" ko number banane ke liye distance ka square (score); phir sabke scores ko jod kar har point ka hissa nikalo — wahi probability hai. Isse starting centres aam taur par alag-alag blobs mein girte hain, aur Lloyd ke bure local minimum mein phansne ki sambhavna ghat jaati hai (guarantee nahi).

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) asli dukaan kisi bhi jagah khul sakti hai; yahan candidate sirf data points hain. (2) "Door ka zyada chance" ka matlab pakka nahi — sabse door wala point chuna na bhi jaye (P4 mein $x_2$ sabse door nahi, phir bhi $25\%$). (3) Door akela ghar (outlier) bhi bahut zyada chance pa jaata hai (Visual 1 ka $(5,8)$) — asli dukaandar use shayad na kholta.

Yeh kya NAHI hai: (a) Lloyd ka step nahi — koi mean ya $F$ nahi nikalta, sirf data points mein se centres chunta hai. (b) Deterministic nahi — sirf tab jab sawaal ka rule likhe ki "highest score chuna jaata hai" (L27). (c) Global optimum ki guarantee nahi. (d) "probability $\propto$ distance" hamesha nahi — standard rule distance² hai, par sawaal apna score likh sakta hai (L23, L24).

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

$$\mu_1\in X,\qquad D_j(x)=\min_{1\le l\lt j}\|x-\mu_l\|,\qquad s_j(x)=D_j(x)^2,\qquad \Pr(\mu_j=x)=\frac{s_j(x)}{\sum_{x'\in X}s_j(x')}\quad(j=2,\dots,K).$$

Symbols aur assumptions: $X$ = $n$ data points; $K$ = clusters ki sankhya; $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_{j-1}$ = ab tak chune hue centres (sab data points); $D_j(x)$ = $x$ se nearest chune hue centre tak doori; $s_j(x)$ = score; sum sab data points $x'$ par. Assumptions: Euclid norm (jab tak sawaal Manhattan ya koi aur score na likhe); $\sum s_j\gt0$ (kam se kam ek point abhi tak centre/duplicate nahi); pehla centre diya hua ya uniformly random.

Words mein: agla centre us point ko mil sakta hai jo ab tak ke centres se door hai; uska hissa = uska $D^2$ kul $D^2$ mein se.

Notice that… (1) chuna hua point: $D=0$ ⇒ $s=0$ ⇒ dobara nahi chuna jaata (L26). (2) $D_j$ mein min hai, isliye naya centre jodne se kisi bhi point ka score sirf ghat sakta hai, badh nahi sakta (L25). (3) $\sum_xs_j(x)=\sum_x\min_l\|x-\mu_l\|^2$ = abhi ke centres ke saath nearest-centre assignment ka $F$ (L28) — yani rule "har point ko uske cost ke hisse ke hisaab se chuno".

Chhota worked example: 1-D points $\{0,1,2,3\}$, $\mu_1=0$. $D=0,1,2,3$; scores $s=0,1,4,9$; $\sum s=14$. $\Pr(\mu_2=0)=0$, $\Pr(1)=\tfrac1{14}$, $\Pr(2)=\tfrac4{14}=\tfrac27$, $\Pr(3)=\tfrac9{14}$. Jod: $0+1+4+9=14$ ⇒ $14/14=1$ ✓. Sabse door point $3$ ko $\tfrac9{14}\approx64\%$ — zyada, par $100\%$ nahi.

Important insight: K-means++ ek probability distribution hai points par, aur woh distribution $D^2$ se banti hai. Jo bhi sawaal poochho — "highest probability", "probability of $x$", "lowest probability", "kaun sa hi nahi chuna ja sakta" — sab usi ek table ($D$ → score → $s/\sum s$) se nikalte hain.

This is why sawaal ko teen cheezein pehle padhni chahiye: kitne centres chune ja chuke (nearest-of-all, L25), kaun sa score (L23), aur kya poochha — ranking (L24) ya number.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Pehla centre $\mu_1$: standard algorithm mein uniformly random data point; PYQs mein diya hota hai.
  2. Har point ka $D$: chune hue sab centres se doori nikalo, sabse chhoti rakho (L25). Pehle step mein sirf $\mu_1$ se.
  3. Score: sawaal ka rule — standard $D^2$; Manhattan likha ho to Manhattan doori; "squared" likha ho to square. Chune hue points ka score $0$ (L26).
  4. Probability: $\Pr(x)=s(x)/\sum s$. Agar sirf "highest/lowest" poochha hai to $\sum s$ mat nikalo — scores ki ranking kaafi hai (L24).
  5. Chunav: sampling (probability ke hisaab se) — ya sawaal bataye ki "highest score chuna jaata hai" (L27).
  6. Dohrao jab tak $K$ centres na ho jaayen; phir Lloyd (assign/update) in centres se shuru.

🧪 Worked examples

WE1 — Simple (1-D, teesra centre, nearest-of-all)

Points $\{0,1,2,6,7,8\}$, $\mu_1=0$. Step 2 scores: $0,1,4,36,49,64$, $\sum=154$. Maan lo $\mu_2=8$ chuna gaya. Step 3 mein har point ka $D$ ab dono centres $\{0,8\}$ mein se nearest tak: $1\to\min(1,49)=1$; $2\to\min(4,36)=4$; $6\to\min(36,4)=4$; $7\to\min(49,1)=1$; $0,8\to0$. $\sum s=10$. Probabilities: $\Pr(1)=\tfrac1{10}$, $\Pr(2)=\tfrac4{10}$, $\Pr(6)=\tfrac4{10}$, $\Pr(7)=\tfrac1{10}$. Dhyan do: $6$ aur $7$ ka score $\mu_1$ ke hisaab se $36$ aur $49$ tha — naya centre jodne ke baad $4$ aur $1$. Galat tareeka (sirf $\mu_1$ se doori) $\Pr(7)=49/(1+4+36+49)\approx54\%$ deta jabki sahi $10\%$ hai. [ADDED]

WE2 — Exam-level (2-D, highest-score variant + nearest-of-two)

Points $A(0,0),B(1,0),C(0,2),D(4,0),E(4,3)$, $\mu_1=A$, squared rule. $D^2$ from $A$: $B=1$, $C=4$, $D=16$, $E=25$; $\sum=46$; $\Pr(E)=\tfrac{25}{46}\approx54.3\%$, $\Pr(D)=\tfrac{16}{46}=\tfrac8{23}$. Agar sawaal kahe "highest score chuna jaata hai" (L27) to $\mu_2=E$ (sampling nahi). Ab teesra centre: $D$ ke liye $\min\big(d^2(D,A),d^2(D,E)\big)=\min(16,\,0+9)=9$; $C$: $\min(4,\,16+1=17)=4$; $B$: $\min(1,\,9+9=18)=1$. $\sum=14$ ⇒ $\Pr(D)=\tfrac9{14}\approx64.3\%$. Sirf $\mu_1$ se (E ko bhula kar) $\tfrac{16}{21}\approx76\%$ aata — galat. [ADDED]

WE3 — Tricky: metric badalne se ranking badal sakti hai

$\mu_1=(0,0)$, candidates $(3,3),(0,5),(2,2)$. Euclid: $D=4.243,\ 5,\ 2.828$ ⇒ squared scores $18,25,8$ ($\sum=51$) ⇒ $(0,5)$ sabse upar, $\Pr=\tfrac{25}{51}\approx49\%$. Manhattan: $D=6,\ 5,\ 4$ ⇒ $(3,3)$ sabse upar, score $=D$ lene par $\Pr=\tfrac6{15}=40\%$. Yani ranking sirf ek hi metric ke andar $D\leftrightarrow D^2$ badalne par nahi badalti; metric (Euclid ↔ Manhattan) badalne par badal sakti hai. Isliye P1 jaise sawaal mein "Manhattan" ka phrase padhna zaroori hai. [ADDED]

WE4 — Tricky / counterexample: K-means++ ke baad bhi Lloyd atak sakta hai

Visual 3 ka rectangle: $\mu_1=(0,0)$; $D^2$ of $(4,0),(0,2),(4,2)=16,4,20$, $\sum=40$. $\mu_2=(0,2)$ (probability $\tfrac4{40}=10\%$) chuna gaya to Lloyd: $(4,0)$ ko $\mu_1$ ($16$ vs $20$), $(4,2)$ ko $\mu_2$ ($16$ vs $20$); clusters bottom/top; means $(2,0),(2,2)$; dobara assign wahi ⇒ frozen, $F=4\cdot4=16$. Left/right split $F=4$ deta hai. $\mu_2=(4,0)$ ya $(4,2)$ (kul $90\%$) par Lloyd seedha $F=4$ par pahunchta hai. Uniform random doosra centre $1/3$ bar bura case deta. [ADDED]

Non-example

"Sabse door point ki probability sabse zyada hai, isliye wahi chuna jayega" — galat. Chunav sampling hai: WE1 mein $\Pr(6)$ aur $\Pr(2)$ barabar ($40\%$ each) hain aur kisi ek ke chune jaane ki koi guarantee nahi; Visual 1 ke step 2 mein sabse zyada probability wale $(5,8)$ ki bhi sirf $27.7\%$ hai. "Highest probability" ≠ "chuna gaya"; sirf C6 jaise sawaal jo kahein "highest score chuna jaata hai", wahan deterministic hai.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — edge cases, proofs, connections

1. Score = cost ka hissa (L28)

Squared rule mein $\sum_xD_j(x)^2=\sum_x\min_{l\lt j}\|x-\mu_l\|^2$ — yeh bilkul wahi expression hai jo B19 ka $F$ hota hai agar har point ko nearest chune hue centre par assign karo. Visual 1 mein $\sum s$: $321$ (sirf $\mu_1$), $75$ ($\mu_1,\mu_2=(9,1)$). K-means++ isliye "jis point par abhi sabse zyada cost ka hissa hai, use centre banne ka zyada chance" hai.

2. Centre jodne se $\sum s$ kabhi nahi badhta

Naya centre jodne par har point ka $\min$ sirf ghat sakta hai (L25), isliye $\sum_xs_{j+1}(x)\le\sum_xs_j(x)$ — wahi monotonicity ka flavour jo B19 ke $F^*(k)$ mein tha. Barabari tab jab koi point naye centre ke paas na aaye.

3. Outlier ka asar

$D^2$ bade distances ko bahut taakat deta hai: Visual 1 mein $(5,8)$ ko $86.7\%$ mil gaya. Highest-score variant (L27) aur bhi outlier-pasand hai — woh hamesha sabse door point chunta hai. Isliye K-means++ outlier-sensitive hai, jaise Lloyd ka mean.

4. Guarantee (syllabus ke bahar) [ADDED]

Arthur–Vassilvitskii (2007) ne dikhaya ki K-means++ ke seeding ke baad expected $F$ best possible $F^*$ ka $O(\log K)$ guna hai ($E[F]\le8(\ln K+2)F^*$), jabki uniform random init par aisi koi guarantee nahi. Yeh exam mein nahi poochha jaata; yahan sirf isliye ki "K-means++ better kyun" ka theory ek line mein dikhe. Yeh guarantee seeding ke baad ke $F$ par hai; Lloyd chalane ke baad $F$ aur kam hi hota hai.

5. Seeding ki cost

Har naye centre ke baad sab $n$ points ka $D$ update karna padta hai: $K-1$ rounds, har round $O(nd)$ — kul $O(nKd)$, jo ek Lloyd iteration ke barabar hai, aur rounds sequential hain (agla centre pichhle par depend karta hai). Uniform random init mein data par ek bhi pass nahi. P3 ke statement 4 ka yahi aadhar hai.

6. Degenerate cases

Agar $K\gt$ distinct points ho to kisi step par $\sum s=0$ ho jaata hai aur probability $0/0$ banti hai; bank mein aisa koi sawaal nahi. Duplicate points ka score bhi $0$ jab unka copy centre ho chuka ho (L26).
