📐 2.6 Dual PCA, Gram matrices, and the d > n case

❓ Pretest — cold attempt (solution neeche 🎯 P1 mein)

2025 July 13 · Original Q9 · ID 6406531302392 · SA · 3 marks · [SRC]

Consider a dataset $X$ of shape $(d,n)$, with $d>n$. The $k$-th principal component can be written as

$$w_k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i x_i,$$

where $x_i$ is the $i$-th data point. The $k$-th largest eigenvalue and corresponding eigenvector of $X^TX$ are $10$ and

$$\begin{bmatrix}\frac2{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac3{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac4{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac6{\sqrt{54}}\end{bmatrix},$$

respectively. What will be the value of $\alpha_2$? Enter the answer correct to two decimal places.

2 minute, bina neeche padhe. Answer + Confidence S / U / G note karo. Sochne wali baat: $w_k$ ki length 1 honi chahiye — eigenvector ko kis number se scale karoge?

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Har PC data points ka mixture hai. PC $w$ hamesha $x_1,\dots,x_n$ ke span mein rehta hai (jis direction mein koi data nahi, wahan variance zero — PC wahan nahi jaata). Isliye $w=\sum_i\alpha_ix_i$: $d$ numbers dhoondhne ki jagah sirf $n$ weights $\alpha_i$ dhoondho.
  • Weights kahan se? Points ke aapas ke dot products ki table — Gram matrix $K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j$ — se. $K$ ka top eigenvector $\beta$ batata hai ki kaunsa point PC mein kitna "vote" karta hai.
  • Scale theek karo. $X\beta$ sahi direction deta hai, par uski length $\sqrt\lambda$ hoti hai. Isliye $w=X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$, yaani $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$.
  • Same variance, alag size. $XX^T$ ($d\times d$) aur $X^TX$ ($n\times n$) ke non-zero eigenvalues bilkul same; $C=\tfrac1nXX^T$ hai, to $\lambda_C=\lambda_K/n$. Bade matrix mein bas extra zeros hote hain.
  • Fayda tab jab $d>n$. $10\,000\times10\,000$ ki jagah $50\times50$ ka eigen-problem. Jab $d<n$, ulta — tab primal (seedha $C$) sasta.

Analogy [ADDED] — class ka group photo vs "kaun kisse kitna milta-julta hai" ki table. 50 students ki 10 000-pixel photos se "sabse common chehra pattern" nikalna hai. Primal: har pixel-pair ka rishta (10 000 × 10 000 table). Dual: sirf har student-pair ki similarity (50 × 50 table). Dual table se pata chalta hai kaunse students pattern mein zyada hain ($\beta$); phir unki photos ko un weights se mila do — pattern ($w$) mil gaya.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) "Similarity" yahan sirf dot product hai (centred data par), koi chehra-pehchaan nahi. (2) Weights negative bhi ho sakte hain — kuch points pattern ko ulta push karte hain. (3) Mix karne ke baad length $\sqrt\lambda$ se divide karni padti hai; asli photos mein aisi "normalisation" ka koi matlab nahi.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "$K$ ke eigenvectors hi PCs hain" — nahi. $\beta\in\mathbb R^n$ (points ki space), PC $w\in\mathbb R^d$ (features ki space). Pul: $w=X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ (B6 P11 / 6406531951447: eigenvectors same nahi).
  • "$K$ ka eigenvalue = PC ka variance" — nahi, $n$ se divide karo: variance $=\lambda_K/n$.
  • "$w=X\beta$" — direction sahi, length galat ($\sqrt\lambda$). Unit vector chahiye.
  • "Dual PCA alag answer deta hai" — nahi. Same PCs, same variances; sirf hisaab ka raasta alag.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

Setting: $X=[x_1\,\cdots\,x_n]\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$, columns centred points ($\sum_ix_i=0$). B12 notation M1–M7 (L B11 ka tha).

M1 (two matrices).

$$C=\tfrac1n XX^T\in\mathbb R^{d\times d},\qquad K=X^TX\in\mathbb R^{n\times n},\quad K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j.$$

$K$ ko Gram matrix (ya B7 ke source mein "kernel matrix") kehte hain: points ke saare pairwise dot products. Dono symmetric aur PSD hain.

M2 (same non-zero eigenvalues) — main formula.

$$K\beta=\lambda\beta,\ \lambda>0\ \Longrightarrow\ (XX^T)(X\beta)=\lambda\,(X\beta),\qquad \lambda_C=\frac{\lambda}{n}.$$
  • Symbols: $\beta\in\mathbb R^n$ = $K$ ka unit eigenvector; $\lambda$ = $K$ ka eigenvalue; $\lambda_C$ = $C$ ka eigenvalue (PC ka variance).
  • Assumption: $\lambda>0$ (warna $X\beta=0$, koi direction nahi milti).
  • Meaning in words: $K$ ka har eigenvector, $X$ se guna karne par, $XX^T$ ka eigenvector ban jaata hai — same eigenvalue ke saath. $C$ mein $\tfrac1n$ hai, isliye variance $\lambda/n$.
  • Proof (ek line):
$$XX^T(X\beta)=X(X^TX\beta)=X(K\beta)=\lambda\,(X\beta).$$

M3 (normalise: coefficients).

$$\|X\beta\|^2=\beta^TX^TX\beta=\beta^TK\beta=\lambda\ \Longrightarrow\ w=\frac{X\beta}{\sqrt\lambda}=\sum_{i=1}^n\alpha_ix_i,\quad \alpha_i=\frac{\beta_i}{\sqrt\lambda}.$$
  • Symbols: $w$ = unit PC; $\alpha_i$ = point $i$ ka coefficient (P1 ka $\alpha$).
  • Meaning: $X\beta$ ki length $\sqrt\lambda$ hai, isliye $\sqrt\lambda$ se divide karke unit vector.

Notice that… $\beta$ unit hona chahiye. Agar diya gaya vector unit nahi hai (P1 jaisa), to $\|X\beta\|^2=\lambda\|\beta\|^2$ ho jaata hai — pehle normalise karo, ya kam se kam check karo.

Small example: $K=\begin{bmatrix}6&-6\\-6&6\end{bmatrix}$ (B9 E2), $\lambda=12$, $\beta=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$ ⇒ $\alpha=\tfrac{\beta}{\sqrt{12}}$ $=\tfrac1{\sqrt{24}}(1,-1)$ $\approx(0.204,-0.204)$; variance $=12/2=6$.

Important insight: PCA ko poore data ki zaroorat nahi — sirf dot products $x_i^Tx_j$ ki. Yahi baat B16 mein kernel PCA ka darwaza kholti hai: dot product ki jagah $k(x_i,x_j)$.

This is why… source mein Gram matrix ko "kernel matrix" $K$ kaha gaya (640653669242), aur $X$ ko "$K$ ke eigenvectors ko $C$ ke eigenvectors mein badalne wala matrix" (640653669244, B7).

M4 (scores bina $w$ banaye).

$$z_i=w^Tx_i=\frac{\beta^TX^Tx_i}{\sqrt\lambda}=\frac{(K\beta)_i}{\sqrt\lambda}=\sqrt\lambda\,\beta_i,\qquad z(x)=\frac1{\sqrt\lambda}\sum_{j=1}^n\beta_j\,(x^Tx_j).$$

Training point ke liye sirf $\sqrt\lambda\beta_i$; naye point $x$ ke liye $x$ ke saare $n$ training points ke saath dot products chahiye (B9 H3, H4).

M5 (counts). Centred data: rank $\le\min(d,n-1)$. Isliye non-zero eigenvalues $\le n-1$ (jab $d>n$); $C$ mein kam se kam $d-n+1$ zeros; $K$ mein kam se kam 1 zero kyunki $K\mathbf 1=X^T(X\mathbf 1)=X^T\cdot0=0$.

M6 (cost). Primal: $C$ banana $\sim nd^2$, eigen $\sim d^3$. Dual: $K$ banana $\sim dn^2$, eigen $\sim n^3$. $d>n$ ⇒ dual sasta.

M7 (jo badalta nahi). Explained-variance ratio $K$ ke eigenvalues se bhi wahi (J4: $n$ cancel); storage $k(d+n)$ wahi (L6); reconstruction $\hat x=\sum_{j\le k}z_jw_j$ wahi.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism (dual PCA)

  1. Data centre karo: $x_i\leftarrow x_i-\mu$. Columns ko $X$ ($d\times n$) mein rakho.
  2. Gram matrix banao: $K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j$ ($n\times n$, symmetric).
  3. $K$ ke eigenpairs nikalo, eigenvalues bade se chhote: $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$; har $\beta_k$ unit length ka.
  4. Har $\lambda_k>0$ ke liye coefficients $\alpha^{(k)}=\beta_k/\sqrt{\lambda_k}$.
  5. PC banao (agar chahiye): $w_k=X\alpha^{(k)}=\sum_i\alpha^{(k)}_ix_i$. Check: $\|w_k\|=1$.
  6. Variance along PC $k$: $\lambda_k/n$. EVR: $\lambda_k/\sum_j\lambda_j$.
  7. Training scores: $z_{ik}=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\,\beta_{k,i}$. Naya point: $z_k(x)=\sum_j\alpha^{(k)}_j\,(x^Tx_j)$.
  8. Kitne PCs, reconstruction, storage — B10/B11 ke rules as-is.

Example 1 (simple) — poora dual pipeline, d = 4, n = 3

Centred points: $x_1=(1,0,1,2)$, $x_2=(-1,1,0,-1)$, $x_3=(0,-1,-1,-1)$. (Har coordinate ka jod: $1-1+0=0$, $0+1-1=0$, $1+0-1=0$, $2-1-1=0$ ✓ centred.)

  1. Gram matrix: $x_1^Tx_1=1+0+1+4=6$; $x_1^Tx_2=-1+0+0-2=-3$; $x_1^Tx_3=0+0-1-2=-3$; $x_2^Tx_2=1+1+0+1=3$; $x_2^Tx_3=0-1+0+1=0$; $x_3^Tx_3=0+1+1+1=3$. $$K=\begin{bmatrix}6&-3&-3\\-3&3&0\\-3&0&3\end{bmatrix}.$$
  2. Eigenpairs: try $(2,-1,-1)$: $$K\begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\-1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}12+3+3\\-6-3\\-6-3\end{bmatrix}=9\begin{bmatrix}2\\-1\\-1\end{bmatrix}\ \Rightarrow\ \lambda_1=9.$$ Try $(0,1,-1)$: $K(0,1,-1)=(0-3+3,\ 3,\ -3)$ $=3\,(0,1,-1)$ ⇒ $\lambda_2=3$. Aur $(1,1,1)$: har row ka jod $0$ ⇒ $\lambda_3=0$.
  3. Unit: $\beta_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(2,-1,-1)$.
  4. PC1: $X\beta_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(2x_1-x_2-x_3)$ $=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(3,0,3,6)$; $\sqrt{\lambda_1}=3$ ⇒ $w_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(1,0,1,2)$. Check: $\|w_1\|^2=\tfrac{1+0+1+4}{6}=1$ ✓.
  5. Coefficients: $\alpha=\beta_1/3$ $=\tfrac1{3\sqrt6}(2,-1,-1)$ $\approx(0.272,-0.136,-0.136)$.
  6. Variance: $9/3=\mathbf 3$; PC2 variance $3/3=1$. EVR(PC1) $=9/12=75\%$.
  7. Scores: $\sqrt9\,\beta_1=\tfrac3{\sqrt6}(2,-1,-1)$ $\approx(2.449,-1.225,-1.225)$. Direct check: $x_1^Tw_1=\tfrac{1+0+1+4}{\sqrt6}=\tfrac6{\sqrt6}=2.449$ ✓.
  8. Naya point $x=(1,1,1,1)$: dot products $x^Tx_j=(4,-1,-3)$ ⇒ $z=\tfrac{2\cdot4-(-1)-(-3)}{3\sqrt6}$ $=\tfrac{12}{3\sqrt6}$ $=1.633$; direct $x^Tw_1=\tfrac{4}{\sqrt6}=1.633$ ✓ (code-verified).

Example 2 (exam-level) — sirf eigenpair diya hai

$n=5$ centred points in $\mathbb R^{1000}$. $X^TX$ ke non-zero eigenvalues $20,12,6,2$; top unit eigenvector $\beta=\tfrac12(1,1,0,-1,-1)$. Pucho: (a) $\alpha_1$, (b) PC1 variance, (c) $x_4$ ka PC1 score, (d) PC1 ka EVR, (e) $C$ ke kitne zero eigenvalues.

  1. (a) $\alpha_1=\tfrac{0.5}{\sqrt{20}}=\tfrac{0.5}{4.472}=\mathbf{0.1118}$.
  2. (b) $20/5=\mathbf 4$.
  3. (c) $\sqrt{20}\times(-0.5)=\mathbf{-2.236}$.
  4. (d) $\tfrac{20}{20+12+6+2}=\tfrac{20}{40}=\mathbf{50\%}$ ($n$ ki zaroorat nahi).
  5. (e) Non-zero count $4=n-1$ (maximum possible); $C$ $1000\times1000$ ⇒ $1000-4=\mathbf{996}$ zeros. (Sab code-verified.)

Example 3 (tricky) — galat $\lambda$ ya galat $\beta$

  • Trap A: Example 1 mein koi $C$ ka eigenvalue $3$ use kare: $w=X\beta_1/\sqrt3$. Length $=\sqrt{9}/\sqrt3=\sqrt3=1.732$ — unit nahi. Sahi: $\sqrt{\lambda_K}=\sqrt{n\lambda_C}=\sqrt{9}=3$.
  • Trap B: non-unit $\tilde\beta=(2,-1,-1)$ seedha lagao: $X\tilde\beta/3$ ki length $=\tfrac{\sqrt{9\cdot6}}{3}=\sqrt6=2.449$. Pehle $\|\tilde\beta\|=\sqrt6$ se divide karo.
  • Sign: $-\beta$ bhi unit eigenvector hai ⇒ $-w$, scores ka sign ulta. Variance aur $|\alpha_i|$ nahi badalte.

Non-example

  • $d<n$ (jaise 3 features, 1000 points): dual formula sahi hai, par $K$ $1000\times1000$ — primal $3\times3$ kahin sasta. "Dual hamesha behtar" galat hai.
  • $K$ ka eigenvector $(1,1,1)$ (eigenvalue 0): $X\mathbf 1=0$ ⇒ koi PC nahi. $\lambda=0$ par M3 lagta hi nahi ($\sqrt0$ se divide).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. Ulta pul bhi: C ke eigenvector se K ka eigenvector

Agar $XX^Tw=\lambda w$, $\lambda>0$, to $X^TX(X^Tw)=X^T(XX^Tw)=\lambda(X^Tw)$. Length: $\|X^Tw\|^2=w^TXX^Tw=\lambda$. Isliye $\beta=X^Tw/\sqrt\lambda$. Dhyaan do: $X^Tw$ ke entries $x_i^Tw$ = training scores! To $\beta=z/\sqrt\lambda$, yaani $z=\sqrt\lambda\beta$ (M4) — wahi baat doosri taraf se.

2. Har PC data ke span mein kyun hai (λ > 0 ke liye)

$Cw=\lambda_Cw$ ⇒ $w=\tfrac1{n\lambda_C}XX^Tw=X\Bigl(\tfrac{X^Tw}{n\lambda_C}\Bigr)$ — yeh $X$ ke columns ka combination hai. Isliye $w=\sum\alpha_ix_i$ hamesha possible (P1 ka premise). $\lambda_C=0$ wale eigenvectors (span ke bahar) ko koi nahi poochhta — un par variance zero.

3. Centred data par K ke eigenvectors ⊥ 1 — aur P1 ka uncentred data

$K\mathbf 1=0$ aur $K$ symmetric ⇒ $\lambda>0$ wale har eigenvector $\beta$ ke liye $\beta^T\mathbf 1=0$ (alag eigenvalues ke eigenvectors orthogonal). Matlab coefficients ka jod zero: $\sum\alpha_i=0$. Example 1: $2-1-1=0$ ✓; Example 2: $1+1+0-1-1=0$ ✓.

P1 ka vector $(2,3,4,6)$ (ya $(2,3,4,5)$) ke saare entries positive hain ⇒ $\beta^T\mathbf 1\neq0$ ⇒ us sawaal ka $X$ centred nahi ho sakta [?]. Stem "centred" bolta bhi nahi. Formula $w=X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ phir bhi $XX^T$ ka unit eigenvector deta hai (uncentred "PCA"), isliye answer par asar nahi.

4. Cost ka hisaab (M6) — kab kaun

Primal: $C$ banana $nd^2$ multiply-adds, eigen $\sim d^3$. Dual: $K$ banana $dn^2$, eigen $\sim n^3$, phir $w_k=X\alpha$ ke liye $dn$ per PC. $d=10\,000$, $n=50$: primal $\approx5\times10^9+10^{12}$, dual $\approx2.5\times10^7+1.25\times10^5$. Jab $d=n$, dono barabar; $d<n$ par primal jeetega. (Order-of-magnitude estimates, exact timings nahi.)

5. Kernel PCA preview (B16, abhi solve nahi)

Dual pipeline mein data sirf $K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j$ ke through aata hai, aur naye point ke liye $x^Tx_j$ ke through. Agar har $x$ ko $\phi(x)$ (bahut badi ya infinite dimension) mein bhejo, to $K_{ij}=\phi(x_i)^T\phi(x_j)=k(x_i,x_j)$ — $\phi$ kabhi banana hi nahi padta. Bas do cheezein naya: $K$ ko feature space mein centre karna (B16 PYQ 6406531302388 ka formula), aur $w$ ko explicitly na likh paana.

Consider a dataset $X$ of shape $(d,n)$, with $d>n$. The $k$-th principal component can be written as $w_k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_i x_i$, where $x_i$ is the $i$-th data point. The $k$-th largest eigenvalue and corresponding eigenvector of $X^TX$ are $10$ and

$$\begin{bmatrix}\frac2{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac3{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac4{\sqrt{54}}\\ \frac6{\sqrt{54}}\end{bmatrix},$$

respectively. What will be the value of $\alpha_2$? Enter the answer correct to two decimal places.

✍️ Try first

$\alpha_i$ aur eigenvector $\beta$ ka rishta kya hai? Aur kya diya gaya vector sach mein unit hai? Confidence: S / U / G?

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: $\alpha_2$, 2 decimals.
  • Given: $n=4$ (vector ke 4 entries), $\lambda=10$, $\beta=\tfrac1{\sqrt{54}}(2,3,4,6)$.
  • Trigger clues: "$d>n$", "$w_k=\sum\alpha_ix_i$", "eigenvector of $X^TX$".
  • Prerequisites: M2, M3 (Procedural + Conceptual) · Core principle: $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$.

2. 🧭 Methods (2 genuinely different)

M1 Formula as given — $\alpha_2=\beta_2/\sqrt\lambda$ seedha. M2 Norm-first — pehle $\|w\|=1$ se scale nikalo ($\|X\beta\|^2=\lambda\|\beta\|^2$), phir $\alpha_2$. Teesra method nahi: ek hi relation hai, bas scale kaise tay ho wohi do raaste hain.

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$

Step 1: $\beta_2=\tfrac3{\sqrt{54}}$.

Step 2: $\alpha_2=\tfrac{3}{\sqrt{54}\,\sqrt{10}}=\tfrac3{\sqrt{540}}$.

Step 3: $\sqrt{540}=23.238$ ⇒ $\alpha_2=\mathbf{0.1291}\approx\mathbf{0.13}$.

M2 · $\|w\|=1$ se

Step 1: $\|\beta\|^2=\tfrac{4+9+16+36}{54}=\tfrac{65}{54}$ — 1 nahi.

📌 Memory card — 2.6 Dual PCA, Gram matrices, d > n

Core model: $K=X^TX$ ($n\times n$) ke eigenpair $(\lambda,\beta)$ se PC $w=X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$; variance $\lambda/n$; training score $\sqrt\lambda\beta_i$.

Backbone chain: centre → $K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j$ → $K\beta=\lambda\beta$ ($\|\beta\|=1$) → $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ → $w=\sum\alpha_ix_i$ → variance $\lambda/n$, EVR $\lambda/\sum\lambda$ → reconstruction/storage as B11.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $XX^T(X\beta)=\lambda(X\beta)$ — $\lambda>0$
  • $w=X\beta/\sqrt\lambda$ — $\beta$ unit; $\|X\beta\|^2=\lambda$
  • $\lambda_C=\lambda_K/n$ — $C=\tfrac1nXX^T$
  • $z_i=\sqrt\lambda\,\beta_i$ — training; $z(x)=\sum_j\alpha_j\,x^Tx_j$ — new point
  • Centred: $K\mathbf1=0$, rank $\le n-1$, $\sum\alpha_i=0$
  • Dual sasta iff $d>n$

Top 3 traps: (1) $\sqrt\lambda$ ki jagah $\lambda$ ya $\sqrt\lambda$ se guna; ya non-unit $\beta$ (P1); (2) $\lambda_K$ ko variance likhna ($n$ se divide bhoolna) ya $K$/$C$ ke eigenvectors ko same maanna; (3) covariance option mein divisor ($n$ vs $n-1$ vs 1) ya figure ke bina eigenvalue check bhoolna (P3).
