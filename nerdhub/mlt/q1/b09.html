📐 2.3 Projection scores aur PC coordinates — part B

❓ Pretest — cold attempt (solution neeche 🎯 P9 mein)

2025 February 23 · Original Q4 · ID 6406531104781 · MCQ · 3 marks · [SRC]

Let $x_1,x_2,\ldots,x_n$ be $d$-dimensional data points $(d>n)$, and let $X$ be the $d\times n$ matrix containing the data points. The $k$-th largest eigenvalue and corresponding unit eigenvector of $X^TX$ are $\lambda$ and $\alpha_k$, respectively. What will be the projection of $x_i$ onto the $k$-th principal component?

  1. $x_i^T\alpha_k$
  2. $\dfrac{x_i^T\alpha_k}{\sqrt{n\lambda}}$
  3. $\dfrac{x_i^TX\alpha_k}{\sqrt{\lambda}}$
  4. $x_i^TX\alpha_k$

2 minute, bina neeche padhe. Answer + Confidence S / U / G note karo.

🧭 Position — yeh kahan baitha hai

Peeche (prerequisites)Yeh subtopic (part B)Aage (kya unlock hota hai)
B8: $z_k=w_k^T\tilde x$ (G1), orthonormal PCs, Pythagoras (G4) · B7: dual facts $w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$, $\lambda_C=\lambda_K/n$ · B3: scalar projection $x^Tw/\|w\|$ (1) Point PC-axis par ho to baaki coordinates 0 · (2) Dual form mein coordinate: $x^TX\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$ · (3) Training point ka shortcut $z_i=\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha_i$ · (4) Numerical projection mein rounding · (5) "pehle $K$ retain" ka matlab (c ko drop karna) B10 explained variance $\sum\lambda_k/\sum\lambda$ · B11 reconstruction $\hat x=\sum z_kw_k$ aur storage · B12 dual PCA, Gram matrices ka poora treatment · B16 kernel PCA (same formula, $x^Tx_j\to k(x,x_j)$)

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Point PC1 ki line par ho to uska poora "size" PC1 mein hi hai. Naye axes par reading: PC1 par poori length ($\|\tilde x\|$), baaki axes par 0 — kyunki point ko in axes par "saaya" daalne ke liye woh line se hat'ta hi nahi.
  • Dual form = "chhoti table se kaam karo". Agar features $d$ bahut zyada hain (say 10000) aur points $n$ sirf 50, to $d\times d$ covariance table bhaari hai, par $n\times n$ Gram table $K=X^TX$ chhoti. Chhoti table ka eigenpair $(\lambda,\alpha)$ nikalo, phir $w=X\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$ se wapas $\mathbb R^d$ ka PC banao.
  • $\alpha$ ek "recipe" hai. $w=\tfrac1{\sqrt\lambda}\sum_i\alpha_ix_i$: PC data points ka weighted mix hai; $\alpha_i$ = point $i$ ka hissa recipe mein.
  • Training point ka score = $\sqrt\lambda\times$ uska recipe-hissa. Isliye $z_i=\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha_i$ — $w$ banaye bina bhi score mil jaata hai (sirf training points ke liye).
  • "Pehle 2 retain" = $(a,b)$ do, $c$ rakho mat. $c$ nikalna sirf check ke liye hai: $c^2$ hi information loss hai (B8 G4).

Analogy [ADDED] — do tarah ke attendance register. Ek register mein har row ek student hai aur 10000 columns din (bada), doosre mein 50 students ke beech "kisne kitne din saath attendance di" ka chhota 50×50 table. Dono mein "pattern" ki jaankari same hai; chhote table par kaam karo, phir ek translation step se bade register ki bhasha mein wapas aao ($w=X\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$).

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Translation mein normalisation ($\sqrt\lambda$) lagta hai — register mein aisa kuch nahi. (2) Centred data mein $K$ ka ek eigenvalue hamesha 0 hota hai; uska koi PC nahi banta (Deeper insight 3). (3) Naye (test) point ke liye chhoti table kaafi nahi — usse $x^TX\alpha$ chahiye (Example 5).

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "$\alpha_k$ hi PC hai" — nahi. $\alpha_k\in\mathbb R^n$ (points ka space), PC $w_k\in\mathbb R^d$ (features ka space); $d>n$ mein shape hi alag hai.
  • "$\sqrt{n\lambda}$ lagta hai kyunki $C$ mein $\tfrac1n$ hai" — nahi. $\lambda$ yahan $X^TX$ ka eigenvalue hai; $\sqrt{\lambda}$ hi $X\alpha$ ki length hai. $\tfrac1n$ sirf variance ($\lambda/n$) mein aata hai.
  • "$z_i=\sqrt\lambda\alpha_i$ kisi bhi point ke liye" — nahi, sirf training points $x_1,\dots,x_n$ ke liye.
  • "On-axis point ka koi coordinate nahi hota" — nahi: PC1 coordinate $\pm\|\tilde x\|$, baaki 0.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\text{(H1)}\ \ z_k=w_k^T\tilde x\ \ (\text{B8 G1});\qquad \tilde x=s\,w_1\ \Longrightarrow\ (z_1,z_2,\dots)=(s,0,\dots)$$ $$\text{(H2)}\ \ X^TX\,\alpha_k=\lambda_k\alpha_k,\quad \|\alpha_k\|=1,\qquad w_k=\frac{X\alpha_k}{\sqrt{\lambda_k}}$$ $$\text{(H3)}\ \ z_k(x)=x^Tw_k=\frac{x^TX\alpha_k}{\sqrt{\lambda_k}}\qquad(\text{koi bhi }x\in\mathbb R^d)$$ $$\text{(H4)}\ \ z_k(x_i)=\sqrt{\lambda_k}\ \alpha_{k,i}\qquad(\text{sirf training point }x_i)$$ $$\text{(H5)}\ \ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_k(x_i)^2=\frac{\lambda_k}{n}=\text{eigenvalue of }C=\tfrac1nXX^T$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $X\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ — data matrix, columns $x_1,\dots,x_n$; $d>n$; dataset centred ($\sum_ix_i=0$). $K=X^TX\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}$ (Gram matrix).
  • $(\lambda_k,\alpha_k)$ — $K$ ka $k$-th largest eigenvalue aur uska unit eigenvector, $\alpha_k\in\mathbb R^n$; $\alpha_{k,i}$ uska $i$-th entry. $\lambda_k$ yahan $X^TX$ ka eigenvalue hai, $C$ ka nahi.
  • $w_k\in\mathbb R^d$ — $k$-th PC (unit, $C$ ka eigenvector). $z_k(x)$ — $x$ ka $k$-th coordinate (scalar). $s$ — koi scalar; H1 mein $s=\pm\|\tilde x\|$.
  • H1 mein $w_1,w_2,\dots$ orthonormal hain (B7); H3 ke liye $\|\alpha_k\|=1$ zaroori hai (H2 ka norm check Deeper 1 mein).

Words mein: H1 — point $w_1$ ka multiple ho to uske baaki coordinates 0. H2 — chhoti table $K$ ka eigenpair bade space ka PC deta hai ($X\alpha$ ko $\sqrt\lambda$ se bhaag kar unit banaya). H3 — kisi bhi point ka coordinate = us point ka $w_k$ se dot, $w_k$ ko expand karke. H4 — training point $i$ ke liye yeh dot $\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha_i$ ban jaata hai. H5 — scores ka variance $\lambda/n$; yahan $\tfrac1n$ ek hi jagah aata hai.

Notice that… (i) H3 mein $X$ beech mein hai: $x^T(X\alpha)$, yani $x$ ka dot $X$ ke columns se (sab $n$ points se) — $x^T\alpha$ shape-wise banta hi nahi. (ii) H3 mein $\sqrt\lambda$ hai, $\sqrt{n\lambda}$ nahi. (iii) H4 ko H3 se nikaalte hain, ulta nahi.

Commonly confused — P9 ke 4 options, E2 ke numbers par ($X=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\2&-2\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}$, $\lambda=12$, $\alpha=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$, point $x_1=(1,2,-1)$, $n=2$, $d=3$)

Small worked example: upar wala $X$. $K=X^TX=\begin{bmatrix}6&-6\\-6&6\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $12,0$. $\lambda=12$, $\alpha=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$. $X\alpha=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(x_1-x_2)$ $=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(2,4,-2)$ $=\sqrt2\,(1,2,-1)$. $\|X\alpha\|=\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt6=\sqrt{12}=\sqrt\lambda$ ✓. $w=\tfrac{X\alpha}{\sqrt{12}}=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}(1,2,-1)$. H3: $z(x_1)=\tfrac{6\sqrt2}{\sqrt{12}}=\sqrt6$. H4: $\sqrt{12}\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt2}=\sqrt6$ ✓ — dono ek hi number.

Important insight: dual form mein $w$ ko $\mathbb R^d$ mein kabhi explicitly nahi banana padta: coordinate sirf inner products $x^Tx_j$ (yani $K$ ya $X^Tx$) se nikalta hai.

This is why exam mein dual-PCA ke options sirf normalisation mein alag dikhte hain ($\sqrt\lambda$ vs $\sqrt{n\lambda}$ vs koi nahi), aur kernel PCA (B16) isi formula mein $x^Tx_j$ ko $k(x,x_j)$ se badalkar bana hai.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Setting pehchaano. "PCs $w_1,w_2,\dots$ diye hain + point" ⇒ primal (B8 G1). "$d>n$, $X^TX$ ka eigenvalue/eigenvector diya" ⇒ dual (H2–H4).
  2. Primal: har $w_k$ ke liye $\|w_k\|=1$ aur $w_1^Tw_2=0$ verify karo; phir $z_k=w_k^T\tilde x$ order mein.
  3. On-axis check (5 second): kya point kisi $w_k$ ka multiple hai? Haan ⇒ coordinate $\pm\|\tilde x\|$ aur baaki 0 (H1); dot products sirf confirmation.
  4. Dual: shapes likho — $x_i:d\times1$, $X:d\times n$, $\alpha:n\times1$. Jo expression define hi nahi hota use hata do.
  5. Normalisation check: $\|X\alpha\|^2=\alpha^TK\alpha=\lambda$ (unit $\alpha$ par) ⇒ $w$ banne ke liye $\sqrt\lambda$ se bhaago.
  6. Point ki kism dekho: training point $x_i$ ⇒ $z=\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha_i$ bhi chalega; naya point ⇒ $x^TX\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$.
  7. "Pehle $K$ retain": sirf pehle $K$ coordinates likho; $c$ (dropped) sirf $c^2=\|\tilde x\|^2-\sum_{k\le K}z_k^2$ ke liye.
  8. Decimal round end mein (exact radical pehle): $7/\sqrt2\to4.95$. Intermediate rounding mat karo ($0.7\times7=4.9$ range se bahar).

Example 1 — simple: on-axis point [ADDED]

P8 wale PCs, point $(3,3)$: $(3,3)=3(1,1)=3\sqrt2\,w_1$ ⇒ $(a,b)=(3\sqrt2,\,0)=(4.243,\,0)$. Dot se confirm: $a=\tfrac{3+3}{\sqrt2}=3\sqrt2$, $b=\tfrac{-3+3}{\sqrt2}=0$. Doosra point $(1,-1)=-\sqrt2\,w_2$ ⇒ $(0,-\sqrt2)$: yeh PC2 axis par hai (code-verified).

Example 2 — exam-level: dual numeric [ADDED]

Upar Formal version ka $X$ ($d=3$, $n=2$). $z(x_1)=\sqrt6=2.449$, $z(x_2)=-\sqrt6$ ($x_2=-x_1$). Variance $=\tfrac{6+6}2=6=\lambda/n$ ✓ (H5). $K$ ka doosra eigenvalue $0$ hai — centred data ka $K\mathbf 1=0$ (Deeper 3).

Example 3 — tricky: shapes se options kaato [ADDED]

$d=100$, $n=5$. Options: (a) $x_i^T\alpha_k$ — $x_i:100\times1$, $\alpha_k:5\times1$ ⇒ dot define nahi. (b) $x_i^TX\alpha_k$ — $(1\times100)(100\times5)(5\times1)=1\times1$ ✓. (c) $\alpha_k^TX^Tx_i/\sqrt{\lambda}$ — $(1\times5)(5\times100)(100\times1)$ ✓ aur yeh (b)/$\sqrt\lambda$ ka transpose hai (scalar ka transpose scalar) ⇒ H3 ke barabar. (d) $X\alpha_k/\sqrt\lambda$ — $100\times1$ vector hai, scalar coordinate nahi (yeh $w_k$ hai). Shape check ne 10 second mein (a), (d) hata diye.

Example 4 — non-example: $\alpha$ unit nahi [ADDED]

Example 2 ka $K$, par software ne eigenvector $\alpha=(1,-1)$ diya (norm $\sqrt2$). $X\alpha=(2,4,-2)$, $\|X\alpha\|=\sqrt{24}$. $\sqrt\lambda=\sqrt{12}$ se bhaago to $\|w\|=\sqrt{24}/\sqrt{12}=\sqrt2\ne1$. H3 se $z(x_1)=\tfrac{12}{\sqrt{12}}=3.464$, jabki sahi $2.449$ — $\sqrt2$ guna bada. Moral: H3 tab hi valid hai jab $\|\alpha\|=1$; warna $\sqrt\lambda$ ke saath $\|\alpha\|$ se bhi bhaago (PYQ mein "unit eigenvector" likha hai, isliye wahan nahi lagta).

Example 5 — tricky: naya (test) point [ADDED]

Example 2 ka model, naya point $x_{\text{new}}=(3,0,0)$ — training set mein nahi. H4 lagane ke liye koi index $i$ nahi. H3: $x_{\text{new}}^TX\alpha=(3,0,0)\cdot\sqrt2(1,2,-1)=3\sqrt2$; $z=\tfrac{3\sqrt2}{\sqrt{12}}=\tfrac3{\sqrt6}=1.2247$. Direct $x_{\text{new}}^Tw=\tfrac3{\sqrt6}$ ✓ (code-verified). Isliye test point ke liye hamesha H3.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. $w=X\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$ unit hai aur $C$ ka eigenvector hai — proof

Norm: $\|X\alpha\|^2=\alpha^TX^TX\alpha$ $=\alpha^T(\lambda\alpha)$ $=\lambda\|\alpha\|^2=\lambda$ ⇒ $\|w\|=1$.

Eigenvector: $Cw=\tfrac1nXX^T\cdot\tfrac{X\alpha}{\sqrt\lambda}$ $=\tfrac1n\cdot\tfrac{X(X^TX\alpha)}{\sqrt\lambda}$ $=\tfrac1n\cdot\tfrac{\lambda X\alpha}{\sqrt\lambda}$ $=\tfrac\lambda n\,w$. Matlab $w$ $C$ ka eigenvector, eigenvalue $\lambda/n$ (B7 ka fact). Code-check: random centred $X$ ($d=6$, $n=4$) par $\|w\|=1$ aur $Cw=\tfrac\lambda nw$ dono sach nikle.

2. H4 aur H5 ka proof

$z_i=x_i^Tw=(X^Tw)_i$ ($X^T$ ki $i$-th row $=x_i^T$). $X^Tw=\tfrac{X^TX\alpha}{\sqrt\lambda}=\tfrac{K\alpha}{\sqrt\lambda}=\tfrac{\lambda\alpha}{\sqrt\lambda}=\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha$. Isliye $z_i=\sqrt\lambda\,\alpha_i$ (H4). H5: $\sum_iz_i^2=\lambda\sum_i\alpha_i^2=\lambda$ ⇒ variance $\tfrac\lambda n$.

Example 2 mein: $\sum z_i^2=6+6=12=\lambda$ ✓. Random $d=6$, $n=4$ example mein bhi $\sum z_i^2=\lambda$ aur H3 = H4 har point par ek jaisa nikla.

3. Centred data ⇒ $K$ ka kam se kam ek eigenvalue 0

$K\mathbf 1=X^TX\mathbf 1=X^T\bigl(\sum_ix_i\bigr)=X^T0=0$ ⇒ $\mathbf 1$ (all-ones) $K$ ka eigenvector hai eigenvalue $0$ ke saath. Isliye centred $n$ points ke liye $K$ (aur $C$) ke $n-1$ se zyada nonzero eigenvalues nahi hote (rank $\le n-1$). Example 2 mein $n=2$ ⇒ sirf 1 nonzero eigenvalue ($12$) ✓. Zero eigenvalue ke liye $w=X\alpha/\sqrt0$ define nahi — koi PC nahi banta.

4. Primal vs dual: sign aur cost

$\alpha\to-\alpha$ se $w\to-w$ aur sab $z\to-z$ (B8 ka sign rule). Dono routes ek hi PC dete hain (sign tak): random example mein $|w^Tv_{\text{primal}}|=1$. Cost: primal mein $d\times d$ eigendecomposition, dual mein $n\times n$; $d>n$ ho to dual sasta.

5. Preview (solve nahi kiya): kernel PCA mein yahi formula [ADDED]

H3 mein $x$ sirf $x^Tx_j$ ki shakl mein aata hai: $z(x)=\tfrac1{\sqrt\lambda}\sum_j\alpha_j\,x^Tx_j$. $x^Tx_j$ ko kernel $k(x,x_j)$ se badal do to kernel PCA ka coordinate mil jaata hai. Woh PYQs B16 mein hain.

🎯 P8 — Point $(2,2)$ ke coordinates PC system mein (2024 July 07) [SRC]

2024 July 07 · Original Q12 · ID 640653829514 · MCQ · 2 marks · Parent Q9 (640653829511, B8 mein P7) · [SRC]

Context (parent): Let $C$ be the covariance matrix of a mean-centered dataset: $C=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$. Standard PCA is performed. The first two PCs are $w_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\end{bmatrix}$, $w_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\1\end{bmatrix}$.

Find the coordinates of the point $\begin{bmatrix}2\\2\end{bmatrix}$ in the new coordinate system formed by the principal components.

  1. $\begin{bmatrix}2\sqrt2\\0\end{bmatrix}$  (source marks this option correct)
  2. $\begin{bmatrix}1\\0\end{bmatrix}$
  3. $\begin{bmatrix}0\\1\end{bmatrix}$
  4. $\begin{bmatrix}2\\0\end{bmatrix}$

✍️ Try first

Pehle dekho: $(2,2)$ kis line par hai? Phir $(a,b)$ andaza lagao aur $a^2+b^2=\|x\|^2=8$ se check karo. Confidence: S / U / G?

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: $(z_1,z_2)$ of $x=(2,2)$.
  • Given: $w_1,w_2$; $C$ bhi diya hai par coordinates ke liye zaroori nahi. Dataset centred ⇒ point ko jaise hai waise lo ($\tilde x=x$).
  • Trigger clues: "coordinates … in the new coordinate system formed by the principal components".
  • Prerequisites: B8 G1 (Procedural), B7 orthonormal PCs (Conceptual) · Core principle: H1 / G1.

2. 🧭 Methods (3 genuinely different)

M1 General — dot products. M2 Shortcut — geometry: $(2,2)$ seedha $w_1$ ka multiple hai. M3 Advanced — $x=aw_1+bw_2$ ko $2\times2$ system ki tarah solve karo.

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · $z_k=w_k^Tx$

Step 1 (verify): $\|w_1\|^2=\tfrac{1+1}2=1$, $\|w_2\|^2=\tfrac{1+1}2=1$, $w_1^Tw_2=\tfrac{-1+1}2=0$ ✓.

Step 2 ($a$): $a=\tfrac{2\cdot1+2\cdot1}{\sqrt2}=\tfrac4{\sqrt2}=2\sqrt2$.

Step 3 ($b$): $b=\tfrac{2\cdot(-1)+2\cdot1}{\sqrt2}=0$.

Step 4: $(a,b)=(2\sqrt2,\,0)=(2.828,\,0)$ ⇒ option 1.

M2 · point = multiple of $w_1$

Step 1: $(2,2)=2\,(1,1)$.

Step 2: $w_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ ⇒ $(1,1)=\sqrt2\,w_1$.

Step 3: $(2,2)=2\sqrt2\,w_1+0\cdot w_2$ ⇒ $a=2\sqrt2$, $b=0$.

Step 4: option 1. (Dot products ki zaroorat nahi padi; $a=\|x\|=\sqrt8=2\sqrt2$ bhi yahi deta hai.)

M3 · $x=aw_1+bw_2$

Step 1: $aw_1+bw_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(a-b,\ a+b)$.

Step 2: $\tfrac{a-b}{\sqrt2}=2$ aur $\tfrac{a+b}{\sqrt2}=2$ ⇒ $a-b=2\sqrt2$, $a+b=2\sqrt2$.

Step 3: jodo: $2a=4\sqrt2$ ⇒ $a=2\sqrt2$. Ghatao: $2b=0$ ⇒ $b=0$.

Step 4: $(2\sqrt2,0)$ ⇒ option 1.

Core model: coordinate = dot with the unit PC. PCs diye hon to seedha dot (point PC-axis par ho to baaki 0). $d>n$ aur $X^TX$ ka eigenpair diya ho to $w=X\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$ se wahi dot, expand karke: $x^TX\alpha/\sqrt\lambda$.
