🧠 Intuition — plain language

Ek convex function ka graph katori (bowl, "smile") jaisa hota hai: graph par kisi bhi do points ko seedhi rassi (chord) se jodo to rassi hamesha graph ke upar ya uspar rehti hai, kabhi neeche nahi. Jensen is rassi wali baat ka bada rup hai: do hi nahi, kitne bhi points aur kisi bhi "weights" ke saath. Weights ko aise lo jaise koi probability — sab $\ge0$, jod $=1$.

Seedha matlab: pehle average, phir function $\le$ pehle function, phir average (convex ke liye). Ulta: concave ke liye "pehle average, phir function" $\ge$ "pehle function, phir average". $\ln$ concave hai — isliye $\ln(\text{average})\ge\text{average}(\ln)$.

Analogy (labelled): jhula-bridge [ADDED] — do khambon ke beech laga rassa seedha (chord) hai; khambon ke beech ki zameen agar katori ki tarah neeche dhansi hai (convex), to rassa zameen ke upar rehta hai. Rassa kitna upar hai woh "Jensen gap" hai. Khambe jitne dur, aur zameen jitni zyada dhansi hui, gap utna bada.

Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: zameen/rassa ek picture hai, theorem sirf $f$ ke convex hone par chalta hai; aur weights negative ho jayein to rassa "khambon ke bahar" extend ho jata hai, jahan picture hi nahi rehti (neeche P1 ka poora point).

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • Jensen har function ke liye nahi: sirf convex (ya concave, ulta inequality) ke liye. $f(x)=-x^2$ ya $x^3$ par seedha galat ho sakta hai.
  • Weights sirf "sum $=1$" kaafi nahi; $\lambda_i\ge0$ bhi chahiye (P1 mein $(-1,1,1)$ aur $(1,-1,1)$ ka sum $1$ hai, phir bhi fail).
  • "Convex" $\ne$ "badhta hua": $x^2$ convex hai par kuch jagah girta hai.
  • Weights barabar hone zaroori nahi: $(0.5,0.25,0.25)$ bhi chalta hai.

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

(a) Convex ki definition

$$f(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)\ \le\ \lambda f(x)+(1-\lambda)f(y)\qquad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathbb R,\ \lambda\in[0,1]$$

Symbols: $f:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ ek function; $x,y$ koi do points; $\lambda\in[0,1]$ $x$ par weight, $1-\lambda$ $y$ par. Assumption: yeh har $x,y,\lambda$ ke liye sach ho, kuch hi ke liye nahi.

Words mein: left side = pehle $x,y$ ko mila kar ek point banao, phir $f$ lagao; right side = pehle $f(x),f(y)$ nikalo, phir milao. Right side wahi hai jo chord $(x,f(x))$–$(y,f(y))$ ki us point par height hoti hai.

Notice that… $\lambda=0$ ya $1$ par dono side barabar (equality) — inequality sirf andar ke points par kuch kehti hai.

Small example: $f(x)=x^2$, $x=-1$, $y=3$, $\lambda=\tfrac12$: mix $=1$, LHS $f(1)=1$; RHS $\tfrac12(1)+\tfrac12(9)=5$; $1\le5$ ✓ (yahi Visual 1 ka left panel).

(b) Jensen — kitne bhi points, kisi bhi weights ke saath

$$f\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_ia_i\Big)\ \le\ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_if(a_i)\qquad\text{jab }f\text{ convex},\quad \lambda_i\ge0,\ \ \sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_i=1$$

Symbols: $a_i$ koi bhi real points; $\lambda_i$ weights ($m=3$ PYQ mein). Assumptions (do, dono zaroori): (i) har $\lambda_i\ge0$, (ii) $\sum\lambda_i=1$.

Words mein: weighted average ka $f$ $\le$ $f$ ka weighted average. Concave ke liye $\ge$ (L59).

Notice that… weights probability ki tarah hain; isliye isi ko $f(\mathbb E[X])\le\mathbb E[f(X)]$ bhi likhte hain.

Important insight: $f(x)=x^2$ ke liye gap exactly variance hota hai: $\sum\lambda_ia_i^2-\big(\sum\lambda_ia_i\big)^2=\operatorname{Var}\ge0$. Variance tabhi hamesha $\ge0$ hai jab $\lambda$ probability jaise ho; negative weights se "variance" negative ho sakta hai — yahi wajah hai ki P1 mein negative weights fail hote hain.

This is why GMM mein $\pi_k$ aur $\gamma_k$ (jo hamesha $\ge0$ aur sum $=1$ hain) Jensen ke weights ban sakte hain.

(c) Convex ya concave kaise pehchaanein

Jab $f$ do baar differentiable ho: $f''(x)\ge0$ har jagah $\Rightarrow$ convex. $x^2$: $f''=2>0$ ✓ convex. $\ln a$: $f'=1/a$, $f''=-1/a^2<0$ ⇒ concave. $e^x$: $f''=e^x>0$ ⇒ convex.

⚙️ How it works — "kaun se weights chalte hain?" ka mechanism

  1. Sum nikalo: $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3$. Agar $\ne1$ ⇒ fail (constant function se pakda jaata hai: $f\equiv c$ ke liye inequality $c\le(\sum\lambda)c$ banti hai, $c=\pm1$ lene par $\sum\lambda=1$ aana hi padta hai).
  2. Signs dekho: koi bhi $\lambda_i<0$ ⇒ fail ($f=x^2$, $a=e_i$ lene par $\lambda_i^2\le\lambda_i$ ban-ta hai, jo $0\le\lambda_i\le1$ maangta hai).
  3. Dono pass: Jensen guaranteed (proof: "Deeper insight" mein, do-point definition se induction).
  4. Sawaal "har convex $f$ ke liye" ho to yahi dono conditions; agar koi khaas $f$ diya ho to alag se check karna padega.

🧪 Worked examples (simple → exam-level → tricky) [ADDED]

Example 1 (simple): gap = variance

$f(x)=x^2$, $\lambda=(0.5,0.25,0.25)$, $a=(0,4,8)$. Weighted average $a$: $0.5\cdot0+0.25\cdot4+0.25\cdot8=0+1+2=3$ ⇒ LHS $f(3)=9$. RHS: $0.5\cdot0+0.25\cdot16+0.25\cdot64=0+4+16=20$. $9\le20$ ✓, gap $=11$; aur $\mathbb E[a^2]-(\mathbb E a)^2=20-9=11$ ✓ (variance).

Example 2 (exam-level): teen weights, teen verdict

(i) $(0.5,0.3,0.2)$: sum $1.0$, sab $\ge0$ ⇒ ✓. (ii) $(0.7,0.7,-0.4)$: sum $=1.0$ par $-0.4<0$ ⇒ ✗; counterexample $f=x^2$, $a=(0,0,1)$: LHS $f(-0.4)=0.16$, RHS $=-0.4\cdot1=-0.4$; $0.16\le-0.4$ false. (iii) $(0.4,0.4,0.4)$: sum $=1.2$ ⇒ ✗; $f\equiv1$: LHS $1$, RHS $1.2$ — arre yahan $1\le1.2$ sach hai! $f\equiv-1$ lo: LHS $-1$, RHS $-1.2$; $-1\le-1.2$ false ✓. (Isliye constant function dono $\pm c$ se test hota hai.)

Example 3 (tricky): $\ln$ par inequality ulti

$\ln$ concave hai: $a=(1,100)$, $\lambda=(0.5,0.5)$: $\ln(50.5)=3.922\ \ge\ 0.5\ln1+0.5\ln100=2.303$. Mixture version: $f_1(x)=0.2$, $f_2(x)=0.6$, $\pi=(0.3,0.7)$: $\ln(0.3\cdot0.2+0.7\cdot0.6)=\ln0.48=-0.734\ \ge\ 0.3\ln0.2+0.7\ln0.6=-0.840$. Yaani "$\ln$ of mixture" $\ge$ "mixture of $\ln$s" — GMM mein yahi direction kaam aati hai.

Non-examples

  • $f(x)=-x^2$ (concave), wahi $\lambda,a$ as Example 1: LHS $=-9$, RHS $=-20$; "$-9\le-20$" galat — inequality ulti ho gayi.
  • $f(x)=x^3$ (na convex, na concave), $\lambda=(\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $a=(-2,0)$: LHS $f(-1)=-1$, RHS $\tfrac12(-8)=-4$; $-1\le-4$ galat.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Jensen ka proof (3 points) — do-point definition se, koi step chhoota nahi

Maano $f$ convex, $\lambda_i\ge0$, $\lambda_1+\lambda_2+\lambda_3=1$. Agar $\lambda_1=1$ to baaki $0$ hain aur dono side $f(a_1)$ — barabar. Warna $1-\lambda_1>0$; define $b=\dfrac{\lambda_2a_2+\lambda_3a_3}{1-\lambda_1}$ (yeh $a_2,a_3$ ka weighted average hai, weights $\tfrac{\lambda_2}{1-\lambda_1},\tfrac{\lambda_3}{1-\lambda_1}\ge0$, sum $=1$).

Step 1: $\lambda_1a_1+\lambda_2a_2+\lambda_3a_3=\lambda_1a_1+(1-\lambda_1)b$. Step 2 (definition, $x=a_1,\ y=b,\ \lambda=\lambda_1$): $f(\cdot)\le\lambda_1f(a_1)+(1-\lambda_1)f(b)$. Step 3 (definition dobara, $b$ par): $f(b)\le\dfrac{\lambda_2}{1-\lambda_1}f(a_2)+\dfrac{\lambda_3}{1-\lambda_1}f(a_3)$. Step 4: Step 3 ko $(1-\lambda_1)$ se multiply karke Step 2 mein daalo: $f(\cdot)\le\lambda_1f(a_1)+\lambda_2f(a_2)+\lambda_3f(a_3)$. $\blacksquare$ (Zyada points par yahi induction.)

2. Weights ki conditions zaroori kyun hain — necessity proof

Agar inequality har convex $f$ aur har $a$ ke liye sach hai: (i) $f\equiv1$: $1\le\sum\lambda_i$; $f\equiv-1$: $-1\le-\sum\lambda_i$ ⇒ $\sum\lambda_i=1$. (ii) $f=x^2$, $a=e_i$ (ek $1$, baaki $0$): LHS $\lambda_i^2$, RHS $\lambda_i\cdot1+0=\lambda_i$ ⇒ $\lambda_i^2\le\lambda_i$ ⇒ $0\le\lambda_i\le1$. To P1 ki dono conditions sufficient bhi aur necessary bhi hain.

3. EM ka bridge: $\ln\sum$ ka lower bound aur woh kab tight hota hai [ADDED]

Kisi bhi weights $q_k>0$, $\sum q_k=1$ ke liye: $\ln\sum_ka_k=\ln\sum_kq_k\dfrac{a_k}{q_k}\ \ge\ \sum_kq_k\ln\dfrac{a_k}{q_k}$ (concave Jensen). Barabari tab, jab $a_k/q_k$ sab $k$ ke liye ek hi number ho, yaani $q_k\propto a_k$, yaani $q_k=\dfrac{a_k}{\sum_ja_j}$.

GMM mein $a_k=\pi_kf_k(x)$ lo — to yeh $q_k$ exactly responsibility $\gamma_k$ hai (L63). Numerical check (7.3 ke P5 ka data): $a=(0.3\cdot0.194,\ 0.7\cdot0.279)=(0.0582,\ 0.1953)$, $\sum a=0.2535$, $\ln0.2535=-1.3724$. $q=\gamma=(0.2296,0.7704)$: bound $=-1.3724$ (barabar ✓). $q=(0.5,0.5)$: bound $=0.5\ln0.1164+0.5\ln0.3906=-1.5454\le-1.3724$ ✓. Poora EM-argument B26 mein; yahan sirf yeh jaan lo ki "E-step mein $q=\gamma$" aakhir mein is tightness se aata hai.

4. Expectation form aur equality case

$f(\mathbb E[X])\le\mathbb E[f(X)]$ (convex $f$). Equality tab: $f$ us range par linear ho, ya $X$ constant ho (saare $a_i$ barabar jahan $\lambda_i>0$).

Tier 1 — General foundations

G1 · Weights aur weighted average (convex combination)

A. Core idea

Weights ka matlab "kitna hissa". Agar sab $\ge0$ hon aur jod $1$ ho to weighted average ek mix hai, jo hamesha values ke beech rehta hai.

B. Notation and operations

$\sum_k\lambda_ka_k$ jab $\lambda_k\ge0$, $\sum\lambda_k=1$. Normalise karna: kachche numbers $u_k$ ko $w_k=\dfrac{u_k}{\sum_ju_j}$ se weights banao.

C. Worked examples

(i) Marks $60,80$, weights $0.3,0.7$: $0.3\cdot60+0.7\cdot80=18+56=74$. (ii) Kachche $u=(3,1,4)$, jod $8$ ⇒ $w=(0.375,0.125,0.5)$.

D. Common mistakes

Normalise karna bhool jana; weights ka jod $1$ na hona; negative weight.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: GMM mein weights kahan? A: $\pi_k$ (component chunne ke chances) aur $\gamma_k$ (data dekhne ke baad ke chances) — dono weights. E-step ka kaam hi "kachche $\pi_kf_k(x)$ ko normalise karna" hai.

G2 · $\sum$, $\prod$ aur $\ln$

A. Core idea

$\ln$ product ko sum bana deta hai: $\ln(abc)=\ln a+\ln b+\ln c$. Par sum ko tod nahi sakta: $\ln(a+b)\ne\ln a+\ln b$.

B. Notation and operations

$\ln\prod_if_i=\sum_i\ln f_i$; iid data ka joint density $=\prod_if(x_i)$ (independence).

C. Worked examples

$\ln(2\cdot3\cdot4)=\ln24=3.178=0.693+1.099+1.386$ ✓. Par $\ln(2+3)=\ln5=1.609$ jabki $\ln2+\ln3=1.792$ — alag.

D. Common mistakes

$\ln(a+b)$ ko $\ln a+\ln b$ likhna; $\prod$ ki jagah $\sum$ (ya ulta) likhna.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: GMM ke log-likelihood mein sum log ke andar kyun rehta hai? A: har point ka density khud ek sum hai ($\sum_k\pi_kf_k$), aur $\ln$ use todta nahi: $\ell=\sum_i\ln(\sum_k\cdots)$.

G3 · Discrete Bayes' rule

A. Core idea

Reverse conditional: $P(A\mid B)$ nikalna jab $P(B\mid A)$ pata ho. Kitna prior thha $\times$ kitna evidence mila, phir normalise.

B. Notation and operations

$P(A_k\mid B)=\dfrac{P(B\mid A_k)P(A_k)}{\sum_jP(B\mid A_j)P(A_j)}$; denominator $=P(B)$ (law of total probability, $A_j$ ek partition).

C. Worked examples

Do urns: $P(\text{urn1})=0.3$, $P(\text{red}\mid\text{urn1})=0.5$, $P(\text{red}\mid\text{urn2})=0.2$. $P(\text{urn1}\mid\text{red})=\dfrac{0.3\cdot0.5}{0.3\cdot0.5+0.7\cdot0.2}=\dfrac{0.15}{0.29}=0.5172$.

D. Common mistakes

$P(B\mid A)$ ko $P(A\mid B)$ maan lena ($0.5\ne0.517$); denominator mein sirf ek term; prior bhool jana.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: E-step kya hai? A: yahi Bayes: hidden $z=k$ "urn" hai, $x$ "red ball"; $\gamma_k=\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x)}{\sum_j\pi_jf_j(x)}$.

Tier 2 — GMM-specific maths

S1 · Gaussian density ko evaluate karna (1-D)

A. Core idea

$\mathcal N(x;\mu,\sigma^2)$ ek bell-curve ki height hai $x$ par — probability nahi, density. $\mu$ par peak, $\sigma^2$ jitna bada utna chauda aur neecha.

B. Notation and operations

L60: $\dfrac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp\!\big(-\tfrac{(x-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^2}\big)$. Peak $=\dfrac1{\sqrt{2\pi\sigma^2}}$ ($\sigma^2=1$ par $0.3989$).

C. Worked examples

$\mathcal N(1;3,1)=0.3989\cdot e^{-2}=0.0540$. $\mathcal N(1.2;1,2)=\dfrac{e^{-0.04/4}}{\sqrt{4\pi}}=0.2793$. $\mathcal N(0;0,4)=\dfrac1{\sqrt{8\pi}}=0.1995$ (variance $4$ ⇒ $\sigma=2$).

D. Common mistakes

$\sigma^2$ ki jagah $\sigma$ exponent mein; normaliser $\tfrac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$ chhodna; density ko probability samajhna (density $1$ se bada ho sakti hai jab $\sigma$ chhota ho).

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: $f_k(x)$ kya hai? A: $k$-th Gaussian ki height $x$ par. Responsibility ratios mein sirf in heights ka anupaat aata hai, isliye density ki unit ka koi dikkat nahi.

S2 · Latent (hidden) variable — pehle $z$, phir $x$

A. Core idea

Data do tarah ke groups se aaya, par group label $z$ kabhi nahi dikhta. Process: pehle $z$ chuno, phir us group ka Gaussian chalao.

B. Notation and operations

$P(z=k)=\pi_k$; $x\mid z=k\sim\mathcal N(\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$; joint $p(x,z=k)=\pi_kf_k(x)$.

C. Worked examples

$\pi=(0.3,0.7)$: 100 samples mein lagbhag $30$ component 1 se, $70$ component 2 se; har sample ka $z$ kisi ko nahi pata.

D. Common mistakes

$\pi_k$ (prior, $x$ dekhne se pehle) ko $\gamma_k$ (posterior, $x$ dekhne ke baad) samajhna; $z$ ko observed maan lena.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: K-means se rishta? A: K-means har point ka label hard chunta hai; GMM label ko probability ($\gamma$) deta hai — "soft K-means with variances and weights".

S3 · Marginal density $p(x)=\sum_k\pi_kf_k(x)$

A. Core idea

$z$ hidden hai to uske saare mumkin values par jodo: $p(x)=\sum_kp(x,z=k)$.

B. Notation and operations

L61. Joint terms $\pi_kf_k(x)$ ka jod $=p(x)$ — yeh G3 ka denominator bhi hai.

C. Worked examples

P2 ke $x=1$ par: $0.3\cdot0.242+0.7\cdot0.054=0.0726+0.0378=0.1104$.

D. Common mistakes

$\pi_k$ chhod ke $\sum_kf_k$; ya $\prod_k$ lena.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: Likelihood mein har point ka factor kya hai? A: yahi $p(x_i)$ — isliye $L=\prod_ip(x_i)$.

S4 · Responsibility $\gamma_k=P(z=k\mid x)$

A. Core idea

$x$ dikh gaya; ab $z$ ke baare mein naya belief: Bayes (G3) ko mixture par lagao.

B. Notation and operations

L63, L64: $\gamma_k=\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x)}{p(x)}$; $\sum_k\gamma_k=1$; do components mein odds $=\dfrac{\pi_1f_1}{\pi_2f_2}$.

C. Worked examples

$\pi=(0.5,0.5)$, $f_1(x)=0.2$, $f_2(x)=0.6$: $p=0.1+0.3=0.4$; $\gamma_1=0.25$, $\gamma_2=0.75$.

D. Common mistakes

$\gamma_1=\dfrac{f_1}{f_1+f_2}$ (prior $\pi$ bhool jana); denominator mein sirf ek term.

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: E-step ka output? A: har point ke liye $K$ numbers jo $1$ par jodte hain; M-step (B26) inhi ko weights ki tarah use karega.

S5 · "$\ln$ sum ko nahi todta" — iska natija

A. Core idea

Mixture ke log-likelihood mein $\ln(\sum_k\cdots)$ bana rehta hai, isliye derivative set-to-zero ka seedha band formula nahi milta (Bernoulli/Poisson jaisa $\bar x$).

B. Notation and operations

L62: $\ell(\theta)=\sum_i\ln\big(\sum_k\pi_kf_k(x_i)\big)$.

C. Worked examples

$\pi=(0.3,0.7)$, $f=(0.2,0.6)$: $\ln(0.06+0.42)=\ln0.48=-0.734$. Galat "tod" $\ln(0.06)+\ln(0.42)=-2.813-0.868=-3.681$ — bahut door.

D. Common mistakes

$\ell=\sum_i\sum_k\ln(\pi_kf_k(x_i))$ likhna (sum ko bahar nikaal dena).

E. Why GMM needs this (ML bridge)

Q: Isi se EM kyun? A: direct MLE mushkil; T6 ka concave Jensen ek lower bound deta hai jise EM baar-baar sudhaarta hai (B26).

⚙️ How it works — data banane ka numbered mechanism

  1. Parameters $\theta=\{\pi_k,\mu_k,\sigma_k^2\}$ diye hain.
  2. Har data point ke liye: $z_i\sim\text{Categorical}(\pi_1,\dots,\pi_K)$ chuno.
  3. Phir $x_i\sim\mathcal N(\mu_{z_i},\sigma_{z_i}^2)$.
  4. Sirf $x_i$ rakho, $z_i$ phenk do — yahi hume dataset ke roop mein milta hai.
  5. Likelihood/E-step ka kaam: ulta chalke $z_i$ ke baare mein belief ($\gamma$) aur $\theta$ ki achchi value dhoondhna.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Mixture ka mean aur variance (derivation)

$\mathbb E[x]=\sum_k\pi_k\mathbb E[x\mid z=k]=\sum_k\pi_k\mu_k$. $\mathbb E[x^2]=\sum_k\pi_k(\sigma_k^2+\mu_k^2)$ (kyunki $\mathbb E[x^2\mid k]=\operatorname{Var}+\text{mean}^2$). Isliye $\operatorname{Var}(x)=\sum_k\pi_k(\sigma_k^2+\mu_k^2)-\big(\sum_k\pi_k\mu_k\big)^2$. P2 ke parameters: $0.3(1+4)+0.7(1+9)=1.5+7=8.5$; $8.5-2.7^2=8.5-7.29=1.21$.

2. Label switching

Components ke naam badalne ($1\leftrightarrow2$, saath mein unke $\pi,\mu,\sigma^2$) se $p(x)$ nahi badalta. Isliye "component 1" ka koi absolute matlab nahi — sirf parameters ka set matter karta hai.

2. 🧭 Methods

2 methods: derive+substitute (General) aur option elimination (Shortcut). (Numeric $\ell(0.6)$ verification mein.)

3. 🧮 Working

M1 · Derive + substitute (General)

Step 1: $f(x_i\mid\theta)=\theta f_1(x_i)+(1-\theta)f_2(x_i)$.

Step 2: $L(\theta)=\prod_{i=1}^nf(x_i\mid\theta)$ (iid).

Step 3: $\ell=\ln L=\sum_{i=1}^n\ln\big[\theta f_1(x_i)+(1-\theta)f_2(x_i)\big]$.

Step 4: $f_1=e^{-x}$, $f_2=3e^{-3x}$ ⇒ $\ell(\theta)=\sum_i\ln\big[\theta e^{-x_i}+(1-\theta)\,3e^{-3x_i}\big]$ ✓.

M2 · Option elimination (Shortcut)

Reject jahan: (i) $\prod_i$ bahar bacha ho ($\ell$ ko sum chahiye); (ii) $\ln$ andar tod diya ho: $\ln(\theta e^{-x_i})+\ln((1-\theta)3e^{-3x_i})$; (iii) $3$ ya $(1-\theta)$ gayab; (iv) $\theta$ ki jagah $\theta$ aur $1-\theta$ ek hi component par.

🧭 Position — yeh kahan baithta hai

  • Pichhla: 7.1 ne model, 7.2 ne uska likelihood diya. EM ka pehla half-step E-step hai: diye hue $\theta^{(t)}$ par har point ke liye $P(z=k\mid x)$ nikalna.
  • Prerequisites: S1 (Gaussian values), S3, S4, G3 (Bayes).
  • Yeh kya unlock karta hai: M-step (B26): $\gamma$ hi weights hain jinse $\pi_k,\mu_k,\sigma_k^2$ update hote hain; $N_k=\sum_i\gamma_k^i$ (L66).

🧠 Intuition — plain language

Ek bolt $x=1.2$ mila. Machine 1 ke bolts aam taur par $0$ ke aas-paas, machine 2 ke $1$ ke aas-paas aur zyada phaile hue. Kaun si machine se aaya? Do cheezein tolni hain: (i) prior: machine 2 se bolts zyada ($\pi_2=0.7$) aate hain; (ii) evidence: $1.2$ ki length machine 2 ke bell-curve par zyada "common" hai ($0.279$) machine 1 se ($0.194$). Dono ko multiply karo aur jod se divide karo — yahi $\gamma$ hai: $\gamma_1=0.2296$, $\gamma_2=0.7704$.

Analogy (labelled): doctor ki jaanch [ADDED] — bimari ka base-rate = prior ($\pi_k$), jaanch ka natija kis bimari mein kitna aam hai = evidence ($f_k(x)$). Dono mila kar doctor ka ant-mein belief = $\gamma_k$. Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: yahan "bimari" ka koi sach nahi hota ($z$ kabhi dikhta nahi), $\gamma$ sirf current parameters $\theta^{(t)}$ ke hisaab se ek belief hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • $\gamma_k\ne\pi_k$: $\pi_k$ data se pehle ka hissa, $\gamma_k$ data dekhne ke baad ka (jab $f_1=f_2$ ho tabhi dono barabar).
  • $\gamma_k\ne f_k(x)$: density ko normalise bhi karna padta hai aur $\pi_k$ se tolna bhi.
  • Hard label nahi: $\gamma_1=0.23$ ka matlab "23% chance", "nahi" nahi.
  • Bada $f_k(x)$ hona kaafi nahi: $f_1>f_2$ par bhi chhota $\pi_1$ jeetne nahi deta (7.1 ka Example 3).
  • E-step parameters badalta nahi; sirf $\gamma$ nikalta hai.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

(a) Responsibility (E-step)

$$\gamma_k^{\,i}=\lambda_k^{\,i}=P(z_i=k\mid x_i;\theta^{(t)})=\frac{\pi_k\,f_k(x_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^{K}\pi_j\,f_j(x_i)}$$

Symbols aur assumptions: $i$ data point, $k$ component; $\theta^{(t)}=\{\pi_k,\mu_k,\sigma_k^2\}$ current (fixed) parameters; $f_k(x_i)=\mathcal N(x_i;\mu_k,\sigma_k^2)$; $\pi_k\ge0$, $\sum\pi_k=1$.

Words mein: numerator = "$z=k$ chuna gaya aur $x_i$ aaya" (joint); denominator = "$x_i$ aaya" ($p(x_i)$, S3). Ratio = Bayes (G3).

Notice that… $\sum_k\gamma_k^i=1$ har point ke liye (numerator ka jod = denominator). Common denominator $p(x_i)$ saare $k$ ke liye ek hi hai.

QuantityMatlabData dekhne se pehle/baad
$\pi_k$$P(z=k)$pehle (prior)
$f_k(x)$$x$ ki height component $k$ mein ($P(x\mid z=k)$)likelihood

(b) Do components: odds aur sigmoid

$$\frac{\gamma_1}{1-\gamma_1}=\frac{\pi_1f_1(x)}{\pi_2f_2(x)},\qquad \gamma_1=\frac{\text{odds}}{1+\text{odds}}=\frac1{1+\frac{\pi_2f_2(x)}{\pi_1f_1(x)}}$$

Barabar variance $\sigma^2$ par: $\ln\text{odds}=\ln\dfrac{\pi_1}{\pi_2}+\dfrac{(x-\mu_2)^2-(x-\mu_1)^2}{2\sigma^2}$ — $x$ mein linear, isliye $\gamma_1=\dfrac1{1+e^{-\ln\text{odds}}}$ sigmoid. (Pura Gaussian normaliser $\tfrac1{\sigma\sqrt{2\pi}}$ dono mein cancel.)

(c) MAP / hard assignment (L65)

$\arg\max_k\gamma_k^i$. Do components: component 1 $\iff\gamma_1>0.5\iff\text{odds}>1$. Q22 ka rule: "comp 2 $\iff\gamma_1\le0.5$".

Small worked example (3 components, $\pi=(0.2,0.3,0.5)$, $f(x)=(0.1,0.4,0.3)$): joints $(0.02,0.12,0.15)$; $p=0.29$; $\gamma=(0.069,\ 0.414,\ 0.517)$ — sum $1$ ✓.

Important insight: $\gamma$ exactly Bayes' rule hai jahan $\pi_k$ prior, $f_k$ likelihood. Agar likelihoods barabar ($f_1=f_2$) to evidence kuch nahi batata aur $\gamma=\pi$ (E-step "kuch nahi karta").

This is why P9 mein sirf $\gamma_1$ aur $\pi_1$ se $f_1/p$ ya odds nikal sakte hain, par $p(x)$ khud nahi (neeche).

⚙️ How it works — E-step ka numbered mechanism

  1. Current $\theta^{(t)}$ pakdo ($\pi_k,\mu_k,\sigma_k^2$).
  2. Ek point $x_i$ ke liye har component ka density $f_k(x_i)$ nikalo (S1, ya table se).
  3. Joint banao: $\pi_kf_k(x_i)$.
  4. Jod $p(x_i)=\sum_k\pi_kf_k(x_i)$.
  5. Divide: $\gamma_k^i=\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x_i)}{p(x_i)}$; check $\sum_k\gamma_k^i=1$.
  6. Do components aur barabar variance ho to shortcut: sigmoid/odds (b).
  7. Hard label chahiye to $\arg\max_k\gamma_k$ (c).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Log-odds linear ya quadratic

Barabar $\sigma^2$: $\ln\text{odds}$ $x$ mein linear ⇒ decision boundary ek point (1-D) — K-class mein linear classifier jaisa. Alag $\sigma^2$: $\ln\text{odds}$ $x$ mein quadratic ⇒ boundary do points tak ho sakti hai (Visual 2 left).

2. Soft se hard tak (K-means ka limit)

$\sigma_k^2=\sigma^2\to0$, $\pi_k$ barabar: sigmoid ki dhal $\sim1/\sigma^2$ khadi ho jaati hai, $\gamma\in\{0,1\}$ — nazdeek mean ka component; yani K-means ka assignment step (T4, B17–B19).

3. Expected counts — M-step ki jhalak [ADDED]

$N_k=\sum_i\gamma_k^i$ component $k$ ke "expected" points hain (L66). Q19 mein $\sum_i\gamma_1^i=2.770$ ($n=4$) ⇒ $N_1/n=0.6925$ — M-step (B26) mein $\theta^{(1)}$ aise milega; yahan sirf yeh dekho ki E-step ka output M-step ka weight banta hai.

4. $p(x)$ aur odds mein kya determinable hai

$\gamma_1$ aur $\pi_1$ se: $\dfrac{f_1}{p}=\dfrac{\gamma_1}{\pi_1}$ aur odds $\dfrac{\gamma_1}{1-\gamma_1}$ nikalte hain (dono ratios), par $p(x)$ nahi — kyunki sab joints ko ek common factor $c$ se scale kar do to $\gamma$ nahi badalta (P9).

🎯 P10 — $f(x\mid1)=3f(x\mid2)$, $\gamma_1=0.60$: $\pi_1$ (2026 July Q10) [SRC]
