🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Centring = origin ko badal ke beech mein le jaana. Covariance "spread" naapta hai, "kahan baitha hai" nahi. Agar mean ghatana bhool gaye, to $\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T$ mein spread ke saath $\mu\mu^T$ (location) bhi ghus jaata hai: $\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T=C+\mu\mu^T$.
  • Sum of all entries of $C$ = ek naya feature banao "saare features ka jod" $s=x_1+\cdots+x_d$; uska variance hi $\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1$ hai. Matrix ke 9 entries jodne ki jagah 8 numbers ka variance — kaafi aasaan.
  • $AA^T$ aur $A^TA$ — ek hi data, do nazariye. $AA^T$ ($d\times d$) features ke beech ka rishta, $A^TA$ ($n\times n$) points ke beech ka rishta (Gram matrix). Non-zero eigenvalues same, par eigenvectors alag duniya mein rehte hain ($\mathbb R^d$ vs $\mathbb R^n$).
  • Linear constraint = chapti (flat) direction. Agar har centred point $a^T\tilde x=0$ satisfy kare, to direction $a$ mein koi spread nahi ⇒ variance along $a$ $=0$ ⇒ $a$ ek eigenvector, eigenvalue 0. Har independent constraint ek zero eigenvalue.
  • Eigenvalue formula diya ho (jaise $100\cdot2^{-i}$) to "direction of maximum variance ka variance" = sabse bada eigenvalue — formula mein smallest allowed $i$ daalo (yahan $i=1$), total variance ke saath confuse mat karo.

Analogy [ADDED] — class ki photo. $AA^T$ = "subjects ka table" (Maths aur Physics marks saath-saath kitne badhte hain); $A^TA$ = "students ka table" (do students ke marks kitne milte-julte hain). Dono ek hi marksheet $A$ se bane, isliye unki "taaqat" (non-zero eigenvalues) same hai — par ek ka answer subjects ki combination hai, doosre ka students ki combination. Dono ko $A$ hi jodta hai: student-combination $v$ ko $A$ se guna karo to subject-combination $u\propto Av$ milta hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: PCA ke covariance ke liye data centred chahiye aur $\tfrac1n$ factor lagta hai — marksheet analogy yeh nahi batati. Aur jab koi eigenvalue repeat ho (tie), to "the eigenvector" unique hi nahi — analogy wahan chup hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • $\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T$ (uncentred second-moment matrix) covariance nahi — jab tak $\mu=0$ na ho. (P8 ka option 1 yahi trap hai.)
  • "Sum of all entries" trace nahi. Trace = sirf diagonal; sum of entries mein off-diagonals bhi judte hain. (Cube mein off-diagonals 0 hain isliye dono 3 — coincidence, rule nahi.)
  • $A^TA$ aur $AA^T$ ke eigenvectors same nahi — $d\neq n$ ho to size hi alag; $d=n$ par bhi aam taur par alag.
  • $\sum_i\lambda_i$ "variance along max-variance direction" nahi — woh total variance hai. (P13 ka key yahi galti lagta hai.)

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\text{(F1)}\ \ C=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)(x_i-\mu)^T=\underbrace{\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^nx_ix_i^T}_{S\ (\text{uncentred})}-\mu\mu^T\qquad\text{(F2)}\ \ \sum_{j,k}C_{jk}=\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1=\operatorname{Var}\!\Big(\sum_{j=1}^dx_j\Big)$$ $$\text{(F3)}\ \ A^TAv=\lambda v,\ \lambda>0\ \Rightarrow\ AA^T(Av)=\lambda(Av)\qquad\text{(F4)}\ \ a^T\tilde x_i=0\ \ \forall i\ \Rightarrow\ Ca=0\cdot a$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$ raw points, $\mu=\tfrac1n\sum x_i$, $\tilde x_i=x_i-\mu$. $S=\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T$ ko "uncentred second-moment matrix" bolenge — yeh covariance nahi hai jab tak $\mu=0$.
  • $\mathbf 1=(1,\dots,1)^T\in\mathbb R^d$. $\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1$ unit vector nahi hai — isliye yeh "variance along $\mathbf 1$" ka $d$ guna hai: $\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1=d\cdot\big(\tfrac{\mathbf 1}{\sqrt d}\big)^TC\big(\tfrac{\mathbf 1}{\sqrt d}\big)$.
  • $A\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$ koi bhi matrix (PCA mein data matrix $X$, points as columns). $AA^T$ $d\times d$, $A^TA$ $n\times n$; dono symmetric PSD.
  • $a\in\mathbb R^d$, $a\neq0$ — constraint vector. "Every point satisfies $a^Tx=0$" aur data centred ⇒ $a^T\tilde x_i=0$.
  • Convention $\tfrac1n$ (P8 ka key isi se aata hai: $\tfrac1{n-1}$ se $\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$ banta, jo kisi option mein nahi).

Meaning in words: (F1) "covariance = uncentred average outer product minus mean ka outer product." (F2) "$C$ ki saari entries ka jod = 'sum-feature' ka variance." (F3) "$A^TA$ ka eigenvector $v$ ho, to $Av$ $AA^T$ ka eigenvector hai, same $\lambda$." (F4) "Jis direction mein data hilta hi nahi, woh zero-eigenvalue eigenvector hai."

⚙️ How it works — mechanism

A. Raw points → $C$ → $\lambda_1$ (P8/P9 type)

  1. Mean nikalo: $\mu=\tfrac1n\sum x_i$. Agar $\mu\neq0$, centring zaroori hai — yeh pehla aur sabse important step hai.
  2. Route 1: har point se $\mu$ ghatao; $C_{jk}=\tfrac1n\sum_i\tilde x_{ij}\tilde x_{ik}$. Route 2 (F1): $S=\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T$ banao, phir $C=S-\mu\mu^T$.
  3. $2\times2$: $\lambda=\tfrac{\operatorname{tr}}2\pm\sqrt{\big(\tfrac{C_{11}-C_{22}}2\big)^2+C_{12}^2}$, ya pattern $\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{bmatrix}\Rightarrow a\pm b$.
  4. Check: $\lambda_1+\lambda_2=\operatorname{tr}C=\tfrac1n\sum\|\tilde x_i\|^2$.

B. "Sum of all entries of $C$" (P10 type)

  1. Har point ke liye $s_i=\sum_j x_{ij}$ (features ka jod) likho.
  2. $s_i$ ka 1-D variance ($\tfrac1n$, mean ghatake) = $\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1$ = answer. Matrix banane ki zaroorat hi nahi.

C. Constraints → zero eigenvalues → PC1 (P12 type)

  1. Har constraint ko $a_k^Tx=0$ form mein likho (e.g. "$x_2=x_1$" ⇒ $a=(1,-1,0,\dots)$).
  2. Independent constraints gino ($a_k$ linearly independent) ⇒ utne zero eigenvalues (baaki features mein koi aur hidden dependence na ho to exactly utne).
  3. PC1: dependent features ko base feature ke multiple ke roop mein likho, $x_j=c_jx_b$. Tab block covariance $=\sigma_b^2\,cc^T$ ⇒ us block ka eigenvalue $\sigma_b^2\|c\|^2$, direction $c/\|c\|$. Baaki uncorrelated features apne variance ko eigenvalue banate hain. Sabse bada = $\lambda_1$.
  4. Check: PC1 unit length hai? Har constraint vector $a_k$ ke perpendicular hai?

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — proofs, edge cases, connections

§1 Proof of F1 (centring identity)

$\tfrac1n\sum(x_i-\mu)(x_i-\mu)^T=\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T-\tfrac1n\sum x_i\mu^T-\tfrac1n\sum\mu x_i^T+\mu\mu^T=S-\mu\mu^T-\mu\mu^T+\mu\mu^T=S-\mu\mu^T$, kyunki $\tfrac1n\sum x_i=\mu$. Taking trace: $\tfrac1n\sum\|\tilde x_i\|^2=\tfrac1n\sum\|x_i\|^2-\|\mu\|^2$ — B5 ke P7 ka "farak $=\|\mu\|^2$" isi ka special case hai.

§2 Proof of F2

$\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1=\tfrac1n\sum_i(\mathbf 1^T\tilde x_i)^2=\tfrac1n\sum_i(s_i-\bar s)^2$, jahan $s_i=\mathbf 1^Tx_i$ aur $\bar s=\mathbf 1^T\mu$. General: koi bhi $v$ (unit ho ya na ho) ke liye $v^TCv=\operatorname{Var}(v^Tx)$. Isliye $C$ PSD hai: variance kabhi negative nahi.

§3 Proof of F3 + kyun eigenvectors same nahi

$A^TAv=\lambda v$ ⇒ left se $A$: $AA^T(Av)=\lambda(Av)$. Agar $\lambda>0$ to $Av\neq0$ (kyunki $\|Av\|^2=v^TA^TAv=\lambda\|v\|^2>0$), aur $\|Av\|=\sqrt\lambda$ jab $\|v\|=1$ ⇒ $u=Av/\sqrt\lambda$ unit eigenvector of $AA^T$. Ulta bhi same tarah. Isliye non-zero spectrum same; zero eigenvalues ki ginti alag ($d-r$ vs $n-r$, $r=\operatorname{rank}A$). Eigenvectors same hone ke liye $u=v$ chahiye, yaani $Av=\sqrt\lambda v$ — yeh tab hi hota hai jab $A$ khud symmetric-type ho; general $A$ mein nahi. Yeh SVD $A=U\Sigma V^T$ ka seedha matlab hai: $AA^T=U\Sigma^2U^T$, $A^TA=V\Sigma^2V^T$.

Numerical check (code-verified): $A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2&0\\0&1&1\end{bmatrix}$: $AA^T=\begin{bmatrix}5&2\\2&2\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $6,1$; $A^TA$ ($3\times3$) eigenvalues $6,1,0$. $\lambda=6$: $v=\tfrac1{\sqrt{30}}(2,5,1)$, $Av/\sqrt6=\tfrac1{\sqrt5}(2,1)$ ✓ eigenvector of $AA^T$.

§4 Constraints ⇔ null space (F4 ka converse)

$Ca=0\iff a^TCa=0\iff\tfrac1n\sum(a^T\tilde x_i)^2=0\iff a^T\tilde x_i=0\ \forall i$. Pehla "⇐" PSD ki wajah se: $a^TCa=0$ aur $C\succeq0$ ⇒ $Ca=0$ (likho $C=Q\Lambda Q^T$; $a^TCa=\sum\lambda_k(q_k^Ta)^2=0$ ⇒ jahan $\lambda_k>0$ wahan $q_k^Ta=0$ ⇒ $Ca=0$). Null space ka dimension $=$ zero eigenvalues $=d-\operatorname{rank}C$.

Edge case: constraint $a^Tx=b$ ($b\neq0$, affine) bhi chalega — centring ke baad $a^T\tilde x=b-b=0$. Isliye "centred" na bhi bola ho, affine constraints zero eigenvalues dete hain.

§5 Ties aur "the" PC1

Cube mein $C=I$ ⇒ har unit $u$ eigenvector, variance 1. "Direction of maximum variance" unique nahi. Exam phir bhi value poochh sakta hai (P10 ke sibling Q9 mein "any unit $v$ ka variance" — B7). Value unique hai, direction nahi.

§6 Eigenvalue sequences

$\lambda_i=c\,r^{i}$ ($0<r<1$, $i=1..m$): $\lambda_1=cr$; total $=cr\frac{1-r^m}{1-r}$. P13: $c=100,r=\tfrac12$ ⇒ $\lambda_1=50$, total $=100(1-2^{-100})$. PC1 ka share $=\frac{1-r}{1-r^m}\approx1-r=50\%$. "Max-variance direction" ⇒ hamesha sabse bada term; agar sequence badhta ho (e.g. $\lambda_i=i^2$), to sabse bada index lo — order check karna mat bhoolo.

§7 $\tfrac1n$ vs $\tfrac1{n-1}$

Saare entries aur eigenvalues ek factor $\tfrac n{n-1}$ se scale; directions, ratios, zero-eigenvalue count same. Isliye MCQ (P8) mein option-set se convention pakdo: agar sirf $\tfrac13$ wale options hain ($n=3$), $\tfrac1n$ hi intended hai.

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: covariance matrix $C$ (MCQ).
  • Given: 3 points in $\mathbb R^2$, "standard PCA" (⇒ centring). Mean $=\tfrac13(0+2+1,\ 0+1+2)=(1,1)\neq0$.

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · Centre → outer products

Step 1: $\mu=(1,1)$. $\tilde x_1=(-1,-1),\ \tilde x_2=(1,0),\ \tilde x_3=(0,1)$.

Step 3: Sum $=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$; $\div n=3$.

Step 4: $C=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ option 2.

M2 · $C=S-\mu\mu^T$

Step 1: $\mu=(1,1)$, $\mu\mu^T=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}3&3\\3&3\end{bmatrix}$.

Step 2: $\sum x_ix_i^T=0+\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&4\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}5&4\\4&5\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ $S=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}5&4\\4&5\end{bmatrix}$ (= option 1!).

Step 3: $S-\mu\mu^T=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}5-3&4-3\\4-3&5-3\end{bmatrix}$.

Step 4: $=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\1&2\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ option 2.

M3 · Size + trace elimination

Step 1: Data $\mathbb R^2$ ⇒ $C$ is $2\times2$ ⇒ options 3, 4 out.

Step 2: $\operatorname{tr}C=\tfrac1n\sum\|\tilde x_i\|^2$. Centred norms² (M1 step 1): $2,1,1$ ⇒ $\operatorname{tr}C=\tfrac43$.

Step 3: Option 1 trace $=\tfrac{10}3$, option 2 trace $=\tfrac43$.

Step 4: Sirf option 2 bacha.

Core model: $v^TCv=\operatorname{Var}(v^Tx)$ har $v$ ke liye — isi se $\lambda_k$ (unit PC), $C_{jj}$ (axis), $\mathbf 1^TC\mathbf 1$ (sum-feature), aur zero eigenvalues (constraint directions) sab nikalte hain.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $C=\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T-\mu\mu^T$ (any data)
