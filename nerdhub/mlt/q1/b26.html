2024 July 07 · Q6: The EM algorithm is run on a dataset of 100 points modeled using a GMM with three components. Let $\lambda_k^i$ be the value obtained in the E-step for data point $i$ and component $k$. The estimated mixture probabilities are $\pi_1=0.3,\ \pi_2=0.5,\ \pi_3=0.2$. For some data point $x_p$, $\lambda_1^p+\lambda_3^p=0.2$. What is the probability that the second component generated $x_p$, given that we have observed $x_p$?

🧭 Position — yeh kahan baithta hai

  • Pichhla: 7.3 (B25) ne diye hue $\theta^{(t)}$ par $\gamma_k^i=P(z_i=k\mid x_i)$ nikala. 7.4 EM ka doosra half-step hai: $\gamma$ ko fixed maan kar parameters badalna.
  • Prerequisites: G1 (weighted average: weights $\ge0$, sum $1$), S4 ($\gamma$, $\sum_k\gamma_k=1$), T5 ka Gaussian MLE ($\hat\mu=\bar x$, $\hat\sigma^2=\tfrac1n\sum(x-\bar x)^2$), T6 ($\ln$ concave — sirf deeper insight mein).
  • Yeh kya unlock karta hai: 7.5 (EM vs Lloyd: M-step ka hard limit Lloyd ka update hai) aur poora EM loop: E → M → E → M…, har round mein $\ell(\theta)$ badhta hai.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

E-step ne har point par ek vote-sheet bana di: point $x_i$ ke $\gamma_1^i$ hisse component 1 ke, $\gamma_2^i$ hisse component 2 ke ($\gamma_1^i+\gamma_2^i=1$). Ab sawaal: "agar yeh hisse sach hote, to component 1 ke parameters kya hote?" Jawab seedha hai: component 1 ke paas kul $N_1=\sum_i\gamma_1^i$ points hain (fractional), uska mean in points ka weighted average hai (jis point ka hissa zyada, uska pull zyada), aur variance weighted spread. Component ka weight $\pi_1$ = uske hisse ka fraction $N_1/n$.

Analogy (labelled): ek class ko do teams mein "aadha-aadha" baantna [ADDED] — student $i$ team 1 ko $\gamma_1^i$ hisse deta hai. Team 1 ki "average height" nikalne ke liye har student ki height ko uske hisse se tolo aur kul hisse se divide karo. Team ka size $N_1$ = hisson ka jod, aur team ka share $\pi_1=N_1/n$.

Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: asli students ek hi team mein hote hain; yahan "aadha" sirf hamari anischitata (uncertainty) hai, koi student sach mein aadha nahi hota. Aur teams ke parameters agle round mein badalte hain to hisse bhi badal jaate hain — jo class ke saath nahi hota.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • $\mu_k\ne$ un points ka mean jinka $\gamma_k>0.5$ (hard cut-off) — saare points ka weighted mean hai, kam hisse wale bhi thoda kheenchte hain.
  • Denominator $n$ nahi: $\mu_k$ aur $\sigma_k^2$ mein $N_k=\sum_i\gamma_k^i$ se divide; sirf $\pi_k$ mein $n$ se.
  • $\pi_k$ ka formula $\mu_k$ mein nahi aata: M-step mein $\pi_k$ pehle ke $\pi_k$ se nahi, naye $N_k/n$ se bana.
  • $\sigma_k^2$ mein purana $\mu_k$ nahi — naya $\mu_k^{new}$ (Example 3 mein dikhaya).
  • M-step $\gamma$ ko badalta nahi (woh E-step ka kaam hai); yahan $\gamma$ fixed hai aur $\theta$ variable.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

(a) Weighted counts aur mixing weights (L67, L68)

$$N_k=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_k^{\,i},\qquad \pi_k^{new}=\frac{N_k}{n}$$

Symbols aur assumptions: $\gamma_k^i=\lambda_k^i$ E-step ka output ($0\le\gamma\le1$, $\sum_k\gamma_k^i=1$); $n$ points; $N_k$ component $k$ ke "expected points". Data i.i.d.

Words mein: $\pi_k$ = component $k$ ka average hissa; yaani sab points ke $\gamma_k^i$ ka simple average.

Notice that… $\sum_kN_k=\sum_i\sum_k\gamma_k^i=n$, isliye $\sum_k\pi_k^{new}=1$ khud ho jaata hai (koi alag normalisation nahi).

(b) Mean (L69)

$$\mu_k^{new}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_k^{\,i}\,x_i}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_k^{\,i}}$$

Words mein: weights $w_i=\gamma_k^i/N_k$ (nonneg, sum $1$) ke saath $x_i$ ka weighted average (G1). Vector data mein har coordinate ke liye same formula.

(c) Variance (L70)

$$\sigma_k^{2,new}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_k^{\,i}\,\bigl(x_i-\mu_k^{new}\bigr)^2}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_k^{\,i}}$$

Notice that… yeh $\mu_k^{new}$ (b) ke baad nikalta hai: pehle mean, phir us mean ke around spread. $d$-dim spherical $\sigma_k^2I$ mein $\|x_i-\mu_k\|^2$ aur denominator mein extra $d$.

Small worked example (Ex1 ka number-by-number): $x=(1,2,6)$, $\gamma_1=(0.9,0.8,0.1)$: $N_1=1.8$, $\pi_1=0.6$, $\mu_1=\dfrac{0.9+1.6+0.6}{1.8}=\dfrac{3.1}{1.8}=1.7222$.

Important insight: M-step ka har formula T5 ka MLE hai jisme har point ko ek fractional multiplicity $\gamma_k^i$ mili. Hard $\gamma$ daalo to seedha Lloyd (L71).

This is why P2 mein $\pi_k$, $n=100$ jaise numbers distractor hain: ek akele point ka $\lambda_2^p$ us point ke baaki $\lambda$ se milta hai ($\sum_k=1$), parameters se nahi.

⚙️ How it works — M-step ka numbered mechanism

  1. E-step ka output $\gamma_k^i$ (table $n\times K$) pakdo; check karo har row ka jod $1$ hai.
  2. Har component $k$ ke liye column ka jod $N_k=\sum_i\gamma_k^i$.
  3. $\pi_k^{new}=N_k/n$.
  4. Ek column bana: $\gamma_k^ix_i$; uska jod $\div N_k$ $\Rightarrow\mu_k^{new}$.
  5. Ek aur column: $\gamma_k^i(x_i-\mu_k^{new})^2$; jod $\div N_k\Rightarrow\sigma_k^{2,new}$.
  6. $\theta^{(t+1)}=\{\pi_k,\mu_k,\sigma_k^2\}$ ko agle E-step ko do.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. $\mu_k$ aur $\sigma_k^2$ ka derivation (weighted Gaussian MLE)

E-step ke baad $\gamma$ fixed hai. Component $k$ ka objective $Q_k=\sum_i\gamma_k^i\ln\mathcal N(x_i;\mu,\sigma^2)=-\tfrac12\sum_i\gamma_k^i\bigl[\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)+\tfrac{(x_i-\mu)^2}{\sigma^2}\bigr]$.

$\dfrac{\partial Q_k}{\partial\mu}=\dfrac1{\sigma^2}\sum_i\gamma_k^i(x_i-\mu)=0\ \Rightarrow\ \mu=\dfrac{\sum_i\gamma_k^ix_i}{\sum_i\gamma_k^i}$. Phir $\dfrac{\partial Q_k}{\partial\sigma^2}=-\dfrac{N_k}{2\sigma^2}+\dfrac{\sum_i\gamma_k^i(x_i-\mu)^2}{2\sigma^4}=0\ \Rightarrow\ \sigma^2=\dfrac{\sum_i\gamma_k^i(x_i-\mu)^2}{N_k}$. Jab $\gamma\equiv1$ hai to yeh T5 ka Gaussian MLE hi hai.

2. $\pi_k=N_k/n$ ka derivation (Lagrange)

$\pi$ ka hissa $\sum_kN_k\ln\pi_k$ hai, shart $\sum_k\pi_k=1$. $\mathcal L=\sum_kN_k\ln\pi_k-\nu(\sum_k\pi_k-1)$; $\dfrac{N_k}{\pi_k}=\nu\Rightarrow\pi_k=N_k/\nu$; jod lene par $1=\sum N_k/\nu=n/\nu\Rightarrow\nu=n$. Isliye $\pi_k=N_k/n$ — aur yeh T5 ke Bernoulli MLE ($k/n$) ka $K$-category roop hai.

3. EM kyun kaam karta hai — $\ell$ kabhi ghatta nahi (T6 ka Jensen) [ADDED]

Kisi bhi weights $q_k^i$ ($\ge0$, har point par sum $1$) ke liye Jensen ($\ln$ concave): $\ell(\theta)=\sum_i\ln\sum_kq_k^i\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x_i)}{q_k^i}\ \ge\ \sum_i\sum_kq_k^i\ln\dfrac{\pi_kf_k(x_i)}{q_k^i}$. Barabari tab hoti hai jab $q_k^i=\gamma_k^i$ (E-step). M-step wahi lower bound ko $\theta$ par maximise karta hai. Isliye $\ell(\theta^{(t)})=\text{bound}(\theta^{(t)})\le\text{bound}(\theta^{(t+1)})\le\ell(\theta^{(t+1)})$.

4. Edge case: component ek point par collapse ($\sigma_k^2\to0$)

Agar koi component ek hi point ko poora hissa de de ($N_k\approx1$, $\mu_k=x_i$) to $\sigma_k^2\to0$ ho sakta hai aur us point par density $\to\infty$, yaani $\ell$ unbounded. Practical GMM mein variance par neeche ki seema (floor) lagate hain. Yeh exam ka kaam nahi, par "EM global optimum nahi deta" (7.5) ki ek wajah hai.

5. Sirf $\pi$ unknown ho to (Example 2)

$f_1,f_2$ known hon to $\mu,\sigma^2$ ka M-step hat jaata hai; sirf $\pi_1=\theta\leftarrow N_1/n$. B25 Q19 aise hi mixture ka sawaal tha. Is chhote model mein $\ell$ ka maximiser $\theta=1$ par boundary hai (data ka mean $0.9\approx1/\text{rate}$ aur $f_1$ ka rate $1$ ke bahut kareeb), isliye $\theta^{(t)}\to1$ — EM boundary ki taraf dheere dheere chalta hai (code se: $\theta^{(7)}=0.910$ aur badhta). Yeh "tay" sirf is data ke liye hai, general rule nahi.

3. 🧮 Working

🧠 Intuition — plain language

Lloyd aur EM ek hi kahani ke do sanskaran hain. Dono "assign karo → parameters update karo" ke loop hain. Lloyd: har point ek hi cluster ko jaata hai (winner takes all) aur cluster ka mean us cluster ke points ka plain average. EM: har point sab components ko hisson mein jaata hai ($\gamma$), aur har component ka mean/variance/weight hisse-weighted. Agar components bahut sakht (chhote $\sigma^2$) aur barabar-weight hon, to $\gamma$ lagbhag $0/1$ ban jaata hai aur EM = Lloyd.

Analogy (labelled): chunaav [ADDED] — Lloyd "first-past-the-post" hai: har voter ek hi seat ko jaata hai. EM "proportional representation" hai: har voter ka vote seats ke beech baantta hai. Har round ke baad seats ke centre (mean) naye bante hain.

Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: chunaav mein har seat ka size/spread seekha nahi jaata; EM $\pi_k$ (seat ka size) aur $\sigma_k^2$ (spread) bhi seekhta hai, Lloyd nahi. Aur dono mein "voters" (points) fixed hain, sirf seats chalte hain.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • EM "better Lloyd" nahi, ek bada model hai ($\pi,\mu,\sigma^2$); dono local optimum dete hain, global ki guarantee kisi ko nahi.
  • "Converged" $\neq$ "optimal": Lloyd ek jagah ruk jaata hai jahan koi step $F$ ghata nahi sakta, par woh sabse acchi partition nahi hona zaroori.
  • EM ka output hard clusters nahi: $\gamma$ hai. Hard label chahiye to MAP ($\arg\max_k\gamma_k$) alag se lagao.
  • $\ell$ ka monotone badhna $\Rightarrow$ global max nahi.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Lloyd = "hard EM" (classification EM)

Barabar $\pi_k$ aur sabke liye same $\sigma^2$ lo. E-step mein soft $\gamma$ ki jagah $\arg\max_k\gamma_k$ (hard) lo. M-step ka $\mu_k$ plain mean aur $\pi_k=|S_k|/n$ ban jaata hai. Squared-distance wala $F$ is hard model ka negative (scaled) log-likelihood hai: $-\ln\mathcal N(x;\mu,\sigma^2)=\tfrac1{2\sigma^2}\|x-\mu\|^2+\text{const}$.

2. Lloyd finite steps mein kyun ruk jaata hai; EM kyun nahi (guarantee)

Lloyd: har assign/update step par $F$ non-increasing; jab koi point cluster badalta hai to $F$ strictly ghatta hai (jab tie na ho). Partitions ki sankhya finite ($K^n$) hai aur $F$ strictly ghatta hai, isliye koi partition dobara aa nahi sakta (B17 ka 2026 July Q4 option 2 yahi hai) — algorithm finite steps mein ruk jaata hai. EM mein $\theta$ continuous hai: $\ell$ badhta hai par ek stationary point ki taraf limit mein; tolerance par rukna padta hai.

Convention (course): data matrix $X$ ka shape $d\times n$ hai (har column ek point $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$), label vector $y\in\mathbb R^n$, aur model bina bias ke $\hat y_i=w^Tx_i$. Sab formulae isi convention mein hain.

R1 · Linear model $\hat y=w^Tx$

A. Core idea

Linear model har feature ko ek weight se guna karke jod deta hai. $w$ ek "recipe" hai: $w_j$ batata hai ki feature $j$ ka output par kitna asar hai. Bias nahi hai, isliye model hamesha origin se guzarta hai ($x=0\Rightarrow\hat y=0$).

B. Notation aur operations

  • $x\in\mathbb R^d$, $w\in\mathbb R^d$, $\hat y=w^Tx=\sum_jw_jx_j$ (ek number).
  • Saare $n$ points ek saath: $\hat y=X^Tw\in\mathbb R^n$ ($X$ shape $d\times n$ ⇒ $X^T$ shape $n\times d$).
  • Residual: $r_i=\hat y_i-y_i$ (prediction minus label). Sign ka convention important hai: is course ke gradient formula mein $r=X^Tw-y$.

C. Worked examples [ADDED]

  • Simple: $w=(2,-1)$, $x=(3,4)$: $\hat y=2\cdot3+(-1)\cdot4=2$.
  • Exam-level: P6 ka data ($x_1=(4,1)$, $x_2=(2,8)$, $x_3=(1,4)$, $x_4=(3,2)$), $w=(1,-2)$: $\hat y=(4-2,\ 2-16,\ 1-8,\ 3-4)=(2,-14,-7,-1)$. Labels $y=(2,-14,-7,-1)$ se exact match (code ✓).
  • Tricky: $w=(1,-1)$ wahi data par: $\hat y=(3,-6,-3,1)$, residual $=(1,8,4,2)$ — bina bias ke bhi alag $w$ alag predictions deta hai.

D. Common mistakes

  • $X^Tw$ ki jagah $Xw$ likhna: $X$ $d\times n$ hai, $Xw$ shape-mismatch hai.
  • Stem "$y=wx$" ko scalar product samajhna: yahan $w,x$ vectors hain ($w^Tx$).
  • Bias maan lena: P6 ke options ($y=x_1-2x_2$ jaise) mein koi constant term nahi.

E. Why regression needs this (ML bridge)

Q: "Linear" ka matlab $w$ mein linear hai ya $x$ mein? A: $w$ mein: $\hat y$ $w$ ka linear function hai. Isiliye loss $w$ ka quadratic bowl banta hai (R3) aur GD ki guarantee milti hai (R5).

R2 · Squared error aur MSE (sum vs mean)

A. Core idea

Prediction aur label ke beech ka farq (residual) square karke jodte hain: square se negative/positive errors cancel nahi hote, aur bade error zyada penalise hote hain. Sum ($L$) ko $n$ se bhaag do to mean (MSE) milta hai.

B. Notation

$$L(w)=\sum_{i=1}^n(w^Tx_i-y_i)^2=\|X^Tw-y\|^2,\qquad \text{MSE}(w)=\frac1nL(w)$$

Symbols: $r=X^Tw-y\in\mathbb R^n$. $\|r\|^2=r^Tr=\sum r_i^2$. Assumption: koi regularisation nahi.

C. Worked examples [ADDED]

  • Simple: residual $(1,8,4,2)$ ($w=(1,-1)$): $L=1+64+16+4=85$; MSE $=85/4=21.25$ (code ✓).
  • Exam-level: $w=(1,-2)$: residual $(0,0,0,0)$ ⇒ $L=0$, MSE $=0$ (P7 yahi hai).
  • Tricky: $w=0$ (sab prediction $0$): residual $=-y=(-2,14,7,1)$, $L=4+196+49+1=250$, MSE $=62.5$. Yaani "kuch nahi karna" ka error sabse bura nahi, par $w=(1,-2)$ se bahut zyada.

D. Common mistakes

  • Sum aur mean ko ek samajhna: $85$ vs $21.25$. Gradient mein bhi $2$ vs $2/n$ ka fark aata hai.
  • Absolute error ($|r|$) aur squared error ($r^2$) mix karna.
  • Residual ka sign ulta karna: square mein farq nahi, par gradient mein farq aata hai.

E. ML bridge

Q: MSE $=0$ ka kya matlab? A: Training data par har prediction exact; ye tab hi hota hai jab labels $X^Tw$ ke column space mein hon (P7 mein hota hai). Naye data par accuracy ka koi daawa nahi.

Mini self-check R2

Q: Residuals $(2,-2,1,0)$: $L$ aur MSE? Ans: $L=4+4+1+0=9$; MSE $=9/4=2.25$.

R3 · Quadratic ka gradient

A. Core idea

Gradient $\nabla f(w)$ woh vector hai jo batata hai ki $f$ sabse tezi se kis taraf badhta hai; $-\nabla f$ ghatne ki disha hai. Squared error $w$ ka quadratic function hai, isliye gradient $w$ mein linear hota hai.

B. Notation aur formula

$$\nabla L(w)=2X(X^Tw-y),\qquad \nabla\,\text{MSE}(w)=\frac2nX(X^Tw-y)$$

Symbols: $X$ shape $d\times n$, $r=X^Tw-y\in\mathbb R^n$, gradient $\in\mathbb R^d$. Derivation (1 point): $\partial_w(w^Tx-y)^2=2(w^Tx-y)x$; $n$ points ka jod $\sum_i2r_ix_i=2Xr$.

C. Worked examples [ADDED]

  • Simple (1-D): $f(w)=(w-3)^2$, $f'(w)=2(w-3)$; $f'(0)=-6$ (negative ⇒ $w$ badhao).
  • Exam-level: P6 data, $w=(1,-1)$: $r=(1,8,4,2)$, $Xr=(4+16+4+6,\ 1+64+16+4)=(30,85)$, $\nabla\text{MSE}=\frac24(30,85)=(15,\ 42.5)$ (code ✓).
  • Tricky: $w=(1,-2)$ par $r=0\Rightarrow\nabla=0$: gradient $0$ hone par update ruk jaata hai (yeh minimum hai).

D. Common mistakes

  • $2/n$ ki jagah $1/n$ ya $2$ likhna: dekho ki sum ka gradient hai ya mean ka.
  • $X^T$ ko beech mein bhool jaana ($X(Xw-y)$): shapes check karo.
  • $r=y-X^Tw$ lekar bhi $+2Xr$ likhna: sign ulta ho jaata hai.

E. ML bridge

Q: Gradient zero kab? A: $Xr=0$, yaani residual har feature direction se orthogonal. Yahi R4 (normal equations) hai.

Mini self-check R3

Q: $f(w)=\frac12\lambda w^2$ ka $f'$ aur wo $w$ jahan $f'=0$? Ans: $f'=\lambda w$; $w=0$.

R4 · Normal equations

A. Core idea

Loss ek bowl hai; bowl ka sabse neecha point wahan hai jahan slope $0$. $\nabla L=0$ ko $w$ ke liye solve karo.

B. Notation

$$XX^Tw=Xy\ \Longrightarrow\ w^*=(XX^T)^{-1}Xy\quad(\text{jab }XX^T\text{ invertible})$$

$XX^T$ shape $d\times d$ (symmetric, PSD). Invertible $\iff$ $X$ ke rows (features) linearly independent. Agar nahi, to infinite solutions (non-unique $w^*$).

C. Worked example (P6 ka data) [ADDED]

$XX^T=\begin{bmatrix}30&30\\30&85\end{bmatrix}$ ($16+4+1+9=30$; $4+16+4+6=30$; $1+64+16+4=85$), $Xy=\begin{bmatrix}-30\\-140\end{bmatrix}$ ($8-28-7-3$; $2-112-28-2$). Solve: $30w_1+30w_2=-30\Rightarrow w_1+w_2=-1$; $30w_1+85w_2=-140\Rightarrow 55w_2=-110\Rightarrow w_2=-2,\ w_1=1$. $w^*=(1,-2)$ (code ✓).

D. Common mistakes

  • $X^TX$ ($n\times n$) ko normal equation mein lagana: yahan sahi matrix $XX^T$ hai ($d\times d$).
  • Invertibility check bhoolna (collinear features).
  • Solution ko "sabse accha model" samajhna jabki sirf training squared error ka minimiser hai.

E. ML bridge

Q: GD ki zaroorat kyun agar closed form hai? A: $(XX^T)^{-1}$ ka cost $O(d^3)$; bade $d$ par GD/SGD sasta hota hai. Dono ka minimum wahi hai.

Mini self-check R4

Q: 1-D, $x=(1,2,3)$, $y=(2,4,7)$, bina bias: $w^*$? Ans: $XX^T=14$, $Xy=2+8+21=31$, $w^*=31/14=2.2143$ (code ✓).

R5 · Gradient descent aur learning rate

A. Core idea

Pahaad se utarne ki tarah: jahan khade ho wahan ki dhal (gradient) dekho, aur uske ulta chhota kadam $\eta$ lo. Kadam bahut bada ho to ghaati paar karke doosri taraf chadh jaoge.

B. Notation

$$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\,\nabla f(w_t),\qquad \eta>0$$

MSE ke liye $\nabla f=\frac2nX(X^Tw-y)$. Smoothness constant $L=\frac2n\lambda_{\max}(XX^T)$. Convergence ki shart: $0<\eta<2/L$ (8.2 mein proof).

C. Worked examples [ADDED]

  • 1-D $f(w)=2w^2$ ($f'=4w$, $L=4$): $w_{t+1}=(1-4\eta)w_t$. $\eta=0.25$: $w$ ek step mein $0$. $\eta=0.5=2/L$: $1,-1,1,-1,\dots$ (oscillation, converge nahi). $\eta=0.6$: $1,-1.4,1.96,-2.744,\dots$ (diverge) — code ✓.
  • P6 data par ($L=49.0985$, $2/L=0.0407$): $\eta=0.02$ par MSE $62.5\to8.6\to5.96\to4.12\to\dots\to0$; $\eta=0.045$ par blow-up.

D. Common mistakes

  • "$\eta$ jitna bada utna tez" maan lena.
  • Sign: $w+\eta\nabla$ (ascent) likhna.
  • Minimum par pahunchne ke baad bhi $\nabla\ne0$ sochna: wahan $\nabla=0$ aur update ruk jaata hai.

E. ML bridge

Q: Regression mein GD global minimum kyun deta hai? A: MSE $w$ ka convex bowl hai (G3 / T6): koi local-but-not-global minimum hai hi nahi, aur $\eta<2/L$ ho to sequence minimum ki taraf jaata hai.

Mini self-check R5

Q: $f(w)=w^2$ ($L=2$): $\eta=1$ par $w_0=1$ ka $w_1,w_2$? Ans: $w_1=1-2=-1$, $w_2=1$; oscillation ($\eta=2/L$).

⚙️ How it works — best fit nikalne ke steps

  1. $X$ ($d\times n$, columns = points) aur $y$ likho.
  2. $XX^T$ ($d\times d$) aur $Xy$ ($d$) compute karo.
  3. $XX^Tw=Xy$ solve karo ($d$ equations, $d$ unknowns).
  4. Check: residual $r=X^Tw-y$ nikalo; agar $r=0$ to MSE $0$.
  5. MSE $=\frac1n\sum r_i^2$ (sum se $n$ ka bhaag mat bhoolo).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Normal equations ka derivation aur geometric matlab

$L=(X^Tw-y)^T(X^Tw-y)$. $w$ ke baare mein gradient $2X(X^Tw-y)$. $=0$ ka matlab $Xr=0$: har feature (row of $X$) residual ke saath orthogonal. Yaani $X^Tw^*$ column space $\mathcal C(X^T)\subseteq\mathbb R^n$ par $y$ ka orthogonal projection hai (T1 ka vector projection, bas yahan subspace par).

2. $XX^T$ kab invertible nahi hota?

Agar koi feature doosre feature ka linear combination ho (rows dependent), to $\det(XX^T)=0$ aur $w^*$ unique nahi: infinite $w$ barabar MSE dete hain. (Ridge isi ko theek karta hai; syllabus ke bahar.)

🧠 Intuition

GD ek andhere pahaad par utarne jaisa hai: har kadam par sirf abhi ki dhal dikhti hai. Regression ka loss ek bowl hai (convex), isliye sab raaste neeche ke ek hi point par jaate hain. Hamara kaam: kadam ka size $\eta$ itna rakhna ki na rengte rahein, na bowl ke us paar uchhal jaayein.

Analogy (labelled): gend aur katori [ADDED] — loss katori hai, $w$ gend ki jagah. Chhota $\eta$: gend dheere neeche. Bada par $<2/L$: gend dono taraf chadhti-utarti hue bhi neeche. $\eta=2/L$: gend baar-baar usi unchai par dono taraf. $\eta>2/L$: har baar zyada unchai par — bahar ud jaati hai.

Yeh kahan toot-ta hai: katori ki shape $L$ (kitni tez curvature) $w$ ke direction par depend karti hai; $d>1$ mein sabse tez-curvature wali direction $\eta$ ki upar-seema tay karti hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • GD "kuch bhi $\eta$ par converge" nahi karta; $\eta<2/L$ chahiye.
  • SGD GD ka sasta version nahi jo wahi guarantee de: constant $\eta$ par SGD ek chhote ilaake mein ghoomta rehta hai.
  • "Convex" ka matlab "ek step mein minimum" nahi; sirf "local $=$ global".

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

$$w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta\nabla f(w_t),\qquad f(w)=\text{MSE}(w),\ \ \nabla f(w)=\frac2nX(X^Tw-y)$$

Symbols/assumptions: $\eta>0$ constant; $f$ convex quadratic; $H=\nabla^2f=\frac2nXX^T$ (constant, symmetric PSD). $L=\lambda_{\max}(H)=\frac2n\lambda_{\max}(XX^T)$.

Words mein: har step mein ($-$gradient) ki taraf $\eta$ jitna chal do.

Notice that… $w_{t+1}-w^*=(I-\eta H)(w_t-w^*)$ (jab $XX^T$ invertible, $\nabla f(w)=H(w-w^*)$). Error har step mein matrix $I-\eta H$ se guna hota hai.

Convergence ki shart

$H$ ke eigenvalues $0<\lambda_1\le\dots\le\lambda_d=L$. $I-\eta H$ ke eigenvalues $1-\eta\lambda_j$. Sab ka $|\cdot|<1$ ho tabhi error $\to0$:

$$|1-\eta\lambda_j|<1\ \ \forall j\iff0<\eta<\frac2{\lambda_j}\ \ \forall j\iff \boxed{0<\eta<\frac2L}$$
$\eta$ ka range$\eta L$Behaviour (sabse tez direction)
$0<\eta<1/L$$<1$Monotone convergence
$\eta=1/L$$=1$Is direction mein ek step mein $0$
$1/L<\eta<2/L$$1$ se $2$Zigzag, par converge
$\eta=2/L$$=2$$\pm$ oscillation, converge nahi
$\eta>2/L$$>2$Diverge

Important insight: Convexity ($H\succeq0$) ki wajah se koi bad local minimum hai hi nahi: GD ka limit (agar converge ho) hamesha global minimum hota hai.

This is why P8 mein statement 1 ✓ aur 2 ✗ (par "$\eta$ sahi" ki shart yaad rakhna).

SGD (ek point ka gradient)

SGD har step par ek random point $i$ ka gradient $2(w^Tx_i-y_i)x_i$ leta hai. Ye unbiased hai (average mein poore gradient ke barabar), par noisy hai. Constant $\eta$ par $w_t$ $w^*$ ke aas-paas ek chhote ilaake mein ghoomta rehta hai (jab tak labels exactly fit na hon).

⚙️ How it works — GD ke steps

  1. $w_0$ chuno (e.g. $0$), $\eta$ chuno ($<2/L$).
  2. Gradient: $g_t=\frac2nX(X^Tw_t-y)$.
  3. Update: $w_{t+1}=w_t-\eta g_t$.
  4. Rukne ka rule: $\|g_t\|$ chhota ya MSE ka badlaav chhota.
  5. Output $w_T\approx w^*$.

Non-example

  • "GD ka koi bhi $\eta$ chalega kyunki loss convex hai" ✗: convex hai par $\eta>2/L$ par diverge.
  • "SGD $\Rightarrow$ hamesha thoda galat" ✗: agar labels exactly fit hon (P6) to SGD bhi exact minimum par converge karta hai (neeche D3-3).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3 — collapsible)

1. Proof ka ek line: $|1-\eta\lambda|<1\iff0<\eta<2/\lambda$

$|1-\eta\lambda|<1\iff-1<1-\eta\lambda<1\iff0<\eta\lambda<2$. Sab $\lambda_j\le L$ ke liye sabse tight shart sabse bade $\lambda=L$ ki hai.

2. Convergence ki raftaar aur condition number

Error har step mein direction $j$ par factor $|1-\eta\lambda_j|$ se ghatta hai; overall rate $=\max_j|1-\eta\lambda_j|$. Tez direction ($\lambda_{\max}$) $\eta$ ko $<2/L$ par rokti hai, aur $\eta$ bada hone par wahi zigzag se rate ko bigaadti hai ($\eta=0.04$: $0.964$); $\eta$ chhota karne par slow direction ($\lambda_{\min}$) rate tay karti hai ($\eta=0.02$: $0.832$). Behtareen $\eta=2/(\lambda_{\max}+\lambda_{\min})$ ho to rate $(\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)$, $\kappa=\lambda_{\max}/\lambda_{\min}$; P6 data par $\kappa=5.84$, rate $0.708$ (code ✓).

3. SGD ke baare mein sahi bayaan [ADDED]

(a) Noisy labels mein constant $\eta$ par SGD global minimum par exactly converge nahi karta (Example 3). (b) $\eta_t\to0$ (aur $\sum\eta_t=\infty$, $\sum\eta_t^2<\infty$) jaisi decaying schedule se convex problem mein convergence ka theorem hai — par PYQ ka statement 3 shart nahi batata, isliye "guaranteed" ✗. (c) Exact-fit (interpolation) case mein har point ka gradient $w^*$ par $0$ hota hai, isliye SGD bhi converge karta hai: P6 data par $\eta=0.005$, $200{,}000$ steps ke baad $w=(1,-2)$ (code ✓).

4. Iterations vs cost per iteration

GD ka ek step $O(nd)$ (sab points), SGD ka $O(d)$. "Kam iterations" (statement 4) aur "kam total cost" alag baatein hain: SGD zyada, par sasti steps leta hai.
