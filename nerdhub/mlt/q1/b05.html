Peeche (prerequisites)Yeh subtopicAage (kya unlock hota hai)

🧠 Intuition — plain language

Data ka ek "badal" (cloud) socho. Har direction $u$ mein dekho to points kitne phaile hue hain — yahi variance along $u$ hai. Kisi direction mein badal lamba hai (zyada variance), kisi mein patla. PCA bas itna kehta hai:

  • PC1 = woh direction jisme badal sabse lamba hai. Uska variance $=\lambda_1$ (covariance ka sabse bada eigenvalue).
  • PC2 = PC1 ke perpendicular directions mein sabse lamba — variance $\lambda_2$. Aise hi aage.
  • Total variance = har feature ka variance jodo $=\operatorname{tr}C=\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_d$. Direction ghumane se total nahi badalta — sirf bantwara badalta hai.
  • "Explained" fraction = top-$k$ $\lambda$ ka sum ÷ total.

Analogy [ADDED] — rugby ball ki torch-parchhai. Ek rugby ball (lamba ellipsoid) ko alag-alag directions se dekho. Lambi side se dekho to sabse lambi parchhai — woh PC1. Uske perpendicular sabse lambi — PC2. Ball ko kaise bhi ghumao, uske "teen axis lengths" wahi rehte hain — yahi eigenvalues hain; har axis ke "length²" ka sum total variance jaisa fixed hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Variance spread ka square hai, length nahi — direction ke saath $u^TCu$ smooth curve banata hai (neeche widget), parchhai ki length jaisa nahi. (2) Real data mein "ball" ki koi surface nahi — points hain, aur variance $\tfrac1n\sum(u^T\tilde x_i)^2$ un points ka average hai. (3) Agar ball perfectly gol ho ($C=kI$), to koi "lambi side" hi nahi — PC1 unique nahi; analogy yahan kuch nahi batati.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • $\lambda_1$ total variance nahi hai — total $=\sum\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}C$. Sirf jab baaki saare $\lambda=0$ (data ek line par) tab dono barabar.
  • "Variance along x-axis" PC1 ka variance nahi — woh $C_{11}$ hai (diagonal entry), $\lambda_1$ nahi.
  • $\tfrac1n\sum\|x_i\|^2$ (uncentred) variance nahi — pehle mean ghatao. Data centred ho tabhi dono same.
  • PC1 sabse bade feature ki direction nahi — sabse bade eigenvalue ki direction hai (features correlated ho sakte hain).

📊 Visual intuition — direction ghumao, variance dekho

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\underbrace{C=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(x_i-\mu)(x_i-\mu)^T}_{\text{covariance}}\qquad \underbrace{\operatorname{Var}_u=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\big(u^T(x_i-\mu)\big)^2=u^TCu}_{\text{variance along unit }u}\qquad \underbrace{\max_{\|u\|=1}u^TCu=\lambda_1,\ \text{at }u=q_1}_{\text{PC1}}$$ $$\operatorname{Var}_{\text{PC}_k}=q_k^TCq_k=\lambda_k,\qquad \text{total variance}=\operatorname{tr}C=\sum_{j=1}^dC_{jj}=\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda_k,\qquad \text{explained by top }k=\frac{\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_k}{\lambda_1+\cdots+\lambda_d}$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$, $i=1,\dots,n$ — data points; $\mu=\tfrac1n\sum x_i$ mean. "Centred dataset" ka matlab $\mu=0$, tab $C=\tfrac1n\sum x_ix_i^T=\tfrac1nXX^T$ ($X\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$, points columns).
  • $u\in\mathbb R^d$ — direction, $\|u\|=1$ zaroori. Non-unit $v$ ho to $\dfrac{v^TCv}{v^Tv}$.
  • $C=Q\Lambda Q^T$ — spectral decomposition (B4-P5): $Q=[q_1\cdots q_d]$ orthonormal eigenvectors, $\Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1\ge\cdots\ge\lambda_d\ge0)$. $C$ symmetric aur PSD hai, isliye yeh hamesha possible hai.
  • PC$_k$ $=q_k$ (unit eigenvector, sign $\pm$ dono chalte hain).
  • Convention: $\tfrac1n$ (P2 ke key se confirmed). $\tfrac1{n-1}$ se saare $\lambda$ ek hi factor $\tfrac{n}{n-1}$ se scale hote hain — directions aur fractions same rehte hain.

Meaning in words: "Har centred point ko $u$ par project karo (B3), un scalars ka mean square lo — woh $u^TCu$ hai. Sabse bada mean square eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ deta hai, top eigenvector par."

Notice that teen alag numbers aksar confuse hote hain, aur sab $C$ se hi nikalte hain:

Small worked example. $C=\begin{bmatrix}4&0\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$. Diagonal hai, isliye eigenvalues $4,1$ aur PCs $e_1,e_2$ (axes). Variance along PC1 $=4$, total $=5$, explained by PC1 $=80\%$. Direction $u=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$: $u^TCu=\tfrac12(4+0+0+1)=2.5$ — $1$ aur $4$ ke beech ✓.

Important insight: $u$ ko PC basis mein likho: $u=\sum_k c_kq_k$, $\sum c_k^2=1$. Tab $u^TCu=\sum_k c_k^2\lambda_k$ — eigenvalues ka weighted average, weights $c_k^2$. Weighted average kabhi sabse bade value se zyada nahi ho sakta, isliye max $=\lambda_1$ (saara weight $q_1$ par). Aur yahi formula "variance along axis" bhi deta hai: $e_1$ ke PC-weights daalo.

This is why PCA "variance along PC$_k$" ko seedha $\lambda_k$ bolta hai, aur "explained variance" eigenvalues ke ratio se nikalta hai — B10 (choosing $k$) aur B11 (reconstruction error $=\sum_{\text{dropped}}\lambda_k$) dono isi par khade hain.

⚙️ How it works — mechanism

  1. Check centred? Question "centred / mean-centred" bolta hai, ya mean khud nikal ke dekho. $\mu\neq0$ ⇒ $\tilde x_i=x_i-\mu$.
  2. $C$ banao (agar diya nahi): $C_{jk}=\tfrac1n\sum_i\tilde x_{ij}\tilde x_{ik}$. Shortcut: points ek line par ⇒ seedha step 5 ka shortcut.
  3. Eigenvalues: 2×2 mein $\lambda^2-(\operatorname{tr}C)\lambda+\det C=0$. Diagonal $C$ ⇒ diagonal entries hi eigenvalues. $\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\b&a\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ $a\pm b$.
  4. Sort: $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$. Variance along PC$_k$ $=\lambda_k$.
  5. Agar sab points ek line par (collinear): rank 1 ⇒ $\lambda_1=\operatorname{tr}C=\tfrac1n\sum\|\tilde x_i\|^2$, baaki $0$.
  6. Direction diya ho ($u$ ya axis): unit karo, $u^TCu$ nikalo.
  7. Percentage: top-$k$ $\lambda$ ka sum ÷ saare $\lambda$ ka sum (zero wale bhi denominator mein — woh 0 hi jodte hain).
  8. Sanity: $\lambda_d\le u^TCu\le\lambda_1$; $\sum\lambda=\operatorname{tr}C$; $\prod\lambda=\det C$; sab $\lambda\ge0$.

$C$: $C_{11}=\tfrac13(4+0+4)=\tfrac83$; $C_{12}=\tfrac13(0+0-4)=-\tfrac43$; $C_{22}=\tfrac13(0+4+4)=\tfrac83$. $C=\tfrac13\begin{bmatrix}8&-4\\-4&8\end{bmatrix}$.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — proof, ties, zero eigenvalues, centred vs uncentred

1. Proof: $\max_{\|u\|=1}u^TCu=\lambda_1$

$C=Q\Lambda Q^T$. Let $c=Q^Tu$ (u ke PC-coordinates). $Q$ orthogonal hai, isliye $\|c\|=\|u\|=1$, yaani $\sum c_k^2=1$. Ab

$$u^TCu=u^TQ\Lambda Q^Tu=c^T\Lambda c=\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda_kc_k^2\le\lambda_1\sum_kc_k^2=\lambda_1.$$

Barabari $c=(\pm1,0,\dots,0)$ par, yaani $u=\pm q_1$. Same argument ulta: $u^TCu\ge\lambda_d$. Aur PC1 ke perpendicular ($c_1=0$) max $=\lambda_2$ — isliye PC2 ka variance $\lambda_2$.

2. Total variance rotation se nahi badalta

$\operatorname{tr}C=\operatorname{tr}(Q\Lambda Q^T)=\operatorname{tr}(\Lambda Q^TQ)=\operatorname{tr}\Lambda=\sum\lambda_k$ (trace cyclic hai). Aur $\operatorname{tr}C=\sum_jC_{jj}=\tfrac1n\sum_i\|\tilde x_i\|^2$. Kisi bhi orthonormal basis $\{v_k\}$ ke liye $\sum_kv_k^TCv_k=\operatorname{tr}C$ — axes ho ya PCs.

3. Zero eigenvalues = dimensions jo data use nahi karta

$\lambda_k=0$ ⇒ $q_k^TCq_k=\tfrac1n\sum(q_k^T\tilde x_i)^2=0$ ⇒ har centred point $q_k$ ke perpendicular. Number of nonzero eigenvalues $=\operatorname{rank}C=$ centred data ka dimension. Sab points ek line par (through mean) ⇒ rank 1 ⇒ $\lambda_1=\operatorname{tr}C$. Yahi P2 aur P7 ka shortcut hai.

4. Ties: number unique, direction nahi

$\lambda_2=\lambda_3$ ho to us 2-D eigenspace ka koi bhi orthonormal pair PC2/PC3 ban sakta hai. $C=kI$ ⇒ har direction PC1. Variance values phir bhi fixed hain — isliye "variance captured by top-$k$" hamesha well-defined hai (P6).

5. Diagonal $C$ ⇔ PCs = axes

Agar PCs $e_1,\dots,e_d$ hain to $Q=I$ aur $C=I\Lambda I=\Lambda$ — diagonal, entries $=\lambda_k\ge0$. Ulta: diagonal $C$ ke eigenvectors standard basis (ties mein aur bhi choices). Diagonal entries 0 bhi ho sakti hain (feature constant ho) — P3 ka option 3 isi par girta hai.

6. Centred vs uncentred: $C=\tfrac1nXX^T-\mu\mu^T$

Uncentred matrix $M=\tfrac1nXX^T$ mein mean ka rank-one hissa $\mu\mu^T$ juda hai. $\operatorname{tr}M=\operatorname{tr}C+\|\mu\|^2$. P7 mein: $\operatorname{tr}C=18.4375$, $\|\mu\|^2=2.25^2+4.5^2=25.3125$, sum $=43.75$ ✓ — key exactly isi "extra $\|\mu\|^2$" ki wajah se 43.75 hai.

7. $\tfrac1n$ vs $\tfrac1{n-1}$

Dono ka ratio $\tfrac{n}{n-1}$ har eigenvalue par same lagta hai: eigenvectors, ordering, aur % explained bilkul same. Sirf absolute "variance along PC1" badalta hai — wahi SA questions mein farak padta hai. P2: $625$ vs $833.33$.

  2. The variance of the dataset along the first principal component is minimum.
  3. The first principal component is the unit norm eigenvector corresponding to the largest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix.
  4. The first principal component is the unit norm eigenvector corresponding to the smallest eigenvalue of the covariance matrix.

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: PC1 ki definition aur uska eigen-characterisation — MSQ, har option judge karna hai.
  • Given: standard PCA, centred data (mean ka jhanjhat nahi).
  • Trigger clues: "maximum / minimum", "largest / smallest eigenvalue", "unit norm".
  • Prerequisites: B4-P5 spectral theorem (Conceptual), Rayleigh bound $\lambda_d\le u^TCu\le\lambda_1$ (Reference).
  • Core principle: PC1 $=\arg\max_{\|u\|=1}u^TCu=q_1$, value $\lambda_1$.

2. 🧭 Methods

Conceptual MSQ — sirf 2 genuinely alag methods: M1 definition + Rayleigh proof; M2 ek concrete $C$ par options test karna (elimination). Teesra method padding hota.

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · Definition + Rayleigh

Step 1: PCA ki definition: PC1 woh unit direction jisme centred data ka variance $u^TCu$ maximum ho. ⇒ Option 1 ✓, option 2 ✗.

Step 2: Weighted-average argument: $u^TCu=\sum\lambda_kc_k^2\le\lambda_1$, barabari $u=q_1$ par. ⇒ maximiser $=$ largest eigenvalue ka unit eigenvector. Option 3 ✓.

Step 3: Smallest eigenvalue ka eigenvector variance minimise karta hai (last PC). Option 4 ✗.

Answer: 1, 3.

M2 · Concrete $C$ par elimination

Step 1: Lo $C=\operatorname{diag}(3,1)$ — data x-direction mein zyada phaila.

Step 2: Along $e_1$: $3$; along $e_2$: $1$; along $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$: $2$. Max $3$ at $e_1$.

Step 3: $e_1$ largest eigenvalue $3$ ka eigenvector hai ⇒ options 1, 3 consistent; option 2 (min) aur 4 (smallest, $e_2$) fail.

Answer: 1, 3.

6. ⚠️ Traps

  • "Minimum" — reconstruction error minimum hota hai PC1 par; variance maximum. Dono yaad hon to mix hote hain.
  • "Unit norm" — PC hamesha unit; non-unit eigenvector bhi eigenvector hai par "the PC" unit wala hai.

Recognition rule: PC1 ↔ max variance ↔ $\lambda_{\max}$ ↔ min reconstruction error. Last PC ↔ min variance ↔ $\lambda_{\min}$.

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: $\lambda_1$ — ek number.
  • Given: 4 points, sab line $y=3x$ par (origin se). Mean: $\tfrac14(-10-5+5+10,\ -30-15+15+30)=(0,0)$ — already centred.
  • Trigger clues: "lie on a line passing through the origin", "variance along the first PC".
  • Prerequisites: B4-P1 centring (Procedural), B4-P3 covariance (Procedural), B4-P4 2×2 eigen (Procedural), rank-1 fact (Conceptual), B3 projection (Analogous).
  • Core principle: data ek line par ⇒ $\operatorname{rank}C=1$ ⇒ $\lambda_1=\operatorname{tr}C$, $\lambda_2=0$.
