Tier 1 · General foundations — quick refresher

G1 · Averages, Σ, squares

A. Average = sab jodo, ginti se divide karo: $\bar z=\tfrac1n\sum_{i=1}^n z_i$.

B. $\sum_i(z_i-c)=\sum_iz_i-nc$. Isliye $c=\bar z$ par yeh sum $0$ hota hai.

C. $z=(2,4,9)$: $\bar z=\tfrac{15}{3}=5$; deviations $(-3,-1,4)$, sum $=0$ ✓.

D. $\overline{z^2}\neq(\bar z)^2$ — pehle square karo phir average, ya pehle average phir square: alag answers.

E. Mean, variance, covariance — teeno averages hain.

G2 · 2×2 determinant + quadratic (NEW)

A. $\det\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}=ad-bc$. Quadratic $t^2-St+P=0$ ke roots ka sum $S$ aur product $P$ hota hai.

B. Roots: $t=\dfrac{S\pm\sqrt{S^2-4P}}{2}$.

C. $t^2-5t+4=0$: $S=5,P=4$ ⇒ $t=\tfrac{5\pm\sqrt{25-16}}2=\tfrac{5\pm3}2$ ⇒ $4,\ 1$. Check: $4+1=5$, $4\cdot1=4$ ✓.

D. $ad-bc$ ki jagah $ab-cd$; $\sqrt{S^2-4P}$ mein sign galti.

E. 2×2 matrix ke eigenvalues exactly isi quadratic ke roots hain, jahan $S$ = trace aur $P$ = determinant (P4).

G3 · T1 toolkit (B2, B3)

A. Unit $u$ par point $x$ ka coordinate $=u^Tx$; projection $(u^Tx)u$; residual uske perpendicular.

B. Outer product $xx^T$ ek $d\times d$ matrix, entry $(j,k)=x_jx_k$; $\operatorname{tr}(xx^T)=\|x\|^2$.

C. $x=(2,2)$: $xx^T=\begin{bmatrix}4&4\\4&4\end{bmatrix}$, trace $8=\|x\|^2$ ✓.

D. $x^Tx$ (number) aur $xx^T$ (matrix) mix karna; $u$ unit na hona.

E. Variance along $u$ = projections $u^Tx$ ka spread; covariance matrix = outer products ka average.

P1 · Mean vector and centring

A. Core idea

Mean vector = har feature ka alag average — data ka "centre of mass". Centring = har point se mean ghatana, yaani origin ko utha ke data ke beech mein rakh dena. Isse data ka shape nahi badalta, sirf uski jagah badalti hai. PCA ki directions origin se guzarti lines hain (B3 jaisi), isliye unhe data ke beech se guzarna ho to pehle origin ko mean par laana padta hai.

Analogy [ADDED]: class photo mein sabki height naapne se pehle tum floor ki jagah "average height" ki line kheench do — ab har bachcha line se kitna upar/neeche hai, woh dikhta hai. Kahan toot-ti hai: height 1-D hai; data mein har feature ka alag average hota hai, ek single line nahi.

What it is NOT: mean koi data point hona zaroori nahi (running data mein $(3,1)$ koi point nahi hai); aur centring scaling nahi hai — har feature ko std se divide karna "standardising" hai, PCA ke default mein nahi hota.

B. Notation and operations (Formula Protocol)

$$\mu=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_i=\frac1n X\mathbf 1,\qquad \tilde x_i=x_i-\mu,\qquad \tilde X=X-\mu\mathbf 1^T,\qquad \sum_{i=1}^n\tilde x_i=\mathbf 0$$
  • $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$ = $i$-th data point (column of $X\in\mathbb R^{d\times n}$). $n$ = number of points. $\mathbf 1$ = length-$n$ all-ones vector.
  • $\mu\in\mathbb R^d$ = mean vector; $\tilde x_i$ = centred point; $\tilde X$ = centred data matrix (har column se $\mu$ ghata).
  • Assumption: $n\ge1$ points, sab same length $d$.

Meaning in words: mean ke around deviations ka sum hamesha zero. Notice that projection aur mean ka order badal sakte ho: $\tfrac1n\sum_iu^Tx_i=u^T\mu$ — projections ka average = mean ka projection (linearity, B2 P2). Important insight: isliye centred data ke projections ka mean $u^T\mathbf 0=0$ hota hai — P2 mein variance formula isi wajah se itna simple banta hai. This is why PCA mein pehla step hamesha "centre the data" hota hai.

C. Worked examples

Example 1 (simple) — running data. Points $(5,3),(1,-1),(4,0),(2,2)$. Mean aur centred points?

Step 1: first features ka sum $=5+1+4+2=12$; second $=3+(-1)+0+2=4$. Step 2: $\mu=\tfrac14(12,4)=(3,1)$. Step 3: ghatao: $\tilde x_1=(5-3,\,3-1)=(2,2)$; $\tilde x_2=(1-3,\,-1-1)=(-2,-2)$; $\tilde x_3=(4-3,\,0-1)=(1,-1)$; $\tilde x_4=(2-3,\,2-1)=(-1,1)$. Step 4 (check): $(2-2+1-1,\ 2-2-1+1)=(0,0)$ ✓.

Example 3 (tricky) — mean of projections. $u=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$. Running data (uncentred) ke projections $u^Tx_i$ ka average?

Seedha: $u^Tx_i=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt2}(5+3),\ \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1-1),\ \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(4+0),\ \tfrac1{\sqrt2}(2+2)=\tfrac{8}{\sqrt2},\ 0,\ \tfrac4{\sqrt2},\ \tfrac4{\sqrt2}$. Sum $=\tfrac{16}{\sqrt2}$, average $=\tfrac{4}{\sqrt2}=2\sqrt2\approx2.828$.

Shortcut: $u^T\mu=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(3+1)=\tfrac4{\sqrt2}$ ✓ — same. Centred data par yahi average $0$ hota.

Non-example: "mean of norms" $\tfrac1n\sum\|x_i\|$ ≠ "norm of mean" $\|\mu\|$. Centred running data ka $\mu=(0,0)$, norm $0$ — par har point ka norm positive hai.

D. Common mistakes

  • $n$ ki jagah $d$ se divide karna (features ki ginti se).
  • $X$ ($d\times n$) mein row ka average lene ki jagah column ka lena — mean har feature (row) ke across points ka average hai.
  • Centring bhool jaana — phir "PCA" ka answer uncentred matrix ka aata hai (yeh exact galti source key mein bhi dikhti hai: F6, B6 mein).
  • Reconstruction mein mean wapas add karna bhool jaana: $\hat x=\mu+\sum_k(u_k^T\tilde x)u_k$.

P2 · Variance — of a feature, and along a direction

A. Core idea

Variance = mean se squared distance ka average — data kitna faila hua hai. Bada variance = numbers door-door; zero variance = sab same. PCA ke liye asli sawaal: agar har 2-D point ko ek line par project karke ek number bana dein, to un numbers ka variance kitna hai? Woh line ki direction par depend karta hai.

Analogy: torch se deewar par chhaaya (shadow). Kisi angle se chhaaya lambi failti hai, kisi se simat jaati hai. PCA woh angle dhundhta hai jahan chhaaya sabse zyada faile. Kahan toot-ti hai: asli shadow perspective se stretch hoti hai; projection mein length kabhi nahi badhti.

What it is NOT: variance range (max − min) nahi hai, aur mean absolute deviation bhi nahi — squares ki wajah se door wale points ka asar zyada hota hai.

B. Notation and operations (Formula Protocol)

$$\operatorname{Var}(z)=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n(z_i-\bar z)^2=\overline{z^2}-\bar z^2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}_u=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\big(u^T\tilde x_i\big)^2\quad(\|u\|=1)$$
  • $z_1,\dots,z_n$ = numbers (ek feature, ya projections). $\bar z$ = unka mean. $\overline{z^2}=\tfrac1n\sum z_i^2$.
  • $u$ = unit direction; $u^T\tilde x_i$ = centred point ka coordinate us direction mein (G3). Centred data ⇒ in coordinates ka mean $0$ (P1), isliye sirf squares ka average bachta hai.
  • Standard deviation $=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}}$ (original units mein).

Notice that $\operatorname{Var}(az+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(z)$: shift ($b$) spread nahi badalta, scale ($a$) square hokar aata hai. Important insight: isi wajah se direction unit honi chahiye — $w=2u$ lo to "variance" 4 guna ho jaata hai bina data badle. This is why PCA mein constraint $\|u\|=1$ lagta hai; warna $u$ ko bada karke variance infinite kar sakte the.

C. Worked examples

Example 1 (simple). $z=(2,4,4,4,5,5,7,9)$. Variance dono conventions mein?

Mean $=\tfrac{40}{8}=5$. Deviations: $-3,-1,-1,-1,0,0,2,4$. Squares: $9,1,1,1,0,0,4,16$, sum $=32$. $\tfrac1n$: $\tfrac{32}{8}=\mathbf 4$ (std $=2$). $\tfrac1{n-1}$: $\tfrac{32}{7}\approx4.571$.

Shortcut check: $\overline{z^2}=\tfrac{4+16+16+16+25+25+49+81}{8}=\tfrac{232}{8}=29$; $29-5^2=4$ ✓.

Non-example: non-unit $w=(1,1)$ lekar $\tfrac1n\sum(w^T\tilde x_i)^2$: coordinates $4,-4,0,0$ ⇒ $\tfrac{32}{4}=8$ — yeh "variance along (1,1)" nahi hai. $\|w\|^2=2$ se divide karo to sahi $4$ aata hai.

D. Common mistakes

  • Square karna bhool jaana ($\sum(z_i-\bar z)$ to hamesha $0$ hai).
  • Mean ghataye bina $\overline{z^2}$ ko variance bol dena.
  • $\tfrac1n$ / $\tfrac1{n-1}$ mix: options mein dono values hon to question ka wording dekho.
  • Non-unit direction; $\operatorname{Var}(az)=a\operatorname{Var}(z)$ likhna.

P3 · Covariance and the covariance matrix

A. Core idea

Covariance batata hai do features saath-saath kaise badalte hain: dono ek saath mean se upar/neeche ⇒ positive; ek upar jab doosra neeche ⇒ negative; koi pattern nahi ⇒ ~0. Covariance matrix $C$ mein saare pairs ek jagah: diagonal par har feature ka variance, off-diagonal par covariances. Sabse badi baat: kisi bhi direction ka variance is ek matrix se nikal aata hai.

Analogy: $C$ data ka "spread ka nakshaa" hai — har direction ka spread usmein encoded hai, jaise ek map se kisi bhi do jagah ki doori nikal sakte ho. Kahan toot-ti hai: $C$ sirf second-order (linear) spread pakadta hai; curved patterns (circle par pade points) uske liye "invisible" hain — isiliye aage Kernel PCA (T3) aata hai.

What it is NOT: correlation nahi (correlation = covariance ÷ dono std, hamesha $[-1,1]$ mein). Aur zero covariance ⇒ independence nahi; sirf "linear relation nahi".

B. Notation and operations (Formula Protocol)

$$C=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\tilde x_i\tilde x_i^T=\frac1n\tilde X\tilde X^T=\frac1nXX^T-\mu\mu^T,\qquad C_{jk}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\big(x_i[j]-\mu[j]\big)\big(x_i[k]-\mu[k]\big)$$ $$\operatorname{Var}_u=\frac1n\sum_i(u^T\tilde x_i)^2=u^T\Big(\frac1n\sum_i\tilde x_i\tilde x_i^T\Big)u=u^TCu,\qquad \operatorname{tr}(C)=\frac1n\sum_i\|\tilde x_i\|^2$$
  • $C\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ (features × features). $x_i[j]$ = point $i$ ka $j$-th feature. $\tilde X$ = centred data ($d\times n$).
  • Pehli line ka teesra form ("shortcut") uncentred $X$ se kaam karta hai: second moment minus mean ka outer product.
  • Doosri line: $(u^T\tilde x)^2=u^T\tilde x\,\tilde x^Tu$ (ek number ka square = number × number, aur $u^T\tilde x=\tilde x^Tu$). Phir average andar le jao.

Meaning in words: $C$ = har centred point ke outer product ka average. Notice that (1) $C^T=C$ (symmetric) kyunki $(\tilde x\tilde x^T)^T=\tilde x\tilde x^T$; (2) $u^TCu=\operatorname{Var}_u\ge0$ har $u$ ke liye — yeh property positive semi-definite (PSD) kehlati hai; (3) trace = diagonal ka sum = har feature ke variance ka sum = total variance. Important insight: PCA ka objective "max $\operatorname{Var}_u$ over unit $u$" ab "max $u^TCu$ over $\|u\|=1$" ban gaya — ek pure matrix problem. This is why PCA ke saare PYQs pehle $C$ banwate hain.

C. Worked examples

Example 1 (simple) — running data. Centred points $(2,2),(-2,-2),(1,-1),(-1,1)$. $C$ outer products se?

Step 1: $(2,2)(2,2)^T=\begin{bmatrix}4&4\\4&4\end{bmatrix}$; $(-2,-2)$ ka bhi same (minus × minus = plus).

Step 2: $(1,-1)(1,-1)^T=\begin{bmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{bmatrix}$; $(-1,1)$ ka bhi same.

Step 3: sum $=\begin{bmatrix}4+4+1+1&4+4-1-1\\4+4-1-1&4+4+1+1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}10&6\\6&10\end{bmatrix}$. Step 4: $\div n=4$: $C=\begin{bmatrix}2.5&1.5\\1.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}$.

Check: $C_{11}=2.5$ = P2 Example 2(c) ✓; $\operatorname{tr}C=5=\tfrac{8+8+2+2}{4}$ ✓. Shortcut se: $\tfrac1nXX^T=\begin{bmatrix}11.5&4.5\\4.5&3.5\end{bmatrix}$, $\mu\mu^T=\begin{bmatrix}9&3\\3&1\end{bmatrix}$, difference $=\begin{bmatrix}2.5&1.5\\1.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}$ ✓.

Example 3 (tricky) — kya yeh covariance matrix ho sakta hai? $B=\begin{bmatrix}4&3\\3&1\end{bmatrix}$.

Symmetric hai, diagonal positive hai — lagta hai theek. Par direction $u=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$ lo: $u^TBu=\tfrac12\big(4-3-3+1\big)=\tfrac12(-1)=-0.5$. Variance negative nahi ho sakta ⇒ B covariance matrix nahi hai. Quick test (2×2): $\det B=4-9=-5<0$ ⇒ ek eigenvalue negative ⇒ PSD nahi.

Non-example: $\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$ — symmetric hi nahi, isliye covariance matrix nahi ho sakta (har covariance matrix symmetric hota hai: $C_{jk}=C_{kj}$).

D. Common mistakes

  • Centre kiye bina $\tfrac1nXX^T$ ko covariance bolna (data centred na ho to galat).
  • $\tfrac1n\tilde X^T\tilde X$ ($n\times n$) likh dena — woh Gram matrix hai, covariance nahi (B12).
  • Total variance = saari entries ka sum — galat; sirf diagonal (trace).
  • Off-diagonal sign galti — minus × minus = plus.
  • Diagonal positive + symmetric ⇒ valid covariance maan lena (Example 3).

E. Why PCA needs this — ML bridge

Q: PCA PYQs mein "eigenvalues of the covariance matrix" itna kyun aata hai?
A: Kyunki $C$ ke eigenvalues hi har PC ka variance hain, aur unka sum $\operatorname{tr}C$ = total variance. Isliye "total variance" aur "$\lambda_1$" ko mix karna ek classic trap hai — source key mein bhi aisi galti flagged hai (F7, B6).

Q: Uncentred matrix ka top eigenvector kahan jaata hai?
A: Running data ke $\tfrac1nXX^T=\begin{bmatrix}11.5&4.5\\4.5&3.5\end{bmatrix}$ ka top eigenvector lagbhag $(0.912,\,0.410)$ hai (≈ 24°) — mean $(3,1)$ ki direction (≈ 18.4°) ke paas, jabki asli spread 45° par hai. Centring isliye zaroori hai.

P4 · Eigenvalues and eigenvectors

A. Core idea

Matrix $A$ vectors ko transform karta hai — aam taur par ghumata bhi hai aur khinchta bhi hai. Kuch special directions aisi hoti hain jinhe $A$ ghumata nahi, sirf scale karta hai: woh eigenvectors hain, aur scale factor eigenvalue. Neeche figure mein $e_1=(1,0)$ ghoom jaata hai, par $q_1$ apni line par hi rehta hai.

Analogy: rubber sheet ko do taraf se khincho. Zyada-tar lines tirchi ho jaati hain, par khinchne ki dishaon wali lines sirf lambi hoti hain. Kahan toot-ti hai: eigenvalue negative ho to direction ulat jaati hai (same line, ulta arrow), aur rotation matrix ki koi real eigen-direction hoti hi nahi.

What it is NOT: eigenvector unique nahi hota — koi bhi nonzero multiple bhi eigenvector hai (sign bhi flip ho sakta hai). Aur eigenvalues general matrix ki diagonal entries nahi hote (sirf triangular/diagonal matrix mein).

B. Notation and operations (Formula Protocol)

$$Av=\lambda v,\ v\neq\mathbf 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad(A-\lambda I)v=\mathbf 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\det(A-\lambda I)=0$$ $$\text{2×2: }\ \lambda^2-(\operatorname{tr}A)\,\lambda+\det A=0,\qquad \lambda_1+\lambda_2=\operatorname{tr}A,\qquad \lambda_1\lambda_2=\det A$$
  • $A\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ square matrix. $\lambda$ = scalar (eigenvalue). $v$ = nonzero vector (eigenvector). $I$ = identity.
  • Kyun determinant? $(A-\lambda I)v=\mathbf 0$ ka nonzero solution tabhi hota hai jab $A-\lambda I$ singular ho, yaani $\det=0$.
  • 2×2 formula: $\det\begin{bmatrix}a-\lambda&b\\c&d-\lambda\end{bmatrix}=(a-\lambda)(d-\lambda)-bc=\lambda^2-(a+d)\lambda+(ad-bc)$ — yahi G2 ka quadratic hai.

Mechanism (⚙️): (1) trace aur det nikalo. (2) quadratic solve karo → $\lambda$'s. (3) har $\lambda$ ke liye $(A-\lambda I)v=0$ ki koi ek row lo, $v$ choose karo. (4) zarurat ho to normalise: $v/\|v\|$. (5) check: $Av$ multiply karke dekho $=\lambda v$.

Notice that $A$ ki jagah $A+cI$ lo to har eigenvalue $c$ se shift hota hai, eigenvectors same; $kA$ ke eigenvalues $k\lambda$; $A^2$ ke $\lambda^2$; $A^{-1}$ ke $1/\lambda$. Important insight: $\lambda=0$ ek eigenvalue hai ⇔ $\det A=0$ ⇔ $A$ singular. This is why covariance ka zero eigenvalue = "us direction mein data bilkul nahi faila" (P3 Example 2).

C. Worked examples

Example 1 (simple) — running $C$. $C=\begin{bmatrix}2.5&1.5\\1.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}$ ke eigen-pairs.

Step 1: $\operatorname{tr}C=5$, $\det C=2.5^2-1.5^2=6.25-2.25=4$.

Step 2: $\lambda^2-5\lambda+4=0$ ⇒ $\lambda=\tfrac{5\pm3}{2}$ ⇒ $\lambda_1=4,\ \lambda_2=1$.

Step 3 ($\lambda_1=4$): $C-4I=\begin{bmatrix}-1.5&1.5\\1.5&-1.5\end{bmatrix}$; first row: $-1.5v_1+1.5v_2=0$ ⇒ $v_1=v_2$ ⇒ $v=(1,1)$, unit $q_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$.

Step 4 ($\lambda_2=1$): $C-I=\begin{bmatrix}1.5&1.5\\1.5&1.5\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ $v_1=-v_2$ ⇒ $q_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$.

Step 5 (check): $C(1,1)^T=(2.5+1.5,\ 1.5+2.5)=(4,4)=4\cdot(1,1)$ ✓; $C(1,-1)^T=(1,-1)$ ✓. Yeh wahi 4 aur 1 hain jo P2 Example 2 mein variances aaye the.

$A=\begin{bmatrix}4&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$. Eigenvalues aur unit eigenvectors? $A$ ke rank ke baare mein kya kehte ho?

General: characteristic equation

$\operatorname{tr}A=5$, $\det A=4\cdot1-2\cdot2=0$. $\lambda^2-5\lambda=0$ ⇒ $\lambda(\lambda-5)=0$ ⇒ $\lambda_1=5,\ \lambda_2=0$.

$\lambda=5$: $A-5I=\begin{bmatrix}-1&2\\2&-4\end{bmatrix}$ ⇒ $-v_1+2v_2=0$ ⇒ $v=(2,1)$, unit $\tfrac1{\sqrt5}(2,1)$.

$\lambda=0$: $A v=0$ ⇒ $4v_1+2v_2=0$ ⇒ $v=(1,-2)$, unit $\tfrac1{\sqrt5}(1,-2)$.

Shortcut: rank-one structure

Notice: $A=\begin{bmatrix}2\\1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}2&1\end{bmatrix}=aa^T$ with $a=(2,1)$.

$Aa=a(a^Ta)=5a$ ⇒ $\lambda=5$, eigenvector $a$.

Koi bhi $v\perp a$: $Av=a(a^Tv)=0$ ⇒ $\lambda=0$, eigenvector $(1,-2)$.

Check: $A(2,1)^T=(8+2,\ 4+1)=(10,5)=5(2,1)$ ✓; $A(1,-2)^T=(4-4,\ 2-2)=(0,0)$ ✓; $5+0=\operatorname{tr}$, $5\cdot0=\det$ ✓. Rank 1 (ek hi nonzero eigenvalue). Rule: $aa^T$ ka nonzero eigenvalue $=\|a\|^2$, eigenvector $a$ — PCA PYQs mein "single point / ek outer product" wale sawaal isi se 10 second mein hote hain.

Example 3 (tricky) — eigenvalues bina dobara solve kiye. $C$ ke eigenvalues 4 aur 1 hain (Example 1). $C+2I$, $3C$, $C^2$, $C^{-1}$ ke eigenvalues?

$Cq=\lambda q$ se: $(C+2I)q=(\lambda+2)q$ ⇒ $6,3$. $(3C)q=3\lambda q$ ⇒ $12,3$. $C^2q=C(\lambda q)=\lambda^2q$ ⇒ $16,1$. $C^{-1}q=\tfrac1\lambda q$ ⇒ $\tfrac14,1$. Eigenvectors sab mein same ($q_1,q_2$).

Non-example: rotation $R=\begin{bmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{bmatrix}$ (90°): $\lambda^2-0\cdot\lambda+1=0$ ⇒ $\lambda^2=-1$ — koi real eigenvalue nahi, kyunki har direction ghoom jaati hai. Symmetric matrices ke saath aisa kabhi nahi hota (P5).

D. Common mistakes

  • $\det(A)-\lambda$ likhna — sahi hai $\det(A-\lambda I)$ ($\lambda$ sirf diagonal se ghatta hai).
  • Characteristic polynomial mein trace ka sign: $\lambda^2\mathbf{-}(\operatorname{tr})\lambda+\det$.
  • Zero vector ko eigenvector likhna; ya do dependent rows dekh ke ghabra jaana (singular hona hi to chahiye).
  • Answer mein eigenvector ka sign/scale alag hai to galat samajh lena — $(1,1)$, $(-2,-2)$, $\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ sab same direction.
  • Normalise bhool jaana jab question "unit eigenvector" ya PC coordinate maange.

E. Why PCA needs this — ML bridge

Q: PCA ka answer eigenvector kyun hai?
A: Max $u^TCu$ subject to $u^Tu=1$ — Lagrangian $u^TCu-\lambda(u^Tu-1)$ ka gradient $2Cu-2\lambda u=0$ ⇒ $Cu=\lambda u$. Har stationary direction eigenvector hai, aur wahan $u^TCu=\lambda u^Tu=\lambda$. Isliye maximum = sabse bada eigenvalue.

Q: "Eigenvalues of $C+\sigma^2I$" type PYQ ka first move?
A: Example 3 ka shift rule — dobara solve mat karo.

P5 · Symmetric matrices, PSD, and eigen-decomposition (spectral theorem)

A. Core idea

Symmetric matrix ($A^T=A$) ke saath teen guarantees milti hain: (1) saare eigenvalues real; (2) eigenvectors ek doosre ke perpendicular chune ja sakte hain; (3) isliye $A$ ko "rotate → axes par stretch → wapas rotate" ki tarah likh sakte hain. Covariance matrix hamesha symmetric aur PSD hota hai, isliye PCA ko ye teeno guarantees free mein milti hain: PCs orthonormal hain aur variances $\ge0$.

Analogy: ellipse (upar figure) — uske axes hamesha ek doosre ke perpendicular. Symmetric matrix = ek "seedha" ellipse, sirf tirchha rakha hua. Kahan toot-ti hai: agar do eigenvalues barabar hon to ellipse circle ban jaata hai — tab koi "special" axis nahi, har direction eigenvector hai.

What it is NOT: symmetric hone se PSD nahi ho jaata ($\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\2&1\end{bmatrix}$ ke eigenvalues $3,-1$). Aur "orthogonal eigenvectors" har matrix ki property nahi — sirf symmetric (real case) ki.

B. Notation and operations (Formula Protocol)

$$A=Q\Lambda Q^T=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\lambda_k\,q_kq_k^T,\qquad Q=[\,q_1\ \cdots\ q_d\,],\ \ Q^TQ=I,\ \ \Lambda=\operatorname{diag}(\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_d)$$ $$\text{For unit }u:\quad u^TAu=\sum_{k=1}^d\lambda_k\,(q_k^Tu)^2,\qquad \sum_k(q_k^Tu)^2=1\ \ \Longrightarrow\ \ \lambda_{\min}\le u^TAu\le\lambda_{\max}$$
  • $A$ = symmetric $d\times d$. $q_k$ = unit, mutually orthogonal eigenvectors (orthonormal). $Q^TQ=I$ ka matlab $Q^{-1}=Q^T$.
  • $\lambda_1\ge\lambda_2\ge\cdots$ (PCA convention: bade se chhote). PSD ⇔ $u^TAu\ge0\ \forall u$ ⇔ saare $\lambda_k\ge0$.
  • Doosri line: $u$ ko orthonormal basis $\{q_k\}$ mein likho, $u=\sum_k(q_k^Tu)q_k$; weights $(q_k^Tu)^2$ ka sum $\|u\|^2=1$ (Pythagoras in $d$ dims).

Meaning in words: $u^TAu$ eigenvalues ka weighted average hai, weights = $u$ ka har eigen-direction ke saath "overlap²". Average kabhi sabse bade number se zyada nahi hota ⇒ max $=\lambda_1$, tab jab saara weight $q_1$ par ho, yaani $u=q_1$. Notice that yeh Lagrange (P4-E) ke bina, sirf averaging se PCA ka result de deta hai. Important insight: $q_1$ ke perpendicular directions mein max $\lambda_2$ (weight $q_1$ par 0) — isliye PC2 = $q_2$. This is why PCA = covariance ka spectral decomposition: PCs $=q_k$, variance along PC $k$ $=\lambda_k$, total $=\sum\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}C$.

C. Worked examples

Example 1 (simple) — running $C$ ko jodkar wapas banao. $\lambda_1=4,\ q_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$; $\lambda_2=1,\ q_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$.

$q_1q_1^T=\tfrac12\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\1&1\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&0.5\\0.5&0.5\end{bmatrix}$; $q_2q_2^T=\begin{bmatrix}0.5&-0.5\\-0.5&0.5\end{bmatrix}$.

$4q_1q_1^T+1\cdot q_2q_2^T=\begin{bmatrix}2+0.5&2-0.5\\2-0.5&2+0.5\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}2.5&1.5\\1.5&2.5\end{bmatrix}=C$ ✓. Orthogonality: $q_1^Tq_2=\tfrac12(1-1)=0$ ✓.

Example 3 (tricky) — Rayleigh weights. Same $A=\begin{bmatrix}2&2\\2&5\end{bmatrix}$. (i) $u^TAu$ ka max/min over unit $u$? (ii) $u=e_1=(1,0)$ par value aur weights?

(i) Max $=\lambda_1=6$ at $u=\pm q_1$; min $=\lambda_2=1$ at $u=\pm q_2$.

(ii) Direct: $e_1^TAe_1=A_{11}=2$. Weights: $(q_1^Te_1)^2=\tfrac15=0.2$, $(q_2^Te_1)^2=\tfrac45=0.8$ (sum 1 ✓). Weighted average: $0.2\cdot6+0.8\cdot1=1.2+0.8=2$ ✓. Isi tarah $e_2$: $0.8\cdot6+0.2\cdot1=5=A_{22}$ ✓. Rule: har diagonal entry $\lambda_{\min}$ aur $\lambda_{\max}$ ke beech hoti hai.

Non-example: $\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\0&2\end{bmatrix}$ (not symmetric): $\lambda=2$ do baar, par $(A-2I)v=0$ sirf $v\propto(1,0)$ deta hai — doosri independent eigen-direction hai hi nahi, isliye $Q\Lambda Q^T$ form possible nahi. Symmetric matrix ke saath yeh kabhi nahi hota.

D. Common mistakes

  • Eigenvectors normalise kiye bina $Q\Lambda Q^T$ / $\sum\lambda_kq_kq_k^T$ likhna.
  • $Q^{-1}=Q^T$ har $Q$ ke liye maan lena — sirf orthonormal columns par.
  • Repeated eigenvalue par "eigenvector unique hai" maan lena: $C=cI$ (isotropic data) mein har unit direction PC hai — PC "not unique" wale PYQ options isi se judge hote hain.
  • Symmetric ⇒ PSD maan lena (non-example $3,-1$).
  • Eigenvalues ko descending order mein na rakhna, phir PC1/PC2 ulta.

E. Why PCA needs this — ML bridge

Q: PCs ek doosre ke perpendicular kyun hote hain?
A: Kyunki woh symmetric $C$ ke eigenvectors hain (proof neeche Deeper mein). 2.2 (B7) ke "orthogonality" PYQs yahi check karte hain: do candidate PCs ka dot product $0$ hona chahiye.

Q: Running data ka PCA ka poora jawab?
A: $C=4q_1q_1^T+1\cdot q_2q_2^T$ ⇒ PC1 $=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$ (variance 4), PC2 $=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,-1)$ (variance 1), PC1 explains $\tfrac45=80\%$. 🔬 2. Deeper insight — proofs and links (D3)

Proof 1: symmetric ⇒ eigenvectors of different eigenvalues are orthogonal

Maan lo $Av=\lambda v$, $Aw=\mu w$, $\lambda\neq\mu$, aur $A^T=A$. Number $v^TAw$ ko do tarah se likho:

(i) $v^T(Aw)=v^T(\mu w)=\mu\,v^Tw$. (ii) $v^TAw=(A^Tv)^Tw=(Av)^Tw=\lambda\,v^Tw$ (yahan $A^T=A$ use hua).

Barabar karo: $\lambda v^Tw=\mu v^Tw$ ⇒ $(\lambda-\mu)v^Tw=0$. $\lambda\neq\mu$ ⇒ $v^Tw=0$. ∎ (Equal eigenvalues ke andar orthogonal basis choose karna hamesha possible hai — yeh spectral theorem ka baaki hissa hai.)

Proof 2: variance maximisation ⇔ reconstruction-error minimisation (B3 → PCA)

Unit $u$, centred $\tilde x_i$. B3 se, har point ke liye: $\|\tilde x_i-(u^T\tilde x_i)u\|^2=\|\tilde x_i\|^2-(u^T\tilde x_i)^2$.

Average lo: $\underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_i\|\tilde x_i-(u^T\tilde x_i)u\|^2}_{\text{avg error}}=\underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_i\|\tilde x_i\|^2}_{\operatorname{tr}C}-\underbrace{\tfrac1n\sum_i(u^T\tilde x_i)^2}_{u^TCu}$.

$\operatorname{tr}C$ fixed hai ($u$ par depend nahi). Isliye error min ⇔ $u^TCu$ max. Running data: har angle par error + variance $=5$ (widget table). Best error $=5-4=1=\lambda_2$ — yaani 1-D PCA ka avg reconstruction error = baaki eigenvalues ka sum. B11 ka core result yahi hai.

Edge cases worth knowing before B5
  • Repeated eigenvalue: $C=\begin{bmatrix}3&0\\0&3\end{bmatrix}$ — har unit $u$ par $u^TCu=3$. PC1 "unique" nahi; koi bhi orthonormal pair chalega.
  • Zero eigenvalue: points ek lower-dimensional subspace mein (P3 Example 2: sab ek line par). PCA ek dimension bina kisi error ke gira sakta hai.
  • Sign ambiguity: $q$ aur $-q$ dono valid PCs; coordinates ka sign flip ho jaata hai. MCQ mein options ka sign alag ho to dono accept-worthy ho sakte hain — question ka wording dekho.
  • $d>n$: $C$ ($d\times d$) ka rank $\le n-1$ (centring ek dimension kha jaati hai), isliye kam se kam $d-n+1$ eigenvalues zero. Dual PCA (B12) isi wajah se $n\times n$ matrix use karta hai. Running data: $\tilde X\tilde X^T$ ke eigenvalues $16,4$; $\tilde X^T\tilde X$ ($4\times4$) ke $16,4,0,0$ — nonzero same.
  • Scale ka asar: $\tfrac1n$ vs $\tfrac1{n-1}$ eigenvalues ko $\tfrac{n}{n-1}$ se scale karta hai, explained-variance ratios same rehte hain.

📌 4. Formula card — PCA prerequisites

Backbone chain: data $\to\mu\to\tilde x_i=x_i-\mu\to C=\tfrac1n\sum\tilde x_i\tilde x_i^T\to\operatorname{Var}_u=u^TCu\to C=Q\Lambda Q^T\to$ PC$_k=q_k$, variance $\lambda_k$, total $\operatorname{tr}C$.

2×2 eigen in 3 lines: $\lambda^2-(\operatorname{tr})\lambda+\det=0$ · row of $A-\lambda I$ ⇒ $v$ · check $Av=\lambda v$.

  • $\operatorname{Var}(az+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(z)$; $\operatorname{Var}=\overline{z^2}-\bar z^2$
  • $C=\tfrac1nXX^T-\mu\mu^T$; symmetric, PSD
  • $\sum\lambda_k=\operatorname{tr}$, $\prod\lambda_k=\det$
  • $A+cI\to\lambda+c$; $kA\to k\lambda$; $A^{-1}\to1/\lambda$
  • $aa^T$: $\lambda=\|a\|^2$ (vec $a$), rest $0$
  • $\lambda_{\min}\le u^TAu\le\lambda_{\max}$ ($\|u\|=1$)

Top traps: (1) centre karna bhoolna (uncentred matrix); (2) total variance vs $\lambda_1$ mix; (3) non-unit direction / un-normalised eigenvectors; (4) $\tfrac1n$ vs $\tfrac1{n-1}$; (5) eigenvector ka sign/scale alag dekh ke sahi option reject karna.
