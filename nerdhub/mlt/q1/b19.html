📘 4.4 Objective, monotonicity, convergence — theory

🔁 Recall warm-up [ADDED]

Pehle khud likho, phir "Show answer" kholo. (B17, B18, B18 se.)

R1 (B17). Ek cluster ke points $x_i$ aur koi bhi $v$ ke liye $\sum_i\|x_i-v\|^2$ ko cluster ke mean $m$ ke terms mein likho.

Show answer

$\sum_i\|x_i-v\|^2=\sum_i\|x_i-m\|^2+n\,\|m-v\|^2$ (cross-term $2\sum_i(x_i-m)^T(m-v)=0$ kyunki $\sum_i(x_i-m)=0$). Isliye $v=m$ sabse achha hai — yahi aaj ka L19 banega.

R2 (B18). Lloyd ($k=2$) concentric rings ko do clusters kyun nahi de sakta?

Show answer

(a) Final cells convex/line-separable hote hain (L15) — ring ko ring se koi seedhi line alag nahi karti; (b) symmetric rings ke centroid ek hi jagah (origin) par aate hain, to distances barabar ho jaate hain, assignment tik nahi sakti.

R3 (B18, 4.6). $F^*(k)$ ($k$ clusters ka best possible $F$) $k$ badhane par kaisa badalta hai, aur $k=n$ par kya?

Show answer

Non-increasing: $F^*(k+1)\le F^*(k)$; $k=n$ (ya $k\ge$ distinct points) par $F^*=0$. Yeh P7 ka poora jawab hai — par isi page par proof ke saath dobara aayega.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

Do dost ek hi score ke liye do alag knobs ghuma rahe hain. Pehla dost (assign) apna knob $z$ ghumata hai: dusre ke setting ($\mu$) ko jaisa hai waisa maan kar, apne liye sabse achha setting chunta hai. Dusra dost (update) $\mu$ ghumata hai: $z$ ko jaisa hai waisa maan kar apna sabse achha setting (mean) chunta hai. Score $F$ kam rakhna dono ka common goal hai, isliye har baari ya to score girta hai ya wahi rehta hai — kabhi badhta nahi. Score $\ge0$ hai aur $z$ ke possible settings ginti ke hain, isliye aakhir mein dono ke liye "ab koi sudhaar nahi" ki sthiti aa jaati hai — wahi convergence.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) asli knobs continuous hote hain; yahan $z$ discrete hai (partition), isliye "dheere dheere neeche" nahi — ek chhalaang mein ek partition se doosre par; zameen mein bahut gaddhe (local minima) hain. (2) Dono dost ek doosre ka agla kadam jaante hain? Nahi — har ek sirf abhi ka setting dekhta hai; isiliye ruk jaane par "dono ki best response ek doosre par" bani hoti hai, jo ek fixed point hai (L17), sabse neeche ka gaddha nahi. (3) Asli duniya mein ek dost aakhir mein sabse neeche pahunch sakta hai; yahan koi guarantee nahi.

Yeh kya NAHI hai: (a) Gradient descent nahi — koi learning rate ya step size nahi; har kadam apne block ka exact minimiser hai. (b) "$F$ girna" $\neq$ "$F$ zero ya global minimum tak girna". (c) "$F$ nahi badhta" $\neq$ "$F$ har kadam par strictly girta hai" — ties par assign step mein $F$ wahi reh sakta hai (neeche WE3).

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

$$F(z,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|x_i-\mu_{z_i}\|^2,\qquad z_i^{t+1}=\arg\min_k\|x_i-\mu_k^t\|^2,\qquad \mu_k^{t+1}=\frac1{n_k}\sum_{i:\,z_i^{t+1}=k}x_i.$$

Symbols aur assumptions: $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$; $z=(z_1,\dots,z_n)$ labels, $z_i\in\{1,\dots,K\}$; $\mu=(\mu_1,\dots,\mu_K)$ centres; $n_k=|C_k|$ cluster $k$ ka size; Euclidean norm. Assumptions: har cluster non-empty (bank ke sawaalon mein yahi hota hai; khaali cluster ki convention B17/B18 jaisi); tie ho to ek fixed deterministic rule (jaise "chhota index").

Words mein: $F$ = har point ki apne centre se doori$^2$ ka jod. Do-kadam loop: (i) $\mu$ pakad kar $z$ chuno, (ii) $z$ pakad kar $\mu$ chuno.

Notice that… $F$ ke do argument hain. Alag-alag kadamon ke baad alag $F$ ki baat hoti hai, isliye notation dhyaan se:

Step A — assign step $F$ ko nahi badhata

Fix $i$. $z_i^{t+1}$ woh $k$ hai jo $\|x_i-\mu_k^t\|^2$ ko sabse chhota karta hai, aur $z_i^{t}$ bhi un $K$ options mein se ek hai. Isliye $\|x_i-\mu_{z_i^{t+1}}^t\|^2\le\|x_i-\mu_{z_i^t}^t\|^2$ har $i$ ke liye. Sab $i$ par jodo: $F(z^{t+1},\mu^t)\le F(z^t,\mu^t)$.

Step B — update step $F$ ko nahi badhata, aur kitna ghatata hai (L19)

Cluster $C$ ke mean $m$ aur koi bhi $v$ ke liye: $\|x-v\|^2=\|(x-m)+(m-v)\|^2=\|x-m\|^2+2(x-m)^T(m-v)+\|m-v\|^2$. $C$ par jodo; $\sum_{x\in C}(x-m)=0$ hone se beech wala term gayab: $\sum_{x\in C}\|x-v\|^2=\sum_{x\in C}\|x-m\|^2+|C|\,\|m-v\|^2$. Ab $v=\mu_k^t$ (purana centre) aur $m=\mu_k^{t+1}$ (naya mean) lo, sab clusters par jodo:

$$F(z^{t+1},\mu^t)-F(z^{t+1},\mu^{t+1})=\sum_{k=1}^{K}n_k\,\|\mu_k^{t+1}-\mu_k^t\|^2\ \ge\ 0.\qquad\text{(L19)}$$

Barabari tab aur sirf tab jab har non-empty cluster ka mean uske purane centre ke barabar ho.

Step C — chain (L8) aur flat ⇒ frozen (L20)

A aur B ko jodo: $F(z^t,\mu^t)\ge F(z^{t+1},\mu^t)\ge F(z^{t+1},\mu^{t+1})$. Ab maan lo do lagataar full passes ke baad $F$ barabar hai: $F(z^{t+1},\mu^{t+1})=F(z^t,\mu^t)$. Tab chain ki dono $\ge$ barabari hain; doosri (L19) se $\sum n_k\|\mu_k^{t+1}-\mu_k^t\|^2=0$ ⇒ $\mu^{t+1}=\mu^t$. Tab agla assign wahi inputs par chalta hai: $z^{t+2}=\arg\min$ w.r.t. $\mu^{t+1}=\mu^t$, yani $z^{t+2}=z^{t+1}$ (deterministic rule), aur $\mu^{t+2}$ = unhi clusters ke means = $\mu^{t+1}$. Induction se hamesha ke liye jam gaya. ∎ (Aur: pichhle pass par labels $z^{t+1}=z^t$ bhi, siwa ek tie-coincidence ke — jo exam data mein nahi hota; isliye "$F$ barabar hua" matlab pichhle pass par hi sab tay ho chuka tha.)

Step D — termination (L21)

Agar kabhi flat na ho to har pass par $F(z^{t},\mu^{t})$ strictly girta hai. Update ke baad $\mu^t$ poori tarah $z^t$ ka function hai (means), isliye $F(z^t,\mu^t)$ sirf partition $z^t$ par depend karta hai. Strictly girne ka matlab koi partition do baar nahi aata. Partitions ki ginti $\le K^n$ hai (finite), isliye flat pass aana hi hai — aur uske baad L20. Isliye Lloyd har initialization se rukta hai; kitni der mein — yeh data par depend karta hai (Visual 1: $K=2$ par 5 passes), $K$ par nahi.

Step E — ruk jaana ≠ optimum (L22)

Ruka hua state do conditions poori karta hai: (i) har point apne nearest centre par (assign $z$ ko nahi badalta), (ii) har centre apne cluster ka mean. Yahi L17 ka fixed point hai. $F^*(K)=\min_{z,\mu}F$ (poore search space par minimum) ko $F_{\text{Lloyd}}\ge F^*(K)$ hi guarantee hai. Barabari ki koi guarantee nahi (B18 ka rectangle: $F=16$ par ruka, jabki $F=1$ wala partition maujood).

Important insight: "$F$ non-increasing" aur "$F\ge0$" dono milkar sirf yeh dete hain ki $F$ ek limit par jaata hai. Loop ka rukna teesri cheez hai jo partitions ke finite hone se aati hai. Aur "flat $F$" ka matlab frozen — isliye F-series se convergence padh sakte hain, bina points dekhe (P3).

This is why P3 sirf numbers se hal ho jaata hai, P4 ke "may fail to converge" ko seedha kaat sakte hain, aur P7 mein "kaun sa $k$" ka sawaal $F$ ki monotonicity se aata hai ($k$ ke saath, iterations ke saath nahi).

⚙️ How it works — F-series se aur data se kaam karna

  1. Data se: init centres ke saath assign table banao ($d^2$ har centre tak), tie ho to chhota index; $F(z^{1},\mu^0)$ likho.
  2. Update: har cluster ka mean; $F(z^1,\mu^1)$ likho. L19 se check: drop $=\sum n_k\|\Delta\mu_k\|^2$.
  3. Dobara assign; agar kuch nahi badla to ruko — yeh confirming pass hai (convention B ise ginti mein leta hai, A nahi).
  4. $F$-series se: do lagataar barabar values dhoondo; pehli barabar value jahan se shuru hoti hai, wahi convention A ki convergence iteration; agli iteration convention B ki.
  5. Impossible series pehchano: koi rise ($F$ badha) ya "barabar phir ghata" — dono Lloyd mein ho hi nahi sakte (L8, L20).
  6. $k$ badalne par: $F^*(k)$ non-increasing; $k\ge$ distinct points par $0$.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — edge cases, proofs, connections

1. Strict decrease ke liye kya chahiye

Pass par $F(z^{t+1},\mu^{t+1})<F(z^t,\mu^t)$ ⟺ ya to assign step mein koi point strictly paas wale centre par gaya, ya update step mein koi centre khisaka ($\Delta\mu\ne0$). Tie-move sirf assign mein $F$ ko wahi rakhta hai; par label badalne se cluster ka mean (aam taur par) khisakta hai, isliye update step $F$ ko strictly ghata deta hai. Exam jaisi generic data mein pass ke $F$ ki "barabari" sirf poore freeze par aati hai.

2. Kyun $K^n$ nahi, par phir bhi tez

$K^n$ partitions ki upper bound bahut dheeli hai; practice mein passes ki ginti chhoti rehti hai (6000 random chhote instances: confirming pass ke saath max 6 passes). Theory mein worst-case mein bahut zyada passes ke examples jaane-maane hain, par bank ka koi sawaal usse nahi poochhta.

3. $F(z,\mu)$ ek "biconvex"-type cheez hai

$\mu$ fix ho to $F$ $z$ ka ek simple function hai (har $i$ alag min); $z$ fix ho to $F$ $\mu$ ka convex quadratic (L19). Aise "dono blocks mein exact minimise" wale algorithms ko block coordinate descent kehte hain; unka common guarantee wahi hai — non-increasing + local (coordinate-wise) minimum, global nahi.

4. EM se connection (T7)

EM mein $F$ ki jagah log-likelihood hai (badhti hai), assign ki jagah soft E-step, update ki jagah M-step. Argument ka dhancha ek-sa: har kadam objective ko ek taraf hi chalata hai, isliye limit milti hai; aur wahan bhi local, global nahi. (B25–B26.)

5. Khaali cluster

Agar assign step ke baad koi cluster khaali ho jaye to mean undefined; convention (centre wahi rakho) se proof ki L19 mein us $k$ ka term $0$ rehta hai ($n_k=0$). Bank mein koi sawaal nahi.

🎯 P7 — 1000 points: kaun sa $k$ minimum $F$ de (2026 July 16) [SRC]

16 July 2026 · Original Q5 · ID 6406532158938 · MCQ · 3 marks · Pages 6–7 · [SRC]

A dataset containing 1,000 points is clustered using the K-means algorithm. Which value of $k$ is most likely to yield the minimum value of the K-means objective function?

  1. $k=1$
  2. $k=10$
  3. $k=100$
  4. $k=1000$ [correct]

✍️ Try first

Agar har point apna alag cluster ho, $F$ kya hoga? Aur $k$ badhane par best possible $F$ ke baare mein kya kah sakte ho? Confidence: S / U / G?

Show solution

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: kaun sa $k$ $F$ ko sabse chhota karta hai. Given: $n=1000$, $k\in\{1,10,100,1000\}$.
  • Trigger clues: "minimum value of objective" aur $k$ alag-alag ⇒ $F^*(k)$ ka $k$ ke saath behaviour (4.6 ka theory + L8).
  • Prerequisites: S2, B18 ka $F^*(k)$. PACER: Conceptual. Principle: $F^*(k)$ non-increasing, $F^*(n)=0$.

2. 🧭 Methods (2 genuinely different)

M1 Proof: $F^*(k)$ ko "centres ke set par min" likho aur dikhao ki ek centre jodne se $F$ nahi badhta. M2 Boundary cases: $k=1$ (sabse bada) aur $k=n$ ($F=0$) ko dekho; beech ke sab beech mein.

3. 🧮 Working

M1 · monotonicity ka proof

Diye hue centres $M=\{\mu_1,\dots,\mu_k\}$ ke liye best labels nearest centre hain, isliye $F^*(k)=\min_{|M|=k}\sum_i\min_{\mu\in M}\|x_i-\mu\|^2$.

Ab $M'=M\cup\{\mu_{k+1}\}$: har point ke paas pehle jitne options the, ab unse $\ge$ options. Har $\min$ nahi badhta ⇒ $F^*(k+1)\le F^*(k)$.

$k=n$: $\mu_i=x_i$ lo ⇒ har point apne centre par ⇒ $F=0$; aur $F\ge0$ hamesha ⇒ $F^*(n)=0$ minimum possible.

Isliye $F^*(1)\ge F^*(10)\ge F^*(100)\ge F^*(1000)=0$ ⇒ $k=1000$ (option 4).

M2 · seema ke cases

$k=1$: ek hi cluster, centre = poore data ka mean, $F$ = total scatter $\sum\|x_i-\bar x\|^2$ — sabse bada (M1 ki monotonicity se).

$k=1000$: har point alag ⇒ $F=0$.

Beech ke $10$ aur $100$ in dono ke beech: $F(1)\ge F(10)\ge F(100)\ge0$. Sabse chhota $k=1000$ par (ya brabar, jab duplicates hon).

4. 📊 Comparison

MethodSpeedDifficultyError riskBest use
M1MediumMediumLowProof maangna ho
M2FastLowLow — "most likely" ko ignore na karoMCQ

⚡ FASTEST: M2 ($k=n\Rightarrow F=0$). 🏆 BEST EXAM METHOD: M2 plus ek line M1 ki; trade-off: M2 "kyun $F^*$ monotone hai" nahi batata, M1 batata hai.

Distractors: $k=1$ sabse bada $F$. $k=10$ aur $k=100$ beech ke hain; jab tak data mein $\le100$ distinct points na hon, inka $F>0$ rehta hai (nahi to $k=1000$ ke barabar ho sakte hain — isliye sawaal "most likely" kehta hai).

5. ✅ Verification

  • Code ([ADDED] synthetic data: 4 Gaussian blobs, $n=1000$, har $k$ par 5 Lloyd runs ka best): $F$ = $8586.3\ (k{=}1),\ 4603.0\ (2),\ 707.8\ (4),\ 382.3\ (10),\ 41.3\ (100),\ 0.0\ (1000)$ — monotone ✓, $k=1000$ par bilkul $0$.
  • Exact chhota check: $\{0,1,5\}$: $k=1$: mean $2$, $F=4+1+9=14$; $k=2$: $\{0,1\}\mid\{5\}$ ⇒ $F=0.5$; $k=3$ ⇒ $F=0$ — $14\ge0.5\ge0$ ✓.

6. ⚠️ Traps

  • "$k=1000$ overfitting hai, isliye galat" — sawaal clustering ki achhai nahi, sirf $F$ ka minimum poochh raha hai.
  • "$F$ minimise karke $k$ chuno" — kabhi nahi chalega ($F$ hamesha $k=n$ chunta hai); $k$ ke liye elbow (B18).
  • Lloyd ke local minima ki wajah se kisi $k$ par $F_{\text{Lloyd}}>F^*(k)$ ho sakta hai, par $k=n$ par $F=0$ pehle hi mil jaata hai.

Recognition rule: "kaun sa $k$ min $F$" ⇒ sabse bada $k$ (ya $\ge$ distinct points).
