R1 · Feature map — data ko "lift" karna

A. Core idea

1-D data socho: positive points $x=-1,0,1$ aur negative points $x=-3,-2,2,3$. Ek threshold ("$x>t$ to positive") number line ko sirf do half-lines mein todta hai — positives beech mein hain, to koi threshold kaam nahi karta. Par har $x$ ko $\phi(x)=(x,\,x^2)$ se plane mein bhejo: positives ke liye $x^2\le1$, negatives ke liye $x^2\ge4$. Ab ek seedhi line ($x^2=2.5$) dono ko alag kar deti hai. Feature map = naye coordinates banana taaki "non-linear" problem naye space mein "linear" ho jaaye.

B. Notation and operations

  • $\phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^D$ — input dimension $d$, feature-space dimension $D$ (aksar $D>d$; kabhi $D=\infty$).
  • $\phi(x)$ ke har coordinate ko feature kehte hain: $\phi(x)=(\phi_1(x),\dots,\phi_D(x))$. Example $\phi(x)=(x,x^2)$: $\phi_1=x$, $\phi_2=x^2$.
  • $\phi$ khud non-linear ho sakta hai ($x^2$); naye space mein model linear hota hai: $w^T\phi(x)+b$.

C. Worked examples

Ex 1 (1-D, upar wala): $\phi(-1)=(-1,1)$, $\phi(0)=(0,0)$, $\phi(1)=(1,1)$ (positives, $u_2\le1$); $\phi(\pm2)=(\pm2,4)$, $\phi(\pm3)=(\pm3,9)$ (negatives, $u_2\ge4$). Line $u_2=2.5$ alag karti hai ✓. (Neeche 3.1 ke Visual mein figure hai.)

Ex 2 (2-D circle): andar wale points $x_1^2+x_2^2<1$, bahar wale $>1$ — plane mein koi seedhi line nahi. $\phi(x)=(x_1^2,\,x_2^2)$ lo: condition $u_1+u_2<1$ ban jaati hai — seedhi line.

D. Common mistakes

  • "$\phi$ linear hona chahiye" — nahi; non-linear hone ka hi fayda hai. Linear $\phi$ (jaise $Ax$) se kuch naya nahi milta.
  • "$D$ hamesha $d$ se bada" — aksar, par zaroori nahi: P5 mein $\phi:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ hai ($D=d=1$).
  • Feature map ko PCA ka projection samajhna: PCA dimension ghatata hai; yahan aksar badhate hain.

E. Why kernels need this — ML bridge

Q: PYQ mein kahan? A: 3.1 ke sawaal $k$ dete hain aur $\phi$ poochhte hain (P1, P2, P3, P5) ya $\phi$ ka size poochhte hain (P4).

R2 · Kernel = feature space ka dot product (kernel trick)

A. Core idea

Do points $x,y$ ki "similarity" ko $\phi(x)$ aur $\phi(y)$ ke dot product se naapo: $k(x,y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$. Bahut algorithms (dual PCA, SVM, ...) data ko sirf dot products ke through dekhte hain. To $\phi$ banane ki jagah seedha $k(x,y)$ ka formula lagao — aur kaam wahi hota hai jaise data feature space mein ho. Yeh kernel trick hai.

B. Notation and operations

  • $k:\mathbb R^d\times\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R$; $k(x,y)=\sum_{j=1}^D\phi_j(x)\phi_j(y)$ — $D$ products ka jod, "$x$ ka feature $j$ × $y$ ka wahi feature $j$".
  • Symmetric: $k(x,y)=k(y,x)$. Aur $k(x,x)=\|\phi(x)\|^2\ge0$.
  • Example $\phi(x)=(x,x^2)$ (scalars): $k(x,y)=xy+x^2y^2$.

C. Worked example

$x=(1,2)$, $y=(3,1)$. $\phi(v)=(v_1^2,\sqrt2v_1v_2,v_2^2)$. Explicit: $\phi(x)=(1,\,2\sqrt2,\,4)$, $\phi(y)=(9,\,3\sqrt2,\,1)$ ⇒ $\phi(x)^T\phi(y)=9+12+4=25$ ($2\sqrt2\cdot3\sqrt2=12$). Kernel se: $(x^Ty)^2=5^2=25$ ✓. Ek hi answer; doosra raasta $\phi$ ke bina (code-verified).

D. Common mistakes

  • $\phi(x)\phi(y)$ ko numbers ka product samajhna — $\phi$ vector hai, yeh dot product hai.
  • $k$ ko distance samajhna — kernel similarity hai (Gaussian mein dikhta hai: paas = bada).
  • "Koi bhi function $k$ chalega" — nahi, $k$ kisi $\phi$ ka dot product hona chahiye (valid kernel; B14).

E. Why kernels need this — ML bridge

Q: Itna jhanjhat kyun? A: $d=100$, degree 3 ke liye $D=176\,851$ coordinates (R3) — par $k$ sirf ek 100-length dot product aur ek cube hai. Q: Dual PCA se rishta? A: B12 mein $K_{ij}=x_i^Tx_j$ aur $z(x)=\sum\alpha_jx^Tx_j$; $x^Tx_j\to k(x,x_j)$ karo to kernel PCA (B16).

R3 · Polynomial expand karna aur features ginna

A. Core idea

$(x^Ty)^2=(x_1y_1+x_2y_2)^2$ expand karo to $x_1^2y_1^2+2\,x_1x_2y_1y_2+x_2^2y_2^2$ milta hai. Har term "$x$ ka kuch × $y$ ka wahi kuch" hai — har term ek feature deta hai. Cross term ka coefficient $2$ hai; use $\sqrt2\cdot\sqrt2$ mein baant ke $\phi$ mein $\sqrt2\,x_1x_2$ rakhte hain. Aur features ginne ke liye: degree $\le m$ ke monomials $d$ variables mein $\binom{d+m}{m}$ hote hain.

B. Notation and operations

  • Monomial: $x_1^{a}x_2^{b}\cdots$ (powers non-negative integers); degree = powers ka jod.
  • Binomial: $(u+v)^m=\sum_{j=0}^m\binom mj u^{m-j}v^j$.
  • Count: degree exactly $m$ in $d$ variables $=\binom{d+m-1}{m}$; degree $\le m$ $=\binom{d+m}{m}$.

C. Worked examples

Ex 1: $d=2$, degree $\le2$: $1,\,x_1,\,x_2,\,x_1^2,\,x_1x_2,\,x_2^2$ — $6=\binom42$ ✓ (code-enumerated). Ex 2: $(1+x^Ty)^2$ expand:

$$1+2x_1y_1+2x_2y_2+x_1^2y_1^2+2x_1x_2y_1y_2+x_2^2y_2^2.$$

⇒ $\phi(x)=(1,\sqrt2x_1,\sqrt2x_2,x_1^2,\sqrt2x_1x_2,x_2^2)$, 6 coordinates, wahi $6$. Ex 3: $d=3$, $(x^Ty)^2$: squares $3$ + cross terms $\binom32=3$ = $6=\binom{4}{2}$.

D. Common mistakes

  • $\sqrt2$ bhool jaana: $\phi=(x_1^2,x_1x_2,x_2^2)$ se $x_1x_2y_1y_2$ ka coefficient $1$ aata, $2$ nahi.
  • Sirf degree exactly $m$ ginna jab $+1$ (ya $+c$) hai — tab saare degrees $0..m$ aate hain.
  • $\binom{d+m}{m}$ mein $d$ aur $m$ ulat dena ya $d+m-1$ lagana (woh homogeneous ka formula hai).

E. Why kernels need this — ML bridge

P1, P2, P3 mein expand karke $\phi$ padhna hai; P4 mein count; P6 mein formula. Yahi ek skill teeno ko cover karti hai.

R4 · Gaussian kernel

A. Core idea

Gaussian (RBF) kernel similarity ko distance se naapta hai: points paas hon to $k\approx1$, door hon to $k\approx0$. $\sigma$ batata hai "kitni door tak paas maana jaaye". Iska feature space infinite-dimensional hai (isliye koi finite $\phi$ nahi), par $k$ sasta hai.

B. Notation and operations

$$k(x,y)=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr),\qquad \sigma>0.$$
  • $$\|x-y\|^2=\sum_i(x_i-y_i)^2=\|x\|^2+\|y\|^2-2x^Ty.$$
  • $k(x,x)=e^0=1$; $0<k(x,y)\le1$; $k$ distance badhne par ghatta hai.
  • Denominator mein $2\sigma^2$ hai ($\sigma^2$ nahi, $\sigma$ nahi).

C. Worked examples

$x=(0,1)$, $y=(1,0)$: $\|x-y\|^2=1+1=2$. $\sigma=1$: $e^{-2/2}=e^{-1}=0.3679$. $\sigma=2$: $e^{-2/8}=e^{-0.25}=0.7788$. $\sigma=0.5$: $e^{-2/0.5}=e^{-4}=0.0183$. Chhota $\sigma$ ⇒ similarity jaldi gir jaati hai (code-verified).

D. Common mistakes

  • Norm par square bhoolna: $\|x-y\|=\sqrt2$ lena, $\|x-y\|^2=2$ nahi.
  • $2\sigma^2$ ki jagah $\sigma^2$ ya $\sigma$ — tab $e^{-2}=0.135$ ya $e^{-1.41}=0.243$ milta hai.
  • "Door = bada $k$" — ulta hai.

E. Why kernels need this — ML bridge

P7 seedha formula hai. Convention ka sabut key deti hai: $0.34$–$0.40$ sirf $2\sigma^2$ wale formula se aata hai (P7 mein dekhna). B14 mein "Gaussian valid kernel hai" ka reason aayega.

R5 · Kernel ki Gram matrix

A. Core idea

$n$ points $x_1,\dots,x_n$ aur kernel $k$ ho to $n\times n$ table $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$ banao — saare pairs ki similarity. Linear kernel $k=x^Ty$ ke liye yeh exactly B12 ki $X^TX$ hai. Kernel PCA (B16) yahi $K$ lekar B12 ka dual PCA chalata hai.

B. Notation and operations

  • $K$ symmetric ($k$ symmetric hai).
  • Gaussian ke liye diagonal $K_{ii}=1$.
  • Valid kernel ⇒ $K$ ke saare eigenvalues $\ge0$ (PSD) — preview; proof aur uska ulta B14 mein.

C. Worked examples

Linear: points $(1,0),(0,1),(1,1)$: $K=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&1\\0&1&1\\1&1&2\end{bmatrix}$, eigenvalues $0,1,3$. Gaussian $\sigma=1$: points $(0,0),(1,0),(0,2)$: $\|\cdot\|^2$ = $1,4,5$ ⇒ $K_{12}=e^{-1/2}=0.6065$, $K_{13}=e^{-2}=0.1353$, $K_{23}=e^{-5/2}=0.0821$.

$$K=\begin{bmatrix}1&0.6065&0.1353\\0.6065&1&0.0821\\0.1353&0.0821&1\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{eigenvalues }0.391,\ 0.966,\ 1.643.$$

Sab $\ge0$ ✓ (code-verified).

D. Common mistakes

  • $K$ ko $d\times d$ samajhna — $K$ $n\times n$ hai (points ki table), $XX^T$ ($d\times d$) alag cheez.
  • Gaussian ka diagonal $0$ likhna (woh distance ka hoga, similarity ka nahi — diagonal $1$).

E. Why kernels need this — ML bridge

B12 M2–M4 ka pura machine bas $K$ se chalta tha; B16 mein $K$ ko feature-space mein centre karke wahi chalayenge (PYQ 6406531302388).

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Kernel = "do cheezein kitni milti-julti hain" ka score. Jo score feature-space mein dot product hota hai, use hi $k(x,y)$ kehte hain.
  • Kernel ko padhna = dot product ko ulta padhna. $k$ ek jod hai: "$x$ ke features $\times$ $y$ ke wahi features". Har aisa jodi-term ek coordinate hai.
  • $\phi$ dhundhna ek puzzle hai: $k$ ko aise terms mein todo jahan $x$ wala hissa aur $y$ wala hissa bilkul same ho.
  • Ek hi $k$ ke kai $\phi$ ho sakte hain (coordinates ka order, sign, ghuma dena). Isliye sawaal mein "may correspond" likha hota hai.

Analogy [ADDED] — dono dost ki playlist match. Do logon ke music taste ko "kitne gaane dono ke common hain" se naapo: har gaana ek feature, aur score = common gaanon ki sankhya (ya unki pasand ke guna ka jod). $\phi(x)$ = tumhari playlist ka vector; $k(x,y)$ = dono playlists ka dot product.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Playlist mein features seedhe gaane hain; kernel mein features aksar naye banaye hue products hain ($x_1x_2$, $x_1^2x_2$), jinka koi "gaana" jaisa matlab nahi. (2) Playlist ke counts negative nahi hote; $\phi$ ke coordinates negative ho sakte hain, to $k$ negative bhi ho sakta hai (P-transfer mein $-15$ aayega). (3) Yahan $k$ ko hum bina playlists dekhe, formula se nikalte hain — analogy mein aisa shortcut nahi.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "$\phi$ unique hota hai" — nahi (N4). $Q\phi$ ($Q$ orthogonal), coordinates ka sign/order, kuch cases mein dimension bhi alag.
  • "$k$ seedha $\phi(x)\phi(y)$ ka number-product hai" — vector ka dot product.
  • "Koi bhi symmetric formula kernel hai" — nahi; $k(x,x)=\|\phi(x)\|^2\ge0$ chahiye (non-example neeche; poori kahani B14).

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

N1 (feature map).

$$\phi:\mathbb R^d\to\mathbb R^D,\qquad \phi(x)=\bigl(\phi_1(x),\dots,\phi_D(x)\bigr)^T.$$
  • Symbols: $d$ = input dimension; $D$ = feature dimension (finite ya $\infty$); $\phi_j$ = $j$-th feature function.

N2 (kernel).

$$k(x,y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)=\sum_{j=1}^{D}\phi_j(x)\,\phi_j(y).$$
  • Assumption: wahi $\phi$ dono arguments par lagta hai.
  • Meaning in words: $D$ jodi-terms ka jod; har jodi "$x$ ka feature $j$ × $y$ ka feature $j$".

Notice that… $k(x,x)=\sum_j\phi_j(x)^2\ge0$ aur $k(x,y)=k(y,x)$ apne aap aa jaate hain.

Important insight: kernel trick ka matlab: algorithm ko sirf $k(x_i,x_j)$ ki table aur $k(x,x_j)$ chahiye; $\phi$ ka kabhi zikr hi nahi aata.

This is why… 3.1 ke sawaal do tarah ke hote hain: (a) $k\to\phi$ (P1, P2, P3, P5), (b) $\phi$ ya $k$ ka size (P4).

N3 (kernel trick). Agar koi algorithm data ko sirf $x_i^Tx_j$ aur $x^Tx_j$ se dekhta hai (jaise B12 ka dual PCA), to har dot product ko $k(\cdot,\cdot)$ se badal do — result wahi jo $\phi(x_i)$ par chalane se aata.

N4 (non-uniqueness). Agar $\phi$ kernel $k$ ka feature map hai aur $Q$ koi orthogonal matrix ($Q^TQ=I$), to $\phi'(x)=Q\phi(x)$ bhi hai:

$$\phi'(x)^T\phi'(y)=\phi(x)^TQ^TQ\,\phi(y)=\phi(x)^T\phi(y)=k(x,y).$$
  • $Q$ = coordinates ka order badalna, kisi coordinate ka sign ulta karna, ya ghuma dena — sab allowed.
  • Isliye "kaunsa $\phi$ may correspond" poochhte hain; options mein ek hi form hota hai jo term-matching se aata hai.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

$k\to\phi$ (term matching):

  1. $k(x,y)$ ko poora expand karo (sab brackets kholo, powers phailao).
  2. Har term ko $c\cdot f(x)\,f(y)$ ki shakl mein likho: $x$-wale factors ek taraf, $y$-wale doosri taraf; dono mein same monomial hona chahiye.
  3. $c>0$ ho to coordinate $\sqrt c\,f(x)$ (coefficient $2$ ⇒ $\sqrt2$, coefficient $9$ ⇒ $3$).
  4. Saare coordinates ko ek vector mein rakho — yeh $\phi$ hai.
  5. Check: $\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$ expand karo — har term wapas aana chahiye, koi extra term nahi.

Options diye hon (MCQ):

  1. Har option ka $\phi(x)^T\phi(y)$ likho aur $k$ se compare karo; ya ek-do test pairs $(x,y)$ par numbers daalo (jo option mismatch kare, kaat do).
  2. Sirf wahi option bachta hai jo har term match kare.

$\phi\to k$: $k=\sum_j\phi_j(x)\phi_j(y)$ — jodi-terms jodo.

🧪 Worked examples [ADDED]

Example 1 (simple) — $k=(x^Ty)^2$ on $\mathbb R^2$

  1. Expand: $$(x_1y_1+x_2y_2)^2=x_1^2y_1^2+2x_1x_2y_1y_2+x_2^2y_2^2.$$
  2. Terms: $(x_1^2)(y_1^2)$; $2\,(x_1x_2)(y_1y_2)=(\sqrt2x_1x_2)(\sqrt2y_1y_2)$; $(x_2^2)(y_2^2)$.
  3. $\phi(x)=(x_1^2,\ \sqrt2x_1x_2,\ x_2^2)^T$.
  4. Check: $x=(1,2)$, $y=(3,1)$: $\phi(x)=(1,2\sqrt2,4)$, $\phi(y)=(9,3\sqrt2,1)$, dot $=9+12+4=25$, aur $(1\cdot3+2\cdot1)^2=5^2=25$ ✓ (code-verified).

Example 2 (exam-level) — coefficients aur mixed terms

$k(x,y)=4+9x_1y_1+x_2y_2+25\,x_1^2x_2\,y_1^2y_2$ on $\mathbb R^2$. Find $\phi$ aur $k\bigl((1,2),(2,-1)\bigr)$.

  2. $\phi(x)=(2,\ 3x_1,\ x_2,\ 5x_1^2x_2)^T$.
  3. $\phi(1,2)=(2,3,2,10)$; $\phi(2,-1)=(2,6,-1,-20)$.
  4. Dot: $4+18-2-200=\mathbf{-180}$. Seedhe $k$ se: $$4+9\cdot2+(-2)+25\cdot(1\cdot2)\cdot(4\cdot(-1))=4+18-2-200=-180$$ ✓ (code-verified). Negative hona theek hai — $\phi$ ke coordinates negative ho sakte hain.

Example 3 (tricky) — non-uniqueness

  • Rotation: $k=(x^Ty)^2$ ka $\phi=(x_1^2,\sqrt2x_1x_2,x_2^2)$. $Q$ = pehle do coordinates ko $45^\circ$ ghumao. $x=(1,2)$: $\phi=(1,\ 2.828,\ 4)$, $$Q\phi=(-1.293,\ 2.707,\ 4).$$ $y=(3,1)$ ke saath bhi ghumao: dot phir bhi $\mathbf{25}$ (code-verified). Naya $\phi$ valid hai, par options mein nahi hota.
  • Dimension bhi badal sakta hai: $k(x,y)=x_1y_1$ on $\mathbb R^2$: $\phi(x)=x_1$ ($D=1$) bhi chalta hai aur $\phi(x)=(x_1/\sqrt2,\ x_1/\sqrt2)$ ($D=2$) bhi: $2\cdot\tfrac{x_1y_1}2=x_1y_1$. $x=(3,5)$, $y=(2,7)$: dono $6$ ✓.

Non-examples

  • $\phi(x)=(x_1+x_2,\ \dots)$ jaisa "sum" feature: $$(x_1+x_2)(y_1+y_2)=x_1y_1+x_1y_2+x_2y_1+x_2y_2$$ mein mixed terms $x_1y_2$, $x_2y_1$ aate hain, jo $(x^Ty)^2+1$ mein nahi hain. Yahi P2 ka option 2 hai.
  • $k(x,y)=x_1y_2+x_2y_1$ symmetric hai, par $x=(1,-1)$ par $k(x,x)=x_1x_2+x_2x_1=-2<0$. Kernel ke liye $k(x,x)=\|\phi(x)\|^2\ge0$ chahiye ⇒ yeh kernel nahi (code-verified).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. $\sqrt2$ kyun? (cross-term ka coefficient)

$k$ mein cross term $2x_1x_2y_1y_2$ hai. Hume ek coordinate $f(x)=c\,x_1x_2$ chahiye jisse $f(x)f(y)=c^2x_1x_2y_1y_2$. $c^2=2$ ⇒ $c=\sqrt2$ (ya $-\sqrt2$). Dono coordinates ko alag karke ($x_1x_2$ aur $x_2x_1$ ko do features maanke, har ek ko coefficient $1$) bhi $2$ ban sakta hai — tab $\phi$ mein $D=4$ ho jaata hai: $(x_1^2,x_1x_2,x_2x_1,x_2^2)$. Yeh bhi valid hai (N4 wali dimension-non-uniqueness), par standard $\phi$ chhota wala hai.

2. $(x^Ty)^2$ ke kitne features, $d$ dimensions mein?

Squares: $d$. Cross terms: $\binom d2$. Kul $d+\binom d2=\tfrac{d(d+1)}2=\binom{d+1}{2}$. $d=2$: $3$; $d=3$: $6$; $d=4$: $10$ (code-verified). $+1$ lagane par ek constant coordinate aur judta hai.

3. Cost: explicit $\phi$ vs kernel (N3)

$d=100$, $m=3$, $(1+x^Ty)^3$: explicit $\phi$ mein $\binom{103}{3}=176\,851$ coordinates. Kernel: ek 100-length dot product ($100$ multiplications), $+1$, cube — lagbhag $d$ steps. Ek $k$ call $D$ se bahut sasta; B16 ka kernel PCA isi par tikta hai. (Order-of-magnitude, timings nahi.)

4. Kaun sa $k$ kisi $\phi$ ka dot product ho sakta hai? (B14 ka preview)

Zaroori: symmetric; $k(x,x)\ge0$; Cauchy–Schwarz $|k(x,y)|^2\le k(x,x)\,k(y,y)$; har Gram matrix $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$ PSD. Ulta bhi sach hai (Mercer): PSD Gram matrices wala $k$ hamesha kisi $\phi$ ka dot product hota hai. Poora treatment B14–B15 mein; yahan sirf itna ki "kuch bhi formula" kernel nahi hota.

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Polynomial kernel = "features ke combinations" ka kernel. $(x^Ty+1)^m$ mein $x$ ke saare products (degree $m$ tak) apne aap shamil hain, par unhe alag-alag banana nahi padta — ek dot product aur ek power.
  • Gaussian kernel = "kitne kareeb" ka kernel. Paas ke points ko $\approx1$, door ke ko $\approx0$. $\sigma$ pad ka size.
  • Finite vs infinite: degree $m$ ka polynomial kernel $\binom{d+m}{m}$ features ka hai; Gaussian ka feature space infinite hai.

Analogy [ADDED] — mohalle ki dosti. Gaussian kernel = do log kitne kareeb rehte hain: ek hi ghar ($x=y$) ho to $1$; mohalle ke andar $0.6$ ke aas-paas; doosre shehar mein lagbhag $0$. $\sigma$ = mohalle ka size: bada $\sigma$ ⇒ poora shehar ek mohalla. Polynomial: "teams" — degree $m$ ka matlab ek baar mein $m$ features ki team banti hai aur har team ek naya feature hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Mohalle ki doori seedhi hoti hai; Gaussian mein $\|x-y\|^2$ aur $2\sigma^2$ ka exponent hai — gira hua bell-curve jaisa, seedha nahi. (2) Dosti sirf do logon tak limited hai; kernel poore feature space ka dot product hai. (3) Polynomial kernel doori nahi, dot product (angle + length) naapta hai: $x=y=(10,10)$ par $(x^Ty+1)^m=201^m$ bahut bada, jabki Gaussian wahan $1$ deta hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "Polynomial kernel hamesha $(x^Ty)^m$" — nahi; PYQs ($+1$ ke saath) aur homogeneous (bina $+c$) alag cheezein hain.
  • "Gaussian kernel distance hai" — similarity hai: paas ⇒ $1$.
  • "Gaussian ka $\phi$ finite likh sakte hain" — nahi (infinite-dimensional).
  • "$\sigma$ badhao to kernel zyada local hota hai" — ulta: bada $\sigma$ ⇒ zyada door tak similarity.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

N5 (polynomial kernel).

$$k(x,y)=(x^Ty+c)^m,\qquad c\ge0,\ m\in\mathbb N.$$
  • Symbols: $m$ = degree; $c$ = constant (PYQs mein $c=1$ maana jaata hai); $x,y\in\mathbb R^d$.
  • Meaning in words: dot product lo, $c$ jodo, $m$-th power.
  • Binomial expansion:
$$(x^Ty+c)^m=\sum_{j=0}^{m}\binom mj\,c^{\,m-j}\,(x^Ty)^j.$$
  • Feature count: $c>0$ ⇒ $\binom{d+m}{m}$ (saare degrees $0..m$); $c=0$ ⇒ $\binom{d+m-1}{m}$ (sirf degree $m$).
  • Explicit $\phi$: exponent-tuple $a=(a_1,\dots,a_d)$, $|a|=\sum a_i\le m$ ke liye
$$\phi_a(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac{m!}{a_1!\cdots a_d!\,(m-|a|)!}\,c^{\,m-|a|}}\;\;x_1^{a_1}\cdots x_d^{a_d}.$$

(Multinomial theorem se; $d=2,m=2,c=1$ mein yeh R3 ka $(1,\sqrt2x_1,\sqrt2x_2,x_1^2,\sqrt2x_1x_2,x_2^2)$ deta hai.)

Notice that… $c$ hi constant-aur-lower-degree features lata hai. $c=0$ pe sirf degree-$m$ features bachte hain, aur kernel degree-$m$ homogeneous ho jaata hai.

N6 (Gaussian / RBF kernel).

$$k(x,y)=\exp\!\Bigl(-\frac{\|x-y\|^2}{2\sigma^2}\Bigr),\qquad \sigma>0.$$
  • Symbols: $\sigma$ = bandwidth.
  • Meaning in words: distance ka square, $2\sigma^2$ se bhaag, minus, exponent.
  • Properties: $k(x,x)=1$; $0<k\le1$; symmetric; $\|x-y\|\uparrow\Rightarrow k\downarrow$.

Small example: $x=(1,2)$, $y=(3,1)$: $(x^Ty+1)^2=(5+1)^2=36$. $x=(0,1)$, $y=(1,0)$, $\sigma=1$: $e^{-1}=0.3679$.

This is why… PYQs sirf formula lagane ko kehte hain (P6, P7) — aur 3.1 ke P4 mein feature count poochha jaata hai.

N8 (feature-space distance).

$$\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|^2=k(x,x)+k(y,y)-2k(x,y).$$

Proof: dono taraf expand karo (G1):

$$\|\phi(x)-\phi(y)\|^2=\phi(x)^T\phi(x)+\phi(y)^T\phi(y)-2\,\phi(x)^T\phi(y).$$

Gaussian ke liye $=2-2k(x,y)$: $x=(0,1)$, $y=(1,0)$ par $2-2(0.3679)=1.2642$. Saare Gaussian images unit sphere par hain ($\|\phi(x)\|^2=k(x,x)=1$).

N7 recall (B12 link): $K_{ij}=k(x_i,x_j)$; Gaussian ka diagonal $1$ (R5).

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

Polynomial kernel ki value:

  1. Form pakdo: $(x^Ty+c)^m$; agar stem form nahi deta to key/convention se $c=1$ lo (PYQs: P4, P6 dono key se $c=1$ confirm karte hain).
  2. $x^Ty=\sum x_iy_i$ nikalo.
  3. $c$ jodo.
  4. $m$-th power lo.

Feature count: $c>0$: $\binom{d+m}{m}$; $c=0$: $\binom{d+m-1}{m}$.

Gaussian kernel ki value:

  1. $x-y$ nikalo, har entry ka square, jodo: $\|x-y\|^2$.
  2. $2\sigma^2$ nikalo.
  3. Bhaag do, minus lagao, $\exp$ lo.
  4. Range check: $0<k\le1$; $x=y$ ⇒ $1$.

Doori dissimilarity hai; Gaussian use $e^{-(\cdot)}$ se similarity mein badalta hai.

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. Gaussian ka feature space infinite kyun? (1-D, $\sigma=1$)

$\|x-y\|^2=x^2+y^2-2xy$ ⇒

$$e^{-(x-y)^2/2}=e^{-x^2/2}\,e^{-y^2/2}\,e^{xy}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Bigl(e^{-x^2/2}\tfrac{x^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\Bigr)\Bigl(e^{-y^2/2}\tfrac{y^n}{\sqrt{n!}}\Bigr).$$

Yeh $\sum_n\phi_n(x)\phi_n(y)$ hai (N2) ⇒ $\phi_n(x)=e^{-x^2/2}x^n/\sqrt{n!}$, $n=0,1,2,\dots$ — infinitely many coordinates. Truncation check ($x=0.5$, $y=1.2$, exact $0.782705$): pehle $1,2,3,5,10$ terms ka jod: $$0.4296,\ 0.6873,\ 0.7646,\ 0.7824,\ 0.782705$$ (code-verified). Isliye koi finite $\phi$ nahi, par kernel exact.

2. Polynomial kernel = homogeneous kernels ka jod

Binomial expansion (N5) $(x^Ty+c)^m=\sum_j\binom mjc^{m-j}(x^Ty)^j$: har $(x^Ty)^j$ degree-$j$ homogeneous kernel hai, positive weights $\binom mjc^{m-j}$ ke saath. Isliye kernel positive combinations aur products ke andar band hai (closure) — B14 ka core.

3. $\sigma\to0$ aur $\sigma\to\infty$

$\sigma\to0$: $k(x,y)\to0$ har $x\ne y$ ke liye, $K\to I$ — har point sirf apne jaisa; model rat-ne lagta hai (overfit). $\sigma\to\infty$: $k\to1$ har pair ke liye, $K\to\mathbf 1\mathbf 1^T$ — sab ek jaise, kuch seekhne ko nahi bachta. Beech ka $\sigma$ chunna hota hai (B16 ke baad ke applications mein).

4. $k(x,x)$ par dhyaan: polynomial vs Gaussian

Gaussian ke saare $\phi(x)$ ki length $1$ hai (unit sphere). Polynomial mein $\|\phi(x)\|^2=(\|x\|^2+c)^m$ — bade $x$ ke liye bahut bada. Isliye polynomial kernel mein features ka scale dhyaan se rakhna padta hai (normalise karke ya $c$ chun ke).
