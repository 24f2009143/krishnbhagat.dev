🧠 Intuition — plain language

Ek shehar socho jismein $K$ post offices hain (centres). Har ghar (point) apne sabse paas ke post office ko jaata hai. Shehar ka naksha catchment areas mein bat jaata hai: har office ke aas-paas ek ilaaka. Do offices ke beech ke ilaake ko alag karne waali deewar wahi seedhi line hai jo dono ke beech ke raaste ko aadha karti hai aur us par seedhi (perpendicular) khadi hai. Teen offices hon to teen aisi deewarein milti hain, aur har ilaaka ek samtal-kone wala (convex) tukda banta hai — andar koi "dent" ya "chhed" nahi.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) asli post offices ke raaste Euclidean nahi hote; K-means mein distance seedhi line ka hai. (2) Post office ki capacity hoti hai; K-means mein cluster ka size koi cheez nahi. (3) Yahan offices khud bhi hilte hain — har round ke baad apne ilaake ke mean par chale jaate hain (4.2); assignment sirf ek frozen snapshot hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai: (a) "apne sabse paas ke doosre point ka label le lo" nahi — sirf centres se doori maayne rakhti hai. (b) Soft/probabilistic nahi — har point ka ek hi label (hard). (c) Cluster ke shape ki koi samajh nahi — isliye rings/crescents nahi mil sakte.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version (Formula Protocol)

$$z_i^{t+1}=\arg\min_{k\in\{1,\dots,K\}}\ \|x_i-\mu_k^t\|^2\qquad\text{(tie ⇒ sabse chhota }k\text{)}$$

Symbols aur assumptions: $x_i\in\mathbb R^d$ data point; $\mu_k^t\in\mathbb R^d$ iteration $t$ par $k$-th centre; $\|\cdot\|$ Euclidean norm; $K$ centres, sab alag maane ja rahe hain; assignment sirf centres dekhta hai — doosre points ya cluster sizes nahi.

Words mein: har point ko us centre ka label do jo uske sabse paas ho.

Notice that… $\arg\min$ ke liye $\|\cdot\|$ aur $\|\cdot\|^2$ ek hi jawab dete hain (square ek increasing function hai, $\ge0$ par); objective $F$ mein squared hi chalta hai. Aur argmin sirf ek point ke liye alag-alag nikalta hai — poore dataset ko ek saath dekhne ki zaroorat nahi.

Do centres ka bisector — poora derivation, koi step chhota nahi

Point $x$ centre $a$ ke utna ya zyada paas hai jitna $b$ ke:

$$\|x-a\|^2\le\|x-b\|^2\iff\|x\|^2-2x^Ta+\|a\|^2\le\|x\|^2-2x^Tb+\|b\|^2\iff 2x^T(b-a)\le\|b\|^2-\|a\|^2.$$

Pehle $\iff$ mein har side ko expand kiya ($\|x-a\|^2=\|x\|^2-2x^Ta+\|a\|^2$); doosre mein dono taraf se $\|x\|^2$ kat gaya (yahi kaaran hai ki condition $x$ mein linear reh jaati hai); phir saare $x$-terms ek taraf. Barabari $2x^T(b-a)=\|b\|^2-\|a\|^2$ ek hyperplane hai: normal $b-a$, aur midpoint $\frac{a+b}2$ ispar hai (check: $2\cdot\frac{(a+b)^T}{2}(b-a)=\|b\|^2-\|a\|^2$ ✓). Yeh L14 hai.

Cell convex kyun hai (L15)

Fix $k$. Har $j\ne k$ ke liye $g_j(x)=2x^T(\mu_j-\mu_k)-(\|\mu_j\|^2-\|\mu_k\|^2)$, jo $x$ ka affine function hai. $V_k=\{x:g_j(x)\le0\ \forall j\ne k\}$. Agar $g_j(x)\le0$ aur $g_j(y)\le0$ to $0\le\lambda\le1$ ke liye $g_j(\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y)=\lambda g_j(x)+(1-\lambda)g_j(y)\le0$ (affine function convex combination ko preserve karta hai). Yeh har $j$ ke liye sach hai, isliye $\lambda x+(1-\lambda)y\in V_k$. ∎

Important insight: cluster ki shape dataset se nahi, centres se tay hoti hai, aur woh hamesha convex hoti hai. Isliye "do points same cluster mein ⇒ unke beech ka segment bhi" aur "rings alag nahi" dono ek hi sach ke do chehre hain.

This is why P4 sirf ek line mein hal ho jaata hai, aur P2 ko figures ke bina bhi criterion se samjha ja sakta hai.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Centres $\mu_1,\dots,\mu_K$ diye hain (ya $z^{(0)}$ diya ho to pehle us se centres banao: $\mu_k=$ us label ke points ka mean).
  2. Har point $x_i$ ke liye har centre tak $d^2$ nikalo (ya do-centre waale cases mein L14 ki ek linear inequality).
  3. Sabse chhota $d^2$ chuno; tie ho to chhota index.
  4. $z$ likho; cluster lists $C_k=\{i:z_i=k\}$ banao. Labels sirf naam hain (L16).
  5. Update (B17): har $C_k$ ka naya mean; koi $C_k$ khaali ho to centre wahi rakho (convention).
  6. Dobara assign karo. Agar $z$ nahi badla to ruko; yeh terminating pass bhi ginti mein aata hai (L18).

WE3 — Tricky: feature scale se assignment badal sakti hai

Point $p=(1,0)$; centres $\mu_1=(0,3)$, $\mu_2=(3,0)$. Raw: $d^2(p,\mu_1)=1+9=10$, $d^2(p,\mu_2)=4$ ⇒ $\mu_2$. Non-example

"$x$ ko uske sabse paas ke data point ka label de do" (nearest neighbour) K-means ka assignment nahi hai — K-means sirf centres se doori dekhta hai. Counter: P4 mein $(1,2)$ aur $(0,0)$ bhi $(0,1)$/$(1,1)$ ke bilkul paas hain (doori $1$), phir bhi cluster guaranteed nahi — "paas hona" kaafi nahi; $(0.2,1)$ isliye guaranteed hai ki woh segment ke andar hai (convex cell).

🔬 Deeper insight (D3) — edge cases, proofs, connections

1. Tie-rule se bhi convexity bachti hai

Agar $x,y$ dono cluster $k$ mein hain to $j\lt k$ ke liye $g_j(x)<0$ aur $g_j(y)<0$ (strict, kyunki tie par chhota index $j$ jeetta), $j>k$ ke liye $\le0$. Strict $\Rightarrow$ strict, $\le0\Rightarrow\le0$ segment par bhi, isliye segment ka har point tie-rule ke baad bhi cluster $k$ mein hi aata hai.

2. Final partition ka test (L17)

Koi partition $\{C_k\}$ Lloyd ka fixed point tab hai jab har $i$ ke liye $\|x_i-\bar x_{C_{z_i}}\|^2\le\|x_i-\bar x_{C_j}\|^2$ sab $j$ ke liye ($\bar x_C$ = $C$ ka mean). Chhote data par saare partitions enumerate karke fixed points chhaante ja sakte hain: P5/P6 ke 4 points ke saare $7$ partitions mein sirf ek fixed point nikla — wahi jo expected tha. Aur 1-D $\{0,1,2,10,11,12\}$ ke saare $31$ ($k=2$) partitions mein sirf $\{0,1,2\}\mid\{10,11,12\}$ fixed point nikla. [ADDED] (code se)

3. Rings kyun nahi (P2 ki jad)

Do conditions toot-ti hain: (a) separability — final cells ek bisector se alag hone chahiye (seedhi line, $d>2$ mein hyperplane); inner ring ko outer ring se koi seedhi line alag nahi karti. (b) fixed point — symmetric rings ke centroid ek hi jagah (origin) par aate hain, isliye distances barabar aur assignment toot jaati hai. Dono alag-alag sach hain; ek hi kaafi hai "cannot be final" kehne ke liye.

4. Boundary kitni badi cheez hai

$\mathbb R^d$ mein do cells ke beech ki boundary ek (bounded ya unbounded) hyperplane ka hissa hai; $K$ centres ke cells ek Voronoi diagram banate hain. Yeh sab cells convex polyhedra hain.

5. Khaali cluster

Agar kisi centre ke cell mein koi data point nahi aata to $C_k=\emptyset$ aur mean undefined. Ispar bank ka koi sawaal nahi; convention (centre wahi rakho) B17 jaisa hai.

6. Connection

Assign step ka $\arg\min$ hi 4.4 mein "$F$ nahi badhta" ka pehla half hai; aur yahi cheez GMM E-step mein $\arg\min$ ki jagah softmax-jaisi weights ban jaati hai (T7).

🧠 Intuition

Ek mohalle mein kitni dukaanein kholein? Dukaan badhane se har ghar ka average chalne ka faasla kam hota jaata hai ($F$ ghatta hai), jab tak har ghar ke paas apni dukaan na ho ($F=0$). Par har dukaan ka kharcha hota hai — jo $F$ ke andar hai hi nahi. "Elbow" woh jagah hai jahan ek aur dukaan kholne par faasla bahut kam ghatta hai.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: $F$ mein dukaan ke kharche ka koi hissa nahi; K-means khud $K$ nahi chunta; $K=n$ par $F=0$ hamesha — par woh koi clustering nahi, sirf copy hai.

Yeh kya NAHI hai: (a) "kam $F$ = behtar clustering" nahi — $k=n$ sabse kam $F$ deta hai aur sabse bekaar hai. (b) Initialization sirf "shuruaat" nahi — final answer badal sakta hai (local minimum). (c) Elbow koi theorem nahi — kabhi saaf mod nahi hota.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version

$$F^*(k)=\min_{z,\ \mu_1,\dots,\mu_k}\ \sum_{i=1}^n\|x_i-\mu_{z_i}\|^2$$

Symbols: $F^*(k)$ = $k$ clusters ke saath best sambhav (global) objective; $z$ labels, $\mu_j$ centres; minimum saare partitions par. Lloyd ka output sirf ek local minimum hota hai, isliye $F_{\text{Lloyd}}(k)\ge F^*(k)$.

Words mein: $k$ clusters mein jitna kam $F$ ho sakta hai.

Notice that… $F^*(k)$ $k$ ka ghatta hua (non-increasing) function hai aur $F^*(n)=0$.

Proof: $F^*(k)\ge F^*(k+1)$ (jab $k\lt n$)

  1. $k$ clusters ka ek optimal partition lo. $n>k$ ⇒ pigeonhole se koi cluster $C$ ke $\ge2$ points hain.
  2. $C$ se ek point $x$ nikaal kar use alag cluster bana do ($k+1$ clusters). Naya $F$: $\mathrm{SSE}(C\setminus\{x\})+\mathrm{SSE}(\{x\})$ $=\mathrm{SSE}(C\setminus\{x\})+0$.
  3. $\mathrm{SSE}(C)=\min_v\sum_{i\in C}\|x_i-v\|^2\ge\min_v\sum_{i\in C\setminus\{x\}}\|x_i-v\|^2=\mathrm{SSE}(C\setminus\{x\})$ (ek non-negative term hataya).
  4. ⇒ naya $F\le$ purana optimum, to $F^*(k+1)\le F^*(k)$. ∎

$F^*(n)=0$: har point apna cluster ⇒ $\mu=x_i$ ⇒ $\|x_i-\mu\|^2=0$. Agar points repeat hon to $F^*(k)=0$ pehle hi tab hota hai jab $k\ge$ alag-alag points ki ginti.

Important insight: optimum $F^*$ monotone hai, par Lloyd ka asli run nahi. Alag $k$ ke alag initializations alag local minima par ruk sakte hain, isliye "$F_{\text{Lloyd}}(k)$ hamesha ghatega" guaranteed nahi hai. This is why elbow plot ke liye har $k$ par kai initializations chalake sabse kam $F$ rakhte hain.

⚙️ How it works — elbow (standard practice, bank mein nahi) [ADDED]

  1. $k=1,2,\dots,k_{\max}$ ke liye K-means chalao (har $k$ par kai alag initializations, sabse kam $F$ rakho).
  2. $F$ vs $k$ plot karo.
  3. Jahan se $F$ ka girna achanak sust ho (mod) — wahi $k$ chuno.
  4. Mod saaf na ho to domain knowledge ya koi aur criterion dekho. (Silhouette jaisi alag methods bank mein nahi hain — main yahan unhe nahi padha raha.)
