Standard PCA has been performed on a centered dataset in $\mathbb{R}^3$. The first two principal components are:

$$w_1=\frac{1}{\sqrt3}\begin{bmatrix}1\\1\\1\end{bmatrix},\qquad w_2=\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\begin{bmatrix}1\\-1\\0\end{bmatrix}$$

Consider the data point in the dataset: $[2\quad1\quad-1]^T$. $(a,b)$ is the representation of this point in the coordinate system formed by the two principal components given above. The first and second coordinates correspond to PC-1 and PC-2 respectively.

What is the value of $a$? Enter your answer correctly to three decimal places.

Peeche (prerequisites)Yeh subtopicAage (kya unlock hota hai)

🧠 Intuition — plain language

  • Coordinates = naye axes par padhi hui reading. Purane axes par point $(x_1,x_2,x_3)$ hai. PCA ne axes ghuma diye ($w_1,w_2,w_3$). Naye axes par point ki reading = "point ka saaya $w_k$ ki line par kitni door (origin se, sign ke saath)" — yahi $z_k$ hai.
  • Dot product kyun? Kyunki $w_k$ unit hai aur baaki PCs uske perpendicular hain — isliye $w_k^T\tilde x$ mein sirf $k$-th axis ka hissa bachta hai, baaki sab cancel (G3 mein 3-line proof).
  • Number hai, arrow nahi. $z_k$ ek scalar hai. Usse $w_k$ se guna karo to woh arrow milta hai jo original space mein dikhta hai ($z_kw_k$, projection vector).
  • "Representation in the system of 2 PCs" = sirf 2 numbers. $\mathbb R^3$ ka point $\mathbb R^2$ mein: $(a,b)$ rakho, $c$ (teesra coordinate) phenk do. Phenka hua $c$ hi information loss hai: $c^2=\|r\|^2$.
  • Sign $w$ ke sign se aata hai. $w\to-w$ karo to $z$ ka sign ulta; length, variance, error sab same. Isliye jo $w$ diya hai usi ka sign follow karo.

Analogy [ADDED] — tirchhi sadak ka address. Shehar ka grid seedha (North–East) hai, par ek naya mohalla 30° ghuma hua bana hai. Mohalle ke gate se kisi ghar tak "kitna sadak ke saath, kitna sadak ke perpendicular" naapna = us ghar ke naye coordinates. Dono naapne ke raste perpendicular hain, isliye ek naap dusre ko disturb nahi karta — tape (dot product) se seedha naap sakte ho.

Analogy kahan tootti hai: (1) Shehar ka grid pehle se diya hota hai; PCA ka grid data se chuna jaata hai. (2) Agar mohalle ki galiyan perpendicular na hon (non-orthogonal), to seedha tape-naap galat aayega — system solve karna padega (Worked Example 4). (3) Mohalle ka gate (origin) = data ka mean; PCA mein naapne se pehle mean ghatana zaroori hai (Example 5).

Yeh kya NAHI hai:

  • "Coordinate = projection vector" — nahi. Coordinate scalar $z_k$; vector $z_kw_k$ hai (P2 mein $a=1.342$, vector $=(0.6,0,1.2)$).
  • "Coordinate = $w^Tx$ chahe $w$ kuch bhi ho" — nahi. $w$ unit hona chahiye, warna $\|w\|$ se divide.
  • "Teesra coordinate bhi diya jaata hai" — nahi. 2 PCs retain hon to answer $(a,b)$ hai; $c$ sirf check ke liye nikalte hain.
  • "Variance along $w_k$ ek point ka number hai" — nahi. Variance poore dataset ke scores par average hai; ek point ka $z_k$ nahi.

📊 Visual intuition

📖 Formal version — Formula Protocol

$$\text{(G1)}\ \ z_k=w_k^T\tilde x\quad(\|w_k\|=1);\qquad\text{non-unit } u:\ \ z=\frac{u^T\tilde x}{\|u\|}$$ $$\text{(G2)}\ \ z=W_K^T\tilde x\in\mathbb R^K,\qquad W_K=\begin{bmatrix}w_1&\cdots&w_K\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb R^{d\times K}$$ $$\text{(G3)}\ \ \tilde x=\sum_{k=1}^{d}z_kw_k\ \Longrightarrow\ w_j^T\tilde x=\sum_kz_k\,(w_j^Tw_k)=z_j$$ $$\text{(G4)}\ \ \|\tilde x\|^2=\sum_{k=1}^{d}z_k^2,\qquad \|r\|^2=\|\tilde x\|^2-\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_k^2=\sum_{k>K}z_k^2$$ $$\text{(G5)}\ \ \frac1n\sum_{i=1}^{n}z_{ik}z_{il}=w_k^TCw_l=\lambda_l\,\delta_{kl}$$ $$\text{(G6)}\ \ \hat x=\sum_{k=1}^{K}z_kw_k=W_KW_K^T\tilde x\in\mathbb R^d$$

Symbols aur assumptions:

  • $\tilde x=x-\mu\in\mathbb R^d$ — centred point ($\mu$ = dataset mean). Jab dataset "centered" kaha jaaye aur point "in the dataset" ho, to $\tilde x=x$ already.
  • $w_1,\dots,w_d$ — PCs: unit aur mutually orthogonal (B7), $\lambda_1\ge\dots\ge\lambda_d$ ke order mein. $w_j^Tw_k=\delta_{jk}$ ($1$ agar $j=k$, warna $0$).
  • $z_k$ — $k$-th PC coordinate (score), ek scalar. $z=(z_1,\dots,z_K)$ — $K$-PC representation. $K\le d$ retained PCs ki ginti.
  • $u$ — koi bhi nonzero direction vector (unit hona zaroori nahi). $r=\tilde x-\hat x$ residual. $z_{ik}$ — point $i$ ka $k$-th score. $C$ covariance, $n$ points, $\delta_{kl}$ Kronecker delta.

Words mein: G1 — coordinate = dot product with the unit PC. G2 — saare $K$ coordinates ek saath ek matrix-vector product. G3 — poora point PCs ke combination ke roop mein likha jaata hai; dono taraf $w_j$ se dot karne par orthonormality ek hi term bachati hai (yahi proof hai G1 ka). G4 — lengths coordinates mein bhi bachti hain (Pythagoras); jo coordinates hataye unka squared jod hi error. G5 — dataset par scores ka variance $\lambda_l$, alag-alag scores uncorrelated. G6 — $K$ coordinates se wapas $\mathbb R^d$ ka approximate point.

Notice that… (i) G1 mein $\tilde x$ ke saath hi dot hota hai, $w_k$ ke saath nahi badalta — order of PCs hi order of coordinates hai. (ii) G3 ka proof sirf $w_j^Tw_k=\delta_{jk}$ use karta hai; agar basis orthonormal nahi hota to yeh collapse nahi hota (Example 4). (iii) $z$ ($K$ numbers) aur $\hat x$ ($d$ numbers) alag objects hain.

Small worked example (2-D): $w_1=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)$, $w_2=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(-1,1)$, $\tilde x=(3,1)$. $z_1=\tfrac{3+1}{\sqrt2}=2\sqrt2\approx2.828$, $z_2=\tfrac{-3+1}{\sqrt2}=-\sqrt2\approx-1.414$. Check G4: $z_1^2+z_2^2=8+2=10=9+1$ ✓. Reconstruct G3: $2\sqrt2\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(1,1)-\sqrt2\cdot\tfrac1{\sqrt2}(-1,1)$ $=(2,2)+(1,-1)$ $=(3,1)$ ✓.

Important insight: PCA ne ek mushkil kaam ("naye axes mein coordinates solve karo") ko $K$ aasaan dot products bana diya — aur sirf isliye ki PCs orthonormal hain.

This is why exam mein "representation in the PC coordinate system" ka matlab hamesha dot products hota hai, aur isi liye B9 ke options mein sirf normalisation ($\sqrt\lambda$, $\sqrt{n\lambda}$) ka farak hota hai.

⚙️ How it works — numbered mechanism

  1. Padho kya diya hai. Kya PCs given hain? Kya woh unit hain? Kya point "in the (centred) dataset" hai ya raw point + mean?
  2. Verify karo (10 second): har $w_k$ ke liye $\|w_k\|^2=1$ (integers mein: $\sum(\text{entries})^2=\text{denominator}^2$), aur $w_1^Tw_2=0$. Ye ho gaya to dot product valid hai.
  3. Centre karo agar zaroori ho: $\tilde x=x-\mu$. Agar point dataset ka hi hai aur dataset centred hai, to koi change nahi.
  4. Integer vectors rakho, radical baad mein: $u_k=\sqrt{(\cdot)}\,w_k$ ke integer version se $u_k^T\tilde x$ nikalo.
  5. Normalise karo: $z_k=u_k^T\tilde x/\|u_k\|$.
  6. Order follow karo: pehla coordinate PC1, doosra PC2 — jaisa sawaal mein likha hai.
  7. Decimal round karo end mein: pehle exact form ($3/\sqrt5$), phir calculator ($1.3416\to1.342$).
  8. Sanity bound: $|z_k|\le\|\tilde x\|$ aur $\sum_kz_k^2\le\|\tilde x\|^2$ (equality tab jab saare $d$ coordinates lo).

Example 4 — non-example: basis orthogonal nahi [ADDED]

🔬 Deeper insight (D3)

1. Scores ka variance $\lambda_k$ aur uncorrelated — 3-line proof

Scores $z_{ik}=w_k^T\tilde x_i$. Mean: $\tfrac1n\sum_iz_{ik}=w_k^T\bigl(\tfrac1n\sum_i\tilde x_i\bigr)=0$ (centred). Covariance: $\tfrac1n\sum_iz_{ik}z_{il}=w_k^T\bigl(\tfrac1n\sum_i\tilde x_i\tilde x_i^T\bigr)w_l$, jo $w_k^TCw_l$ hai. $Cw_l=\lambda_lw_l$ aur $w_k^Tw_l=\delta_{kl}$ ⇒ $=\lambda_l\delta_{kl}$ (G5). Matlab $k=l$: variance $\lambda_k$; $k\ne l$: 0.

Numerical check (P7 ka $C$, dataset ±(2,1), ±(1,2), ±(1,−1)): $z_1\sqrt2=(3,-3,3,-3,0,0)$, $z_2\sqrt2=(-1,1,1,-1,-2,2)$ ⇒ $\operatorname{Var}z_1=\tfrac{36}{2\cdot6}=3$, $\operatorname{Var}z_2=\tfrac{12}{2\cdot6}=1$, $\sum z_1z_2=\tfrac12(-3-3+3+3)=0$ ✓ (code-verified).

2. $W^T$ ek rotation (ya reflection) hai — lengths aur angles bachte hain

Saare $d$ PCs lo: $W^TW=I$ ⇒ $W$ orthogonal matrix. $z=W^T\tilde x$ ke liye $\|z\|^2=\tilde x^TWW^T\tilde x=\|\tilde x\|^2$ ($WW^T=I$ bhi). Isliye G4 sach hai. $K\lt d$ coordinates lena = rotate karna + $d-K$ coordinates ko zero maan lena = original point ka PC-subspace par orthogonal projection (G6).

3. Sign aur ties — kab answer unique nahi

$w_k\to-w_k$ se $z_k\to-z_k$; variance, $|z_k|$, $\hat x$, error sab same. Isliye PYQ mein jo $w$ diya hai uska sign follow karo; agar sawaal sirf "length of projection" maange to $|z_k|$. Agar $\lambda_1=\lambda_2$ ho to PC1/PC2 ek 2-D eigenspace mein koi bhi orthonormal pair ho sakte hain ⇒ individual coordinates unique nahi (B7 cube example). Source PYQs mein PCs diye gaye hain, isliye unique answer milta hai.

4. Non-orthonormal basis: coordinates $=W^{-1}\tilde x$, $W^T\tilde x$ nahi

Agar columns $U=[u_1\ u_2\ \dots]$ ek (square, invertible) basis hon, to $\tilde x=Uc$ ⇒ $c=U^{-1}\tilde x$. $c=U^T\tilde x$ tab hi jab $U^{-1}=U^T$, yaani $U^TU=I$. Example 4 mein $U=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{bmatrix}$, $U^{-1}\binom32=\binom12$ ✓ jabki $U^T\binom32=\binom35$ ✗.

5. B9 ka preview (solve nahi kiya): $d>n$ mein coordinates dual form se

$w=X\alpha/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$ (B7) ko G1 mein daalo: $w^T\tilde x=\alpha^TX^T\tilde x/\sqrt{\lambda_K}$ — isi se B9 ke formula-options ($x_i^TX\alpha_k/\sqrt\lambda$ jaise) padhe jaate hain. Woh PYQs B9 mein solve honge.

1. 🔍 Decode

  • Asked: $a$ = PC-1 coordinate, $b$ = PC-2 coordinate of $x=(1,2,1)$.
  • Given: $w_1,w_2$ (PCs); $x$ "in the (centered) dataset" ⇒ $\tilde x=x$, mean ghatana nahi.
  • Trigger clues: "representation … in the coordinate system formed by the two principal components", "first and second coordinates correspond to PC-1 and PC-2".
  • Prerequisites: B3 scalar projection (Procedural), B7 PCs orthonormal (Conceptual), dot product (Procedural) · Core principle: G1, $z_k=w_k^T\tilde x$.

2. 🧭 Methods (2 genuinely different)

M1 General — har unit PC ke saath dot. M2 Advanced — poora basis ($w_3$ jodkar) $x=aw_1+bw_2+cw_3$ solve karo. Teesra method banane ka koi genuine tareeqa nahi — integer-vector shortcut M1 ka hi bookkeeping hai, alag method nahi.

3. 🧮 Side-by-side working

M1 · $z_k=w_k^Tx$

Step 1 (verify): $\|w_1\|^2=\tfrac{1+0+4}5=1$, $\|w_2\|^2=\tfrac{4+1+1}6=1$, $w_1^Tw_2=\tfrac{-2+0+2}{\sqrt{30}}=0$ ✓ ⇒ dot product valid.

Step 2 ($a$): $a=\tfrac{1\cdot1+0\cdot2+2\cdot1}{\sqrt5}=\tfrac3{\sqrt5}$.

Step 3 ($b$): $b=\tfrac{(-2)\cdot1+1\cdot2+1\cdot1}{\sqrt6}=\tfrac1{\sqrt6}$.

Step 4 (numbers): $\sqrt5=2.2361$ ⇒ $a=1.3416\to\mathbf{1.342}$. $\sqrt6=2.4495$ ⇒ $b=0.4082\to\mathbf{0.41}$.

M2 · $x=aw_1+bw_2+cw_3$

Step 1 ($w_3$): cross product $(1,0,2)\times(-2,1,1)$:
1st: $0\cdot1-2\cdot1=-2$
2nd: $2\cdot(-2)-1\cdot1=-5$
3rd: $1\cdot1-0\cdot(-2)=1$
⇒ $(-2,-5,1)$, $\|\cdot\|^2=30$, so $w_3=\tfrac1{\sqrt{30}}(-2,-5,1)$.

Step 2 (unknowns): $A=\tfrac a{\sqrt5}$, $B=\tfrac b{\sqrt6}$, $\Gamma=\tfrac c{\sqrt{30}}$. Components of $x=(1,2,1)$:
eq1: $A-2B-2\Gamma=1$
eq2: $B-5\Gamma=2$
eq3: $2A+B+\Gamma=1$

Core model: PC coordinate = dot product of the (centred) point with the unit PC. PCs orthonormal ⇒ koi system solve nahi karna. $K$ PCs retain ⇒ $K$ numbers; baaki ka squared jod = reconstruction error.

Backbone chain: point $\tilde x$ → check $\|w\|=1$, $w_j^Tw_k=0$ → $z_k=w_k^T\tilde x$ (order mein) → $(a,b)$ → bound $\sum z^2\le\|\tilde x\|^2$ → (optional) $c$ via $w_3$, $\hat x=\sum z_kw_k$.

Formulas (with conditions):

  • $z_k=w_k^T\tilde x$ — $w_k$ unit, PCs orthonormal
  • Non-unit $u$: $z=u^T\tilde x/\|u\|$
  • $\|\tilde x\|^2=\sum_{k=1}^dz_k^2$; $\|r\|^2=\sum_{k>K}z_k^2$
  • $\tfrac1n\sum_iz_{ik}z_{il}=\lambda_l\delta_{kl}$ (centred data)
  • $\hat x=\sum_{k\le K}z_kw_k=W_KW_K^T\tilde x$
  • Non-orthonormal basis: $c=U^{-1}\tilde x$, $U^T\tilde x$ nahi

Top 3 traps: (1) bina normalise raw dot ($a=3$ instead of $3/\sqrt5$); (2) coefficient ($u^Tx/u^Tu$) ya projection vector ko coordinate bata dena; (3) order/sign/centring — $a$, $b$ swap, $w$ ka sign, ya double / no centring.
