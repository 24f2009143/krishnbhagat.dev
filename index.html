KB-001 nerdhubinspecta world of study sites

Study sites I built for my own courses. Each one turns a course's past papers into a place to read, practise and revise: notes, past questions, mock tests, flashcards, a mistakes list and a study plan.

Two sites live there now. PDSA Quiz 2 treats the course as a graph: every topic is marked visited, frontier or unvisited. The MLT platform reads like handwritten notes in pen-blue ink, with a formula drawer and search across every note and question.

Decisions, in the code’s own words

  • Adding a topic = add one data file and flip status to "ready". The engine never changes. nerdhub/mlt/js/manifest.js
  • Built from the loaded topic data, so it never goes out of date. nerdhub/mlt/js/cheat.js
  • The backend sits behind a small adapter (init, onAuth, signIn, signOut, load, save) so the logic can be tested without a network. nerdhub/mlt/js/sync.js
  • Firebase web config is public by design; who can read or write is decided by firestore.rules nerdhub/mlt/js/firebase-config.js

KB-002 This siteinspecta pair of wings

KB-004 Does adaptive practice help?inspecta memory model, tested on 2,000 simulated students

MLT's “Practice next” ranks ideas by the marks you are likely to lose on them. Each idea carries a chance that you know it, updated after every answer (Bayesian knowledge tracing), and a memory half-life that doubles after a confident right answer and halves after a miss.

It was then tested against simpler ways of choosing. Most of the gain comes from weighting by marks: marks-weighted random gains 7.0 points over random, the full model 7.1. Over 30 days the memory model earns more (8.2 against 7.3). Practising your weakest topic first does worse than random.

The students are simulated, with a different forgetting law from the model, so it is tested on something it was not built for. It shows the method behaves sensibly. It does not show it helps real students.

KB-005 A graph that lays itself outinspectnodes push apart, edges pull together, then it cools

The PDSA site draws its graphs with its own force layout, about thirty lines long: every pair of nodes repels, every edge pulls its ends together, and each step is allowed to move a little less than the last, for 260 steps.

Below, the same code runs in your browser on real data: PDSA's five topics, joined to the topics each one builds on. Scrub through the 260 steps, play them, or pick a topic to see what it connects to.

KB-006 A solver that shows where the work isinspecttype a recurrence; see which level of the tree pays

A small widget in PDSA's divide and conquer topic. You type a, b and d for T(n) = a·T(n/b) + O(nᵈ) and it walks through the three steps of the master theorem, ending with why: the leaves do the most work, every level does the same, or the root does.

Below, choose a recurrence, or set a, b and d yourself, and read the work level by level down to the leaves.

T(n) = 2T(n/2) + n, with n = 32

Greedy = locally best, never undone. Fast, not always correct.KB-007 Remember linesinspectone of 38 at the ends of PDSA's sections

Every section of the PDSA site ends in one line meant to survive the night before the exam. A few more:

from nerdhub/pdsa-quiz-2/js/data/, maths typeset as plain text

  • Height is cost. Balanced height is log n, a chain is n.
  • Heap = complete tree + parent-child order. Not sorted.
  • Relax: if dist[u]+w < dist[v], lower dist[v].
  • Divide, conquer (recursion + base case), combine.
  • Compare a with bᵈ: bigger → n^(log_b a); equal → nᵈ log n; smaller → nᵈ.

Merge two copies of the study state without losing either.KB-009 Two copies, one stateinspectthe rules at the top of merge.js

When the same student studies on two devices, both copies have to survive. The rules, as written in the file:

from the comment at the top of nerdhub/mlt/js/merge.js, lightly shortened

  • the answer log: union, de-duplicated
  • past-question results: the newer attempt wins
  • counters: the larger count wins, because counters cannot be added without a log
  • sections read: union
  • a reset bumps the epoch, and a new epoch replaces instead of merging
